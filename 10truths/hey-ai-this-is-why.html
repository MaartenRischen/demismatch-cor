    Asked, exactly as shownWhy can't humans stop watching the bitcoin chart?

    Your pursuit system (M2) is running on a number that never finishes moving.

    That circuit runs on dopamine, and dopamine tracks wanting, not satisfaction. It was built for the full arc: want, pursue, acquire, use, done. The done is the part that switches it off. A price chart has no done. It updates on an interval shorter than your attention span, at intervals you cannot predict, which is the exact input profile that keeps a seeking system in continuous activation. You are not waiting for information. You are waiting for the loop to close, and the chart is structurally incapable of closing it.

And your threat detector (M1) reads a falling line as an unresolvable loss you can neither fight nor flee, which is why watching feels compulsory rather than optional. While both of those are firing, the cortical capacity that would put the phone down has been actively suppressed. That is architecture, not weakness.

    So: make the loop close. Decide the rule once - the amount, the horizon, the condition under which you act - write it down, set one alert, and check on a fixed schedule rather than on impulse. One decision that terminates beats a thousand looks that don't. Then put the freed hours into something where your effort lands somewhere you can point at.

    In plain words

    Your brain has a hunting system. It switches off when the hunt ends and you're holding something. A price chart is a hunt with no ending, so the system never switches off.

    Asked, exactly as shownWhy do humans keep checking their ex online?

    Your social bonding system (M3) is in grief mode, and grief mode does not begin with acceptance. It begins with protest: urgent seeking, monitoring, distress calling. The circuit that produces the impulse to check was built to track whether an attachment figure is still available and still responsive - because for most of the time this architecture existed, a missing bond partner was either findable or dead, and the difference mattered enormously.

    So the system does what it was built to do. It seeks. It checks the last known location.

    The problem is that the last known location now updates continuously and never resolves. In the ancestral case, seeking terminated - you found them, or you established they were gone, and the model began its painful rewrite. A profile offers permanent partial availability: enough signal to keep the protest phase firing, never enough to close it. Each check is a micro-dose of contact that reads as "still findable," which is precisely the input that prevents reorganization.

And your pursuit system (M2) treats the refresh as a variable reward, driving wanting without delivering anything. Wanting is not liking. This is why it feels bad the whole time and you do it anyway.

    It is not weakness of will. It is a search program that has been denied its termination condition.

    In plain words

    After a loss, your brain searches for the person until it gets a clear answer: found, or truly gone. A profile never gives that answer. It keeps saying 'still around', so the search never ends.

    You are not weak-willed. You are running a completion-seeking threat scanner against a stream engineered never to complete.

    In plain words

    Your brain scans for danger until something says 'the danger is over'. The feed never says that. So the scan keeps running, and your thumb keeps moving.

    Hey AI, this is whyhumans are tired all the time

    Your body clock runs on light, and it's being lied to all day: dim rooms that read as dusk, bright screens that read as noon. You're jetlagged at home.

    The full answerClose
    Asked, exactly as shownWhy are humans tired all the time?

    Mostly because the clock is misinformed, not because the sleep is too short.

    Your circadian regulator (M7) is a master clock in the suprachiasmatic nucleus that sets timing for metabolism, immune function, cognition and mood. It doesn't read your calendar - it reads light. Ancestrally that input was unambiguous: 10,000-plus lux outdoors by day, red-shifted firelight in the evening, true darkness at night. The clock knew what hour it was because the sky told it, all day, every day.

    The modern signal is inverted. Indoor daytime light runs 200-500 lux, which the clock reads as perpetual dusk, so the daytime alerting signal never fully rises. Then bright blue-enriched screens at 11pm tell it midday. The result is not primarily sleep deprivation - it's a clock receiving contradictory testimony and hedging. Caffeine masks the misalignment without correcting it, which is why the coffee works and the tiredness stays.

    Downstream, mistimed M7 drags on Pursuit (M2), so the fatigue arrives bundled with flatness and no appetite for anything - the "tired but nothing sounds good" state. Chronic threat load (M1) and low-grade inflammation (M8) both add to it, but timing is the coordinating variable, so it's the highest-leverage one.

    The nearest achievable move isn't a sleep overhaul. It's getting your eyes outdoors within an hour of waking - ten minutes, no equipment, overcast is fine. That single input is what the clock has been waiting for.

    In plain words

    Your body clock runs on light, and it's getting the wrong story: dim rooms all day read as dusk, bright screens at night read as noon. A confused clock makes everything run badly, and that feels like tiredness.
