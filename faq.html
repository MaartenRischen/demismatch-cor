Frequently asked
What it is
What is Cor, in one sentence?

Cor is the science of what a human needs: the systems behind what humans want, feel, fear and chase, what each system needs, and which substitutes set the feeling off and deliver nothing.

Is it an atlas or a specification?

An atlas. It maps what is known and marks what is not. A specification, something a builder or a machine can test against line by line, is what Cor is being built toward.

What is the core idea?

Feeling bad in the modern world is, in most cases, a healthy body and mind reporting a world that is wrong for a human. Anxiety, low mood, loneliness and restlessness are those systems doing their job. Where a doctor can measure a physical cause, that is a disease. Everywhere else the fix is to change the world the human lives in.

Why does that matter beyond psychology?

Nearly everything built for humans assumes that what a human wants shows what is good for that human. It does not. A lonely human can want an AI companion. A human caught in a feed can want the next video. The wanting is real, and it comes from an old system reacting to a fake. Anything that serves the wanting without knowing the system ends up serving the fake. Cor is the record of the systems.

How it is built
Is this made by AI?

Yes, on purpose. AI did the reading, the grading and the source-checking and wrote this site, under one human's direction. Everyone alive grew up inside the modern world, so no human reads it from outside. AI does, when it is held to the studies. Every record carries its source, a DOI or PubMed ID and a word-for-word quote, so nothing has to be taken on trust. Where we stand on AI.

How does a source get into Cor?

Three gates. It has to be real and findable: a DOI, a PubMed ID, or the physical book. Everything taken from it has to come from the full text. Abstracts, snippets and summaries are rejected, because an audit found that every claim taken from a snippet was wrong: invented quotes, drifted paraphrases, findings that were not in the paper. And every quote has to be word for word. A quote stitched together from fragments counts as fabrication. A separate background tier, marked on every record and left out of every count, holds wider reading used on the decode pages.

How do findings become the map?

From the evidence up. A work is read and single findings are pulled from its full text. Each one is graded by how hard it was tested. Where separate lines of research, from different labs, methods and decades, land on the same fact, Cor records a convergence. A convergence that is strong enough forces a system into the map. Five of the fifteen systems are forced that way. The other ten rest on converging evidence without one convergence forcing them, and Cor says so on each. Nothing is bent to fit a theory chosen in advance.

What does the structure look like?

Six layers, fixed: a frame, premises, properties, consequences, convergences, and the mechanisms on top. The premises are that survival and reproduction are what evolution selects for, that the organism is a set of specialised systems working together, and that modern environments push those systems outside the range they were built for. The stack page shows how well each layer is supported: five mechanisms forced by a convergence, ten associated, and two convergences left open on purpose where attaching them to the nearest plausible parent would have been a guess.

What are the fifteen mechanisms?

Threat management, pursuit and exploration, social bonding, social calibration through play, status monitoring, agency, circadian regulation, immune regulation, care and alloparenting, movement, cooperation and alliance, contamination avoidance, energy regulation, reproductive motivation, and touch. Each is a system shaped by evolution, with a defined input it checks for and a defined cost when that input is missing.

How big is Cor?

The live counts are on the Cor home page. Size is the least interesting thing about it. What counts is how hard each record was checked.

How hard it is checked
What is the rule?

Evidence earns its place or it is marked down. In doubt, the grade goes down and never up. "Replicated" means several studies agree. A study that failed to replicate does not get in. Cor's own readings stay marked as theory until they are tested directly.

Books or papers?

Papers. A book describes research and a paper is the research. A claim found in a popular book sends Cor to the original study.

Doesn't it explain away anything that challenges it?

No. Cor keeps records of the evidence against itself, and they stay in. It publishes the strongest attacks on its whole approach, each in the attacker's own words with a verdict, and it has conceded several of them. It names the results that would kill it.

What are its weak points?

Three, all on the record. A real source is not the same as strong evidence: a record can carry a real identifier and an exact quote and still rest on a small study. Ten of the fifteen mechanisms are not forced by a single convergence. And nobody outside the project has re-run the method yet to see whether they land in the same place. Each is logged in the gap register and on the objections page.

Are you saying the caveman life was better?

No. The ancestral environment is the baseline Cor measures against: it shows what the human systems were shaped to receive. It was also short and dangerous, and nobody is asked to go back. What counts is delivering what each system needs, in any form that does it.

Does "mismatch" mean evolution broke us?

The opposite. The systems work. A smoke detector that goes off in a room full of smoke is doing its job. The human's wanting is never the villain. The villain is the gap, and the habit of calling it normal.

How does Cor fit into Demismatch?

Cor is the science. demismatch.com is the front door. whatreally.is reads one subject per page against Cor.

Can other people build on Cor?

Yes. It is public and free to build on, for anyone: builders, schools, clinics, city planners, AI.
