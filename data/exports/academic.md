## How to read this document

This document is a complete printout of the Cor atlas: a framework for locating modern human suffering within an evolutionary-functional architecture rather than within a presumption of individual defect. The central thesis is deflationary with respect to the disorder model and inflationary with respect to signal: where no clear physical defect is the cause, aversive affective and physiological output is accurate biological signaling produced by adaptations operating correctly, but operating on inputs that fall outside the ranges within which those adaptations were calibrated to resolve. Suffering, on this account, is frequently diagnostic information about a mismatched environment, not noise to be suppressed and not evidence of broken design. Preferences are mechanism outputs, not ground truth.

### The derivation stack

The framework is built as a layered derivation in which each layer is constrained by the one beneath it. Only a minority of mechanisms are strictly forced by a dedicated convergence; the rest are grounded in convergent evidence.

- **Frames.** OF1 (Fitness Interface) holds that organismal access to the world is mediated by evolved fitness interfaces, not by guaranteed veridicality. OF2 (Signal-Default Epistemology) treats motivational-emotional outputs as prima facie informative about the organism's regulated conditions, by default. This is a diagnostic default, not a presumption of veridicality (OF1 still stands). Reading the signal as information is no argument against relieving it. The default is displaced in two ways. A clear physical defect displaces it outright, whether a test has found it yet or not, and the output is a symptom of that defect: acute organic disease or state shift (an infection or autoimmune disease, an endocrine disease such as a thyroid fault, delirium), structural damage, a large-effect genetic variant with measurable physical effects, and substance or medication effects. Developmental miscalibration carried forward and chronic dysregulation displace the reading of the current input without making the output a disease, and so do sleep loss and the low-grade inflammation that a mismatched life produces.
- **Premises.** P1 (Inclusive Fitness) makes inclusive fitness the loss function of selection, not of the organism's runtime computation. Inclusive fitness is one measure, Hamilton's: an individual's own reproductive success plus the reproductive success of relatives, weighted by how closely they are related. Survival counts because the dead do not reproduce. P2 (Domain-Sensitive Interacting Adaptations) specifies an organism of evolved, domain-sensitive, interacting functional adaptations rather than a general-purpose learner. P3 (Systematic Mismatch) holds that where modern environments depart from the conditions these adaptations evolved in, they push the adaptations outside their expected or regulatable operating ranges.
- **Properties.** DA1: where no clear physical defect is the cause, aversive outputs are intelligible defensive signals under mismatch; where the line falls is a stance, the clear-defect rule: only a clear physical defect is a disease, found or not yet found, and a marker of sensitivity is no defect. DA2: human regulation is constitutively socially scaffolded, and social context is the highest-leverage cross-cutting input dimension. DA3: systems are recurrently coupled, so perturbations propagate and self-maintain. DA4: proxy cues can activate systems without meeting the conditions that normally regulate or terminate them. DA5: defensive systems err toward over-activation under asymmetric error costs. DA6: the architecture contains partially competing motivational programs and tradeoff structures. DA7: the architecture calibrates to developmental and ongoing input within evolved ranges. DA8: priority runs by brain part; the old parts deep in the brain, similar in all mammals, produce emotion and fear without the newer outer layer, the prefrontal cortex can hold them down, and stress that cannot be controlled, or that lasts, weakens it and switches control of behaviour and emotion to the older circuits. DA9: mechanism resolution conditions are not fully interchangeable; cross-system buffering does not generally replace the specific resolution conditions of an impaired mechanism, and well-being is often bottlenecked by severe unresolved deficits in individual mechanisms rather than well described as a simple average across mechanism states. DA10: architecture parameters (drive gains, thresholds, calibration setpoints) vary between individuals within heritable ranges; heritability here is a population statistic under a given environment distribution, not individual determinism, and it does not weaken the mismatch premise - mismatch shifts the whole distribution regardless of where an individual sits in it.
- **Consequences.** DC1: chronic unresolved activation accumulates as allostatic load. DC2: markets can industrialize proxy exploitation of unmet regulatory needs. DC3: because cortical override of subcortical activation is metabolically expensive and chronic defensive activation downregulates the override machinery itself, interventions that change actual input conditions are frequently more durable and less effort-dependent than override-targeting interventions alone; the environment is therefore a primary, currently underweighted intervention layer. The claim concerns leverage in systemic design and compares no treatments head to head. For medication Cor applies the clear-defect rule and the medication line of its Clinical Practice application: where a defect is the cause the drug may be treating a disease, and where none is the drug gets replaced by the real thing.

### How to read a proxy gradient

Each mechanism carries an ordinal ladder from full resolution to pure proxy. The load-bearing claim of every gradient is this: **the cue is identical at every level; only the resolving function changes.** A proxy can fire the cue without supplying what actually resolves the system. Reading the ladder top to bottom, the detectable cue stays roughly constant (or, in the cases flagged Kind B, the real input is progressively withdrawn or the terminating signal progressively disabled) while the conditions that would terminate the program drop away. The floor levels are where the cue fires and nothing is supplied - the open-loop state where activation cannot resolve. Verdict tags ('resolution met', 'partly resolved', 'fires, no resolution') record this independently of the level's ordinal name. Evidence tags (ARCHITECTURE-DERIVED, SPECULATIVE, CONTESTABLE) record the warrant for each rung; they are reported verbatim where present.

Each section also records the phylogenetic age, the forcing or grounding convergences and their named independent research streams with verbatim evidence-link counts, the cascade topology (which mechanisms a system suppresses, is suppressed by, and interacts with), and any sex-differentiated parameterization.

## M1 - Threat Management

**Phylogenetic age:** ~460-535Myr (defence centre, all vertebrates); amygdala circuit ~340Myr+ (four-limbed vertebrates). **Evidence link count:** 115. **Forced by convergence:** C10 (Threat-Detection via Ancient Subcortical Circuits).

The defensive system responding to threat cues. Includes an offensive mode (frustration-aggression) absorbed from the old M3 RAGE system. In the plainest terms: it detects danger and produces freeze, fight, or flee. **Resolution conditions:** safety established, threat eliminated or avoided.

C10 establishes that threat detection and defensive response are mediated by phylogenetically ancient subcortical circuits (amygdala, periaqueductal gray, hypothalamus) operating pre-attentively and capable of overriding cortical processing. The convergence is supported by Panksepp's FEAR-system neuroanatomy, the Öhman/Mineka backward-masking fear-module research program, and Nesse's clinical evolutionary psychiatry linking panic and anxiety to these defensive systems. Key researchers: Panksepp, LeDoux, Mobbs.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels along a resolvability/actionability axis; a true DA4 proxy appears only at the floor.

- **FULL** (resolution met): acute danger you can fight or flee and then escape
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): a real present threat resolved by a trusted other
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): chronic slow-burn real threat with no terminating action
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): abstract or anticipated threat with no physical target
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): engineered supernormal outrage/threat media (contestable)

**Mismatch prediction:** modern threats - debt, emails, social media, status anxiety - cannot be fought or fled. The system stays on.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M2, M4, M14, M10, and M6. Suppressed by M3, M6, and M15. Interacts with M8.

## M2 - Pursuit / Exploration

**Phylogenetic age:** ~460-535Myr (dopamine basal ganglia, all vertebrates; contested); reward pathway ~165Myr+ (mammals). **Evidence link count:** 67. **Forced by convergence:** C6 (Wanting-Liking Dissociation as Proxy Hijack).

The general-purpose seeking system. The same wanting system also runs when what is wanted is a partner: in human brain imaging the networks for sex are comparable to those for other rewards and follow the same phases of wanting, liking and satiety, and in male rats accumbens dopamine rose again during mating with a new receptive female. What is specific to partners belongs to M14. M2 resolves through engagement with real-world challenges producing tangible outcomes, goal progress, skill building, and resource acquisition. **Resolution conditions:** engagement with real challenges producing tangible outcomes.

C6 establishes that motivational pursuit ('wanting', mediated by mesolimbic dopamine) is dissociable from hedonic satisfaction ('liking'), creating an open-loop vulnerability in which proxy cues can activate the SEEKING system without providing the conditions that normally terminate or resolve it. Established independently by Berridge & Robinson's incentive-salience neuroscience, Tinbergen's supernormal-stimuli ethology, and Panksepp's SEEKING-circuit affective neuroscience. Key researchers: Panksepp, Berridge.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. The cue is constant (SEEKING/wanting); the ladder is ordered by tangible outcome plus consummatory-liking closure supplied, not by cue strength.

- **FULL** (resolution met): engaging a real challenge that yields a real outcome
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): knowledge work with a real but abstracted, delayed outcome
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): variable-reward app/feed with intermittent real payoff
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): infinite novelty, content scroll/idle browsing
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): machine gambling engineered for time-on-device with guaranteed net loss

Note: the most contestable ordering is MIXED-app vs MINIMAL-scroll.

**Mismatch prediction:** variable-reward apps fire SEEKING without resolution. Open loop.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by M1, M13, M3, M7, and M8. Interacts with M4, M14, and M3.

## M3 - Social Bonding

**Phylogenetic age:** ~165-200Myr (attachment, mammals); human layered network not dated. **Evidence link count:** 192. **Forced by convergence:** C5 (Socially Scaffolded Regulation via Attachment).

The attachment and co-regulation system. Includes the grief mode absorbed from the old M9 GRIEF system. It runs in two states: security (enables broadly) and grief (suppresses broadly). It forms bonds with other humans and produces pain-grade distress when they are gone.

**Resolution conditions:** reliable human co-regulation through physical presence, operating through nested social layers - ~5 intimate co-regulators (primary emotional/physical support), ~15 sympathy group, ~50 close social partners, and ~150 coherent face-to-face relationships. Each layer represents qualitatively different relationship types with different obligations and investment requirements. The innermost ~5 provide the majority of social regulation; the critical variable at this layer is reliability, not count. The layer sizes are found among foragers, where periodic multi-group gatherings across 339 societies averaged 165 people (Hamilton et al. 2007), and in modern phone, Christmas-card and Facebook data.

C5 establishes that human regulation is constitutively socially scaffolded: the organism's default expectation is proximity to trusted co-regulators, and this scaffolding is architectural rather than optional. Established independently by Coan's Social Baseline Theory fMRI program, Bowlby's attachment theory, Hrdy's cooperative-breeding thesis, Dunbar's social-brain hypothesis, and Holt-Lunstad's mortality meta-analysis showing a 50% survival advantage from strong social relationships. Key researchers: Bowlby, Panksepp, Coan, Hrdy.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels, ordered by real co-regulatory function supplied, not by amount of contact.

- **FULL** (resolution met): reliable physically co-present inner circle
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): real bonded inner circle held at a distance, remote contact
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): large mediated network routing only partly to real co-presence
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): one-sided parasocial attachment to a non-reciprocating figure
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): AI companion engineered to simulate a reciprocating bond

Note (off-gradient): isolated/solo living is empty-shelf deprivation, not a rung on this ladder.

**Mismatch prediction:** nuclear family, isolated living, and mediated contact substituting for physical presence.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M1, M2, M4, and M10. Suppressed by M13. Interacts with M14 and M2.

## M4 - Social Calibration / Play

**Phylogenetic age:** ~60Myr+ (social play, all primates; up to ~170Myr if one mammal system); play in reptiles and fish arose separately. **Evidence link count:** 55. **Grounded in:** C2, C4, and C12.

The play system develops social skills; it is absent in threatened or ill animals. It learns social rules through rough-and-tumble play and requires real playmates. **Resolution conditions:** self-directed, unsupervised physical play with other children; in forager childhoods this is a mixed-age group.

Three converging streams ground this mechanism. C2 (Domain-Sensitive Organism Architecture) holds the organism contains evolved, domain-sensitive, interacting functional adaptations rather than a general-purpose learner, supported independently by Tooby & Cosmides' design-principle derivation, Panksepp's conserved subcortical affective-motivational systems, and Bowlby's attachment-as-primary-motivational-system framework. C4 (Phylogenetic Conservation of Subcortical Affective Systems) establishes that the core affective-motivational systems (SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY, LUST) are anatomically and functionally conserved across mammals and extend into birds for specific systems, established independently by Panksepp's deep-brain-stimulation and cross-species neuroscience, Bowlby's attachment cross-mammalian homology, and Hrdy's cross-primate parental/alloparental evidence. C12 (Developmental Calibration within Evolved Ranges) holds that specific systems calibrate during specific developmental windows to inputs within evolved parameters, with out-of-parameter inputs producing persistent miscalibration, established independently by Bowlby's attachment developmental calibration, McEwen's HPA-axis calibration neuroendocrinology, and Felitti's ACE Study dose-response evidence. The ACE leg is an association from adults' own recall: for psychiatric outcomes about half of it is carried by genes and family and part stays, and for early death no design that controls for genes or family has tested it. Key researchers: Panksepp, Pellis & Pellis, Gray.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. The cue is approximately a constant PLAY/SEEKING social-joy pull, fired in attenuated or substitute form at lower screen levels; the ladder is ordered by how much real embodied reciprocity with an identity-bearing partner survives.

- **FULL** (resolution met): self-directed, unsupervised physical play with other children, mixed-age as in forager childhoods
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): adult-structured supervised physical activity, organized sports
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): screen-mediated multiplayer play with real peers
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): solitary single-player screen play, no reciprocating partner
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): engineered companion-AI / supernormal variable-reward 'play' product (architecture-derived)

Note: the most contestable ordering is screen-mediated-real-peers (MIXED) ranked above solitary scripted game (MINIMAL).

**Mismatch prediction:** structured activities replacing free play; screens replacing bodies; play deprivation.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by M1, M13, and M3. Interacts with M2.

## M5 - Status Monitoring

**Phylogenetic age:** ~420-445Myr (rank circuit, bony vertebrates); rank-linked health ~25-34Myr+ (Old World primates). **Evidence link count:** 88. **Grounded in:** C2, C7, C9, and C13.

Rank regulation: shame, pride, submission, defeat, rank vigilance. It is not optional and runs continuously, monitoring where one stands relative to others. **Resolution conditions:** an acceptable position in a ~150-person reference group, with earned prestige preferred over coerced dominance (the Henrich & Gil-White distinction, which needs explicit treatment).

Four convergences support this mechanism. C2 (Domain-Sensitive Organism Architecture), supported by Tooby & Cosmides, Panksepp, and Bowlby. C7 (Adverse Experience Cascading Dose-Response), establishing that chronic or developmental adversity cascades across physiological systems in a dose-response manner with effects that propagate and self-maintain across metabolic, immune, neural, and behavioral domains, established independently by Felitti's ACE Study clinical epidemiology, McEwen's allostatic-load neuroendocrinology, and Panksepp's cross-system inhibition evidence. The ACE leg is an association from adults' own recall: for psychiatric outcomes about half of it is carried by genes and family and part stays, and for early death no design that controls for genes or family has tested it. C9 (Allostatic Load Accumulation), establishing cumulative multi-organ damage from chronic unresolved stress activation, partly reversible by environmental correction, established independently by McEwen's neuroendocrinology of allostasis, Felitti's ACE dose-response epidemiology, and Sapolsky's primate stress physiology linking hierarchy type to cortisol and downstream pathology. C13 (Aversive Outputs as Intelligible Defensive Signals), established independently by Nesse's 'good reasons for bad feelings', Panksepp's affective neuroscience, and Fehr & Gächter's finding that moralistic outrage is the proximate mechanism for prosocial norm enforcement rather than a dysregulation. Key researchers: Gilbert, Price, Mazur, Sapolsky, Marmot.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. The cue is approximately a constant comparison/ranking signal, ordered primarily by real standing supplied in a real knowable group, with prestige > dominance quality folded in at the top.

- **FULL** (resolution met): earned prestige in a small face-to-face group
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): coerced/unstable dominance rank in a real group
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): status-striving in a large impersonal society
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): global mediated comparison by follower/like counts (architecture-derived)
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): purchased status signals, luxury-brand display (architecture-derived)

Note: the most contestable point is the floor ordering of purchased-signals (ZERO) vs social-media comparison (MINIMAL).

**Mismatch prediction:** global comparison via social media, with millions of "peers," producing a status-defeat cascade.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by nothing recorded. Interacts with M14 and M8.

## M6 - Controllability / Agency

**Phylogenetic age:** ~60-90Myr+ (placental mammals; possibly ~165-200Myr, all mammals; contested). **Evidence link count:** 38. **Grounded in:** no convergence linked.

The vmPFC-DRN controllability circuit. Critically, learned helplessness is the DEFAULT; agency is the override. This mechanism is central to breaking cascades. It tracks whether actions produce results; the default is passivity, and agency must be learned. **Resolution conditions:** genuine action-outcome feedback and decision latitude.

Key researchers: Maier & Seligman.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. This is a Kind A gradient, but the cue is detection of response-outcome contingency rather than a constant appetitive drive; the ladder is ordered by real causal efficacy over fitness-relevant outcomes supplied.

- **FULL** (resolution met): decision latitude over consequential, visibly-feedbacking outcomes
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): high demand with reduced but real decision latitude
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): abstract/surveilled/screen-mediated work where effort doesn't visibly register
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): chronically uncontrollable real environment, learned helplessness
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): engineered simulated-agency loop (slot machine/game/dashboard) firing the contingency cue with zero real consequence (architecture-derived)

Note: this is dual-axis. PARTIAL to MINIMAL withdraw real contingency; ZERO substitutes a false one. The most contestable ordering is MINIMAL vs ZERO.

**Mismatch prediction:** abstract work where effort doesn't visibly register; rigid hierarchies; bureaucratic systems.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M1. Suppressed by M1. No interactions recorded.

## M7 - Circadian Regulation

**Phylogenetic age:** ~500Myr+ (animal clock genes, insects and vertebrates); cyanobacterial clocks are older but a separate origin. **Evidence link count:** 20. **Grounded in:** C3, C9, and C12.

The SCN master clock. It gates metabolism, immunity, cognition, and mood. The mismatch is usually one of timing or quality, not duration. It synchronizes every system to the light-dark cycle and is the master timing architecture. **Resolution conditions:** dark-cued sleep onset, bright daylight exposure, and a consistent rhythm.

C3 (Systematic EEA-Modern Environment Mismatch) holds that modern post-agricultural, post-industrial environments push evolved adaptations outside their expected operating ranges in systematic and predictable ways, established independently by Eaton & Konner's discordance hypothesis, the Li/van Vugt/Colarelli mismatch-identification framework, Boyd & Richerson's gene-culture coevolution constraint (matched must include cultural scaffolding), and Henrich's WEIRD empirical diagnostic showing Western populations anchor the extreme end of the cross-cultural distribution. C9 (Allostatic Load Accumulation) and C12 (Developmental Calibration within Evolved Ranges) co-ground it, with their named streams as above. Key researchers: Walker, Roenneberg, Czeisler.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. This is a Kind B gradient: not a constant-cue hijack but a case where the entraining light signal itself degrades and is finally inverted; ordered by per-individual signal degradation.

- **FULL** (resolution met): outdoor light-dark cycle with dark-cued sleep
- **ATTENUATED** (resolution met): indoor daytime light with a mostly-dark, dimly-lit night
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): correctly-phased clock overridden by a misaligned social schedule / social jetlag
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): shift work and always-on connectivity with the dark cue largely gone
- **DEFEATED** (fires, no resolution): evening blue-light screens actively mis-setting the clock, false daylight at biological night

Note: the most contestable points are the social-jetlag (PARTIAL) vs shift-work (MINIMAL) ordering, and whether screens' signal-inversion is the true floor.

**Mismatch prediction:** electric light, blue screens, shift work, caffeine.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M2. Suppressed by nothing recorded. Interacts with M10, M8, and M13.

## M8 - Immune Regulation

**Phylogenetic age:** ~550-830Myr (innate immunity, animals; contested); adaptive immunity ~420-470Myr (jawed vertebrates). **Evidence link count:** 37. **Grounded in:** C3, C7, and C9.

The cytokine signaling system. It overrides voluntary intention and is the hub connecting inflammation to low mood via the DA3 cascade. In plainest terms, it produces sickness behavior. **Resolution conditions:** pathogen cleared, inflammation resolved.

C3 (Systematic EEA-Modern Environment Mismatch), with its named streams as above. C7 (Adverse Experience Cascading Dose-Response), established independently by Felitti, McEwen, and Panksepp. C9 (Allostatic Load Accumulation), established independently by McEwen, Felitti, and Sapolsky. Key researchers: Dantzer, Danese, Irwin.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. This is a Kind B gradient: the trigger drifts from a real clearable pathogen toward psychosocial/lifestyle signals with nothing to clear, while the inflammatory off-switch degrades; ordered by how achievable clear-to-resolve-to-baseline remains.

- **FULL** (resolution met): acute infection/injury fought off and fully resolved
- **ATTENUATED** (resolution met): recurrent acute stress resolving each time but with incomplete recovery
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): sustained lifestyle drivers (sedentarism, poor diet, sleep loss) keeping inflammation simmering
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): chronic social isolation/status stress defeating the inflammatory off-switch
- **DEFEATED** (fires, no resolution): self-sustaining chronic low-grade inflammation, allostatic-load collapse

Note: the most contestable point is lifestyle-drivers (PARTIAL) ranked above social-isolation (MINIMAL) - these could legitimately swap or collapse.

**Mismatch prediction:** chronic low-grade inflammation from diet, sedentarism, and social isolation.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M2 and M13. Suppressed by M10. Interacts with M12, M1, M5, and M7.

## M9 - Care / Alloparenting

**Phylogenetic age:** ~165-200Myr (maternal care, mammals); human cooperative breeding ~2Myr+ (argued). **Evidence link count:** 64. **Grounded in:** C1, C4, C5, and C12.

The parental and alloparental care system, running on oxytocin/prolactin circuits. The architecture expects distributed care - a village, not solo parenting. **Resolution conditions:** a distributed care network rather than solo or dyadic parenting. In forager societies people other than the mother hold and care for infants: Hadza allomothers hold infants under four about 31% of the time, and Efe infants are cared for by many people from birth. The critical resolving variable is the presence and continuity of real alloparental supply (care plus provisioning), NOT bare kinship.

Four convergences ground this mechanism. C1 (Inclusive Fitness as Loss Function), demonstrated independently by Darwinian selection theory and Hamilton's kin-selection formalism. C4 (Phylogenetic Conservation of Subcortical Affective Systems), established by Panksepp, Bowlby, and Hrdy. C5 (Socially Scaffolded Regulation via Attachment), established by Coan, Bowlby, Hrdy, Dunbar, and Holt-Lunstad. C12 (Developmental Calibration within Evolved Ranges), established by Bowlby, McEwen, and Felitti. Key researchers: Hrdy, Panksepp, Kenkel.

### Proxy gradient

This mechanism has TWO SEPARATE scales (axes), not one ladder. They are presented separately and must never be merged.

**Axis A - real alloparental supply** (an empty-shelf/deprivation axis, ordered by caregiver count × continuity, NOT kinship):

- **FULL** (resolution met): distributed kin-and-community network
- **REDUCED** (resolution met): multiple reliable caregivers including high-quality paid care
- **THIN** (partly resolved): single intermittent helper / high-turnover institutional care
- **EMPTY SHELF** (fires, no resolution): solo/dyadic parent, no network (deprivation floor, not a proxy)

**Axis B - fake-fix proxy** (real alloparental function ≈ 0 for both, ordered by completeness of the caregiver-OTHER counterfeit):

- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): screen/app babysitter to AI/robot 'caregiver' (architecture-derived)

**Mismatch prediction:** nuclear family without an alloparental network; the parent-burnout cascade.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M14. Suppressed by nothing recorded. No interactions recorded.

## M10 - Movement

**Phylogenetic age:** ~2Myr (human form, argued); older regulatory roles not dated. **Evidence link count:** 29. **Grounded in:** C3.

Physical activity functioning as a zeitgeber, anti-inflammatory, and mood regulator. Movement is not merely exercise: it activates repair mechanisms throughout the organism. **Resolution conditions:** movement that is part of daily life rather than set aside as 'exercise'. Hadza men walk about 11 km a day and women about 6 km, as a side effect of foraging.

C3 (Systematic EEA-Modern Environment Mismatch), with its named streams as above. Key researchers: Pontzer, Lieberman, Ratey.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. This is a Kind B gradient that inverts the usual proxy story - the real input is withdrawn, not a false cue fired; ordered by how much of the all-day muscle-activity distribution is supplied.

- **FULL** (resolution met): all-day incidental movement of foraging life, locomotion plus active rest postures
- **ATTENUATED** (resolution met): a physically active job, farming/manual labour/walking-heavy trade
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): mostly sedentary day broken up by walking, standing and fidgeting / incidental NEAT
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): sedentary day plus a discrete gym/exercise session, a partial re-supply not a false cue
- **DEFEATED** (fires, no resolution): chair-bound sedentarism with essentially no movement

Note: the most contestable point is the gym (MINIMAL) ranked below incidental all-day NEAT (PARTIAL) - this would swap on a fitness/mortality rather than distribution axis.

**Mismatch prediction:** sedentarism, with gym sessions as partial compensation.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M8. Suppressed by M1 and M3. Interacts with M7 and M13.

## M11 - Cooperation / Alliance

**Phylogenetic age:** ~25-34Myr+ (reciprocity, Old World primates); human norm enforcement under ~10Myr. **Evidence link count:** 73. **Forced by convergence:** C11 (Reciprocity, Norm Enforcement, and Coalition Architecture).

Reciprocity plus norm enforcement plus alliance dynamics. This mechanism absorbs the old M10 (Cooperation) and M11 (Coalitional). Convergence C11 forces M11. Three bodies of tests support it (Fehr & Gächter, Boehm, Tomasello), with Trivers 1971 as the theory behind them. In plainest terms: reciprocal exchange, cheater detection, and coalitional identity.

**Resolution conditions:** a trusted reciprocal network with norm enforcement within a group small enough for direct reputation tracking (on the order of ~150, the size found among foragers and in modern networks). Norm enforcement requires repeated direct interaction where defectors can be identified, remembered, and sanctioned. Beyond that size, cooperation requires institutional scaffolding (formal rules, third-party enforcement, shared fictions) rather than direct reciprocity. There is no shared fiction at the direct-reciprocity layer.

C11 establishes that humans have specialized architecture for reciprocal exchange, cheater detection, norm enforcement, and coalitional identity, supported independently by Fehr & Gächter's experimental altruistic-punishment findings, Boehm's reverse-dominance coalition dynamics in hunter-gatherer anthropology, and Tomasello's shared-intentionality comparative-cognition research. Key researchers: Trivers, Boehm, Fehr & Gächter, Tomasello.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. The cue is approximately a constant alliance/reciprocity machinery. FULL to PARTIAL is resolution-via-prosthesis - institutions re-supply the same function beyond ~150 - so proxy failure begins at MIXED; ordered by how much enforceable reciprocal function reaches a real reputation-bearing counterparty.

- **FULL** (resolution met): trusted reciprocal network under ~150 with direct reputation tracking and norm enforcement
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): institutionally-scaffolded cooperation beyond ~150, formal rules / third-party enforcement / shared fictions
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): market exchange among strangers backed by gameable reputation systems
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): coalition-signaling and parasocial alliance via symbolic in-group markers
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): anonymous one-shot transactions and AI 'allies' with no reputation and no shared narrative (architecture-derived)

Note: the most contestable point is the PARTIAL/MIXED boundary - where proxy failure begins.

**Mismatch prediction:** anonymous transactions, ghosting, no shared narrative.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by nothing recorded. No interactions recorded.

## M12 - Contamination Avoidance

**Phylogenetic age:** ~25-34Myr+ (contamination avoidance, Old World primates); parasite avoidance across animals has no single age. **Evidence link count:** 12. **Grounded in:** C2 and C8.

Three kinds of disgust, pathogen, sexual and moral: ratings of how disgusting acts and objects are fall into these three factors. How the moral kind began is left open. The behavioral immune system. **Resolution conditions:** clean food, clean social environment, moral disgust contained.

C2 (Domain-Sensitive Organism Architecture), supported by Tooby & Cosmides, Panksepp, and Bowlby. C8 (Error Management Asymmetry in Defensive Systems), established by Nesse, Haselton & Buss, and the Öhman/Mineka program. Key researchers: Rozin, Curtis, Schaller.

### Proxy gradient

A 3-rung pathogen-disgust gradient (FULL to PARTIAL to MIXED).

- **FULL** (resolution met): clean food and clean social environment, disgust firing on real pathogen cues
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): vulnerability-calibrated over-firing, e.g. pregnancy/immunosuppression
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): behavioral-immune smoke-detector false positives on harmless people (floor)

**Moral disgust, the third kind, has no rungs on this ladder:** purity-coded social aversion and weaponized moral disgust (purity spirals). How the moral kind began is left open. Tybur et al. 2013 treat moral disgust as a separate adaptation that shares mainly its facial and vocal expression with pathogen disgust (a theoretical framework). A meta-analysis of 50 studies finds that incidental disgust does not amplify moral judgment once publication bias is corrected (Landy & Goodwin 2015, d = -0.01).

**Mismatch prediction:** moral disgust weaponized in politics, purity spirals.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by nothing recorded. Interacts with M8.

## M13 - Energy Regulation

**Phylogenetic age:** ~420Myr+ (appetite brake, jawed vertebrates); human energy budget under ~10Myr. **Evidence link count:** 34. **Grounded in:** C3, C6, and C9.

Pontzer's constrained energy model: energy is redistributed, not simply burned. The mechanism comprises hunger, satiety, and constrained total energy expenditure. **Resolution conditions:** effortful, diverse, seasonal whole foods; metabolic variety.

C3 (Systematic EEA-Modern Environment Mismatch), with its named streams as above. C6 (Wanting-Liking Dissociation as Proxy Hijack), established by Berridge & Robinson, Tinbergen, and Panksepp. C9 (Allostatic Load Accumulation), established by McEwen, Felitti, and Sapolsky. Key researchers: Pontzer, Keys.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels. This is a Kind B gradient: the satiety / intake-termination signal itself is progressively disabled then actively defeated, not a constant cue hijacked. The scale is food-processing intensity, ordered by how much regulatory function the food permits.

- **FULL** (resolution met): effortful, diverse, seasonal whole foods
- **ATTENUATED** (resolution met): minimally processed traditional whole foods
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): conventionally processed energy-dense food
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): energy-dense palatable food optimized for crave
- **DEFEATED** (fires, no resolution): ultra-processed bliss-point food, an engineered supernormal stimulus

Note: the middle three architecture-derived. The most contestable point is whether the ATTENUATED/PARTIAL/MINIMAL middle is three real tiers or a bimodal whole-vs-ultra-processed split.

**Mismatch prediction:** ultra-processed foods defeating satiety signals.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses M2, M3, M4, and M14. Suppressed by M8. Interacts with M10 and M7.

## M14 - Reproductive Motivation

**Phylogenetic age:** ~460-535Myr (sexual hormone axis, all vertebrates); human pair bond ~2-4Myr (contested). **Evidence link count:** 58. **Forced by convergence:** C14 (Reproductive Motivation as Distinct Architecture).

Fisher's three-system model, running on the HPG axis, not just brain circuits. The three components - desire, attraction, and pair bonding - can work independently of one another, and this system shapes much more of human behavior than reproduction itself. **Resolution conditions:** sexual fulfillment within a pair bond, plus pair-bond stability, plus reproductive success.

C14: within reproductive motivation (desire, attraction, pair-bonding), sexual excitement runs on a distinct affective system, LUST, one of at least seven basic affective systems that Panksepp's cross-species brain stimulation, lesion and drug studies find in the ancient subcortical regions of mammalian brains. Men and women differ in what they prefer in a mate: across 37 cultures (Buss 1989), and again in 14,399 people in 45 countries (2016 sample), men on average preferred young and attractive partners more than women did, and women preferred partners with good financial prospects more than men did. As Haselton and Buss's error-management theory predicted, in their studies women underestimated men's commitment and men overestimated women's sexual intent. As Demismatch's own position, deduced from mismatch: everything that engages reproductive motivation sits on one ranking, from a real partner down to a pure stand-in such as pornography or an AI partner, ordered by how much of what reproductive motivation needs it supplies. Key researchers: Panksepp, Fisher, Buss, Trivers, Hrdy.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels, top (need met) to bottom (cue fires, nothing supplied).

- **FULL** (resolution met): real partner
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): attenuated real / LDR
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): partial proxy / dating app to real meeting
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): pure proxy soft / romance fiction / parasocial
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): pure proxy hard / pornography / AI sexual

**Mismatch prediction:** pornography, dating apps, delayed reproduction, social media mate-value comparison.

### Cascade topology

Suppresses nothing recorded. Suppressed by M1, M13, and M9. Interacts with M3, M5, and M2.

## M15 - Touch

**Phylogenetic age:** ~60-90Myr (C-tactile touch fibres, placental mammals). **Evidence link count:** 18. **Grounded in:** C4 and C5.

M15 is touch, an input to M3, carried by C-tactile afferents. **Resolution conditions:** regular physical contact with trusted others.

C4 (Phylogenetic Conservation of Subcortical Affective Systems), established by Panksepp, Bowlby, and Hrdy. C5 (Socially Scaffolded Regulation via Attachment), established by Coan, Bowlby, Hrdy, Dunbar, and Holt-Lunstad. Key researchers: McGlone, Field.

### Proxy gradient

Five levels, flagged CONTESTED - possibly Kind C, and NOT a flagship. The cue-function separability premise is disputed (per #1584). Ordered by remaining relational/bond function; note that monotonic_ok = False, because MINIMAL and ZERO both have zero relational function and are split only by a secondary cue-magnitude axis.

- **FULL** (resolution met): affectionate touch from a trusted, reciprocating other
- **PARTIAL** (partly resolved): affective touch from a non-intimate caregiver / massage
- **MIXED** (partly resolved): incidental, non-affective everyday contact
- **MINIMAL** (fires, no resolution): weighted / deep-pressure substitute, no living other (speculative; /future-conditional)
- **ZERO** (fires, no resolution): synthetic/haptic/AI C-tactile stimulation, zero relational embedding (speculative; /future-conditional)

**Mismatch prediction:** touch-starved modern environments. Kangaroo-care evidence shows large developmental and autonomic gains from skin-to-skin care.

Suppresses M1. Suppressed by nothing recorded. No interactions recorded.

### Social structure

#### Social network layers (~5/15/50/150)
`EEA-SOC-01 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** The ancestral social world was structured as a series of hierarchically inclusive, nested relationship layers, each roughly three times the size of the one inside it: an innermost support clique of ~5 close confidants who provided emotional and physical support, a sympathy group of ~15 (contacted at least monthly), an affinity/band layer of ~50 (the typical overnight foraging camp), and an active personal network of ~150 (the outer ceiling of stable, individually-known relationships). Each layer carried a qualitatively different grade of intimacy and contact frequency, with the heaviest investment concentrated in the inner ~5-15. These sizes are found among foragers: across 339 forager societies, aggregated residential groups averaged 54 people and periodic multi-group gatherings 165 (ext 1048). Measured residential camps are often smaller: the median residential group across 263 forager societies was 27 people (ext 754), and Hadza camps center near 30 individuals (ext 603). They are found again in modern networks (modern side below). This was the full architecture within which status was monitored and alliances tracked: a personally-known reference group bounded at ~150, not an anonymous mass. Human social groups cluster at characteristic values of approximately 5, 12, 35, 150, 500, and 2,000 (ext 490). Forager populations were organized in nested levels, with each level about three to four times larger than the one below it, from families through camps to regional groups (ext 685).

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - unit: individuals
  - layer_sizes: {"affinity_band": "50, the typical hunter-gatherer overnight camp (ext 404); 35, the band (ext 490); 27, the median residential group across 263 forager societies (ext 754)", "active_network": "150 (ext 404, 490)", "support_clique": "5 (ext 404, 490)", "sympathy_group": "15 (ext 404); 12 (ext 490)"}
  - scaling_factor: approximately three (ext 404); about three to four in forager population structure, a constant ratio close to 4 (ext 685)
  - outer_ceiling_approx: 150
  - tribal_clan_grouping: clusters in the low hundreds (ethnographic)

**Modern default.** The modern environment delivers a radically inverted network shape: nominal friend counts that for a small minority run to many hundreds or thousands (ext 4624), while the layers that actually carry support and intimacy stay under the same ceiling. Online networks reproduce the same ~5/15 inner structure when genuine ties are measured (support clique ~4, sympathy group ~14, not significantly different from offline values), and mean platform friend counts hover near ~150-190 (across all 721 million Facebook users of May 2011 the mean was about 190 and the median 99; Ugander et al. 2011, ext 4624), but the bulk of nominal connections are not considered close friends (only ~27.6% of Facebook friends are considered 'genuine' (i.e. close) friends, ext 508). Dunbar proposes that face-to-face meetings are needed from time to time to keep friendships from sliding down the network layers until they become mere acquaintances, and that social media may slow this decay and cannot replace in-person contact (ext 509). The result is a wide, shallow periphery of weak or fictive ties layered on top of a core that the larger network does not enlarge: among 117 adults, more time on social media went with more online friends and with no larger or emotionally closer offline network (Pollet, Roberts and Dunbar 2011).

**The gap.** The mismatch is between the number of ties the modern environment presents (a mean of about 190 Facebook friends, and many hundreds or thousands for a small minority, ext 4624) and the number of people a person actually knows and invests in, found among foragers and in modern networks (~150, with real support concentrated in the inner ~5-15). People with large networks had on average weaker ties, measured as time spent calling (ext 4349). Status monitoring (M5) and alliance/reputation tracking (M11), evolved for a ~150 personally-known reference group, are now flooded by reference groups, while the trust-bearing inner layers remain capped and are under-fed when face-to-face contact is displaced.

**Serves mechanisms:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding, M5 - Status Monitoring

#### Band / camp size (a few dozen people)
`EEA-SOC-03 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Ancestral humans lived in small, residentially co-present foraging bands (camps): a single overnight camp typically held on the order of a few dozen people - a median residential group of 27 people across 263 forager societies (ext 754), and about 30 individuals in Hadza camps (ext 603) - a scale that sits within the Dunbar 'band' layer (~35) and 'overnight camp' layer (~50) of the nested social network. At this scale every co-resident is a personally known, named individual whose behaviour can be directly observed and tracked over time.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - units: co-resident people in a single band / overnight camp
  - source_status: Band about 35 (Dunbar 1998, ext 490) and overnight camp about 50 (Dunbar 2010, ext 404). Median residential group of 27 people across 263 forager societies (Marlowe 2005, ext 754).   - dunbar_band_layer: 35
  - dunbar_overnight_camp_layer: 50
  - mean_experienced_band_size_adults: 28.2

**Modern default.** Modern humans do not live in a single bounded, personally-known residential band. Daily life is partitioned across many large, partly anonymous groupings - workplaces, apartment buildings, transit systems, schools, and online networks. The small, stable, fully-known co-resident group of a few dozen people has no direct modern equivalent; its functions are split across institutions.

**The gap.** The ancestral social field was a small set of a few dozen personally-known people sharing a camp, sitting within the band/overnight-camp Dunbar layer. The modern default replaces this with chronic immersion in groupings far larger than the band scale. The delta is a mismatch between the scale at which our cooperation, status-monitoring, and bonding machinery was calibrated (a bounded, fully-known band/camp of a few dozen people) and the scale we actually inhabit (large, partly anonymous aggregations plus a thin set of disposable ties), so the band-sized layer that once carried daily social life is no longer delivered by any single modern structure.

#### Within-camp kin fraction (low; campmates mostly unrelated)
`EEA-SOC-04 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** A person's residential camp/band was NOT a clan of close relatives. Across forager societies the typical residential group (~28 adults) was composed mostly of individuals to whom the focal person was not closely related: primary/first-order kin (parents, siblings, offspring) made up only a small minority of co-residents (under 10% of a residential camp; Apicella et al. 2012, ext 601), and most campmates were unrelated or only distantly related. An agent-based model suggests that, even if all individuals seek to live with as many kin as possible, within-camp relatedness is reduced if men and women have equal influence in selecting camp members (Dyble et al. 2015, ext 687).

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

**Modern default.** Modern social environments invert the ancestral pattern in two opposite directions at once. (1) The family one is related to is, for about two in three US adults aged 25 or over with a living parent or adult child, not all within 30 miles (three in four have one within 30 miles, about a third have all of them that near, and 13 percent share a home with one; Choi et al. 2020, ext 4204). (2) The larger groups one spends daily life inside (workplace, neighborhood, online networks, institutions) are NOT self-assembled through mutual choice, reciprocity, and reputation tracking; membership is assigned by employment, address, or platform. The result is that 'who you live and work among' is no longer the output of a relationship-selection process; it is administratively or commercially determined.

**The gap.** The ancestral signal was: 'the group around me is mostly unrelated people I (or my band) actively chose and continuously vetted through reciprocity and reputation; kinship is not what holds us together, the relationships are.' The modern signal is degraded on the selection axis: the unrelated people around us are still mostly unrelated (that part matches), but they are assigned rather than chosen, and the reciprocity/reputation machinery that turned unrelated campmates into durable trusted allies is largely absent or shallow. Mechanisms M3 (bonding) and M11 (cooperation/alliance) evolved expecting that low kin-relatedness would be compensated by self-selected, reciprocal, reputation-anchored dyads. Modernity delivers the low relatedness WITHOUT the compensating chosen-and-vetted dyadic structure, so the mechanisms fire in a world of unrelated-but-also-unbonded co-residents, which is a key part of why being surrounded by people can still register as social isolation.

#### Membership turnover (camp dissolves and reconstitutes; most campmates change between survey waves)

#### Selection rule / "shuffle algorithm" (choice-driven; homophily + cooperation + reciprocity)
`EEA-SOC-06 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**EEA baseline.** Who you lived alongside was produced by an ongoing, choice-driven assortment process operating across frequent residential reshuffling. In Hadza camps this "algorithm" had recurring structure: (1) homophily - in both the campmate and gift networks, social ties were more likely between people similar in age, height, weight, body fat, and handgrip strength; (2) strong reciprocity - in the campmate network an ego was roughly 37-51x (point ~44x) more likely to name an alter who named them; (3) cooperator assortment - cooperators clustered with cooperators, an association extending out to two degrees of separation in gift networks; plus transitivity and geographic decay. #### Relationship longevity & maintenance (durable dyads; maintenance institutions like hxaro conserve bonds across distance)
`EEA-SOC-07 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**EEA baseline.** In ancestral foraging life the load-bearing social unit was the durable, reciprocal DYAD, not the group. Groups continuously reshuffled (entire camps shifted location every 4 to 8 weeks, with most campmates changing from one survey wave to the next). Among the Ju/'hoansi, hxaro was a formal delayed-reciprocity gift-partnership system with ties passed down across generations (ext 2272), spanning dispersed bands up to ~200 km apart; the average person spent 4.4 months a year living with hxaro partners (ext 752). Trivers's conditions for the evolution of reciprocal altruism describe exactly this regime: a long lifetime, low dispersal, and repeated interaction with the same small set of individuals, so that an investment in a specific partner reliably paid back over time.

#### Novel-stranger frequency
`EEA-SOC-08 · Social structure` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** Social cognition operated over a bounded pool: a concurrent active network capped at about 150 people, nested in ~5/15/50/150 layers of decreasing intimacy. Within that architecture almost every face encountered was already known, placed, and reputationally tracked. A genuinely novel stranger, an individual who could not be located anywhere in one's known network or in the known networks of one's allies, was therefore a comparatively rare event. Because the bounded pool had no slot for such a person, novelty itself was diagnostic: a never-before-seen individual could not be vouched for, had no shared reputational history, and could not be assumed to be bound by the local web of reciprocity and norm enforcement. Encounters with unplaceable strangers were thus both infrequent and highly salient, and the threat-management system (M1) treated unfamiliarity as a plausible danger cue to be resolved (placed, vouched-for, or avoided) rather than as a routine background condition. This is an inference from the bounded-pool architecture plus M1, not a directly measured ancestral rate.

**Modern default.** The M1 machinery still treats unplaceable others as potential threats.

**The gap.** The delta is one of base rate and resolvability. Ancestrally, novel strangers were rare, salient, and resolvable (place them in the known web, or treat unfamiliarity as an actionable threat signal); modernity inverts this to constant, non-diagnostic, unresolvable novelty. The threat-management system inherited from the rare-stranger regime cannot down-regulate to the new base rate: it either over-fires (chronic low-grade vigilance, stranger-directed wariness in crowds and online) or, where it habituates, leaves people transacting with a stream of others who carry none of the reputational accountability the system implicitly assumes. The conserved unit being eroded is not the count of strangers but the durable, reputationally-tracked dyad against which any new face used to be evaluated; once almost everyone is a stranger, the bounded-pool premise that made novelty meaningful no longer holds.

**Resolution unit.** The durable, reputationally-tracked reciprocal dyad: ancestrally every new face was evaluated against a finite web of known, vouched-for relationships, and it was that web of bonds - not the count of strangers per se - that gave novelty its diagnostic and protective meaning. The mismatch resolves only insofar as new encounters can be anchored back into durable, accountable relationships rather than left as anonymous one-off contacts.

**Serves mechanisms:** M1 - Threat Management, M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding

#### Behavioral observability / reputation visibility
`EEA-SOC-09 · Social structure` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** In a band/camp embedded in a personally-known reference group, a person's relevant behavior was directly observable by, or transmitted through gossip to, people who knew them by name and knew their history. Reputation was a shared, continuously-updated, distributed record: as Boehm puts it, "the band keeps a dossier on every individual, noting positive and negative points." Where campmates differed, they knew it: asked one by one in 12 Hadza camps, they agreed more on who hunts best (r 0.23) and a little more on who works hardest (r 0.08). They barely agreed on who shares the most food, who tells the fewest lies and who has the best heart (ext 2795). Project position (21 Nov 2026): anyone still in camp shared and was honest enough to be kept there, so on those points there was little left to rank, and such labels sort strangers more than campmates. The study's authors read the low agreement as moral behaviour that changes across time and situations to fit local circumstances (ext 2796). Observability had teeth: those who deviated (theft, deception, stinginess, free-riding) were subject to gossip, ridicule, ostracism, and ultimately exclusion, and everyone knew this monitoring was constant, so it functioned as a deterrent and most foragers tried to exert self-control accordingly. Tomasello argues that because foraging was obligately collaborative, individuals had to evaluate partners and track how they themselves were being evaluated (ext 239). The net result: visibility was embodied, hard to fake over time, attached to a single durable identity, and backed by real consequences.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - identity: single durable identity
  - observability: within band/camp; gossip diffusion
  - accountability: gossip, ridicule, ostracism, exclusion
  - reference_group_basis: personally-known, named, repeat-interaction reference group

**Modern default.** The visibility that does exist is mediated, curated, and quantitatively inflated. On average, UK respondents considered only 27.6% of their Facebook friends genuine close friends (with a strong modal value between 0 and 10%), so networks are padded with weakly-accountable acquaintances rather than people tracking one's actual conduct. Mediated contact cannot substitute for the embodied, face-to-face observation the bonding/reputation system evolved for: Dunbar proposes that without periodic in-person contact, ties decay through the network layers into mere acquaintance, and that social media may slow this decay (ext 509). Visibility is now selectively self-presented (gameable), fragmented across multiple personas and contexts, and policed (if at all) by distant institutions or algorithms rather than by named peers with whom one shares a future. Reputation is either absent (anonymity), sortable away (exit/blocking, fresh accounts), or captured by platform metrics.

**The gap.** The ancestral environment delivered embodied, hard-to-fake, single-identity behavioral observability inside a reference group whose members knew one's history and held real enforcement power. The modern environment delivers visibility that is mediated, curated, gameable, fragmented across exitable contexts, padded with non-accountable weak ties, and largely stripped of peer-enforced consequences. The reputation-tracking machinery (M11) and status-position monitoring (M5) evolved to run on dense, durable, accountable observation now operate on thin, performative, low-accountability signals, producing both reputational impunity for defection among strangers and chronic exposure of curated personas to vast unfamiliar audiences with no reciprocal knowledge of the person behind them.

#### What marks someone as "them" (alliance cues)
`EEA-SOC-19 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Foragers marked groups by language and dialect, and, Kurzban, Tooby and Cosmides argue, rarely met people genetically distant enough to look like another race (ext 1052). The tested part: the mind sorts people by alliance, and the sorting can be redrawn within minutes. When team membership cut across race, US participants sorted people by race less (a preregistered replication with 463 people found the drop at a smaller size; race was not erased). Arbitrary labels produce favouritism toward one's own group; a meta-analysis of cooperation experiments gives d 0.32, larger when outcomes depend on each other, and driven more by favouring 'us' than by harming 'them'.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - ingroup_favouritism_d: 0.32 overall; 0.19 bare labels; 0.42 interdependent (Balliet et al. 2014)

**Modern default.** Large labelled groups (parties, nations, teams) supply alliance cues without any shared life. The rise is not general: across 12 OECD democracies six saw it fall. Posts about the political out-group are shared more (each out-group word about 67% higher odds, US media and Congress). #### Food shared and eaten in company

### Mating & reproduction

#### Who picks the partner (kin-arranged marriage)
`EEA-MATE-01 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested once**

**EEA baseline.** In most forager societies parents or close kin arranged marriages. In Apostolou's coding of 190 forager societies, arrangement by parents or close kin was the main way to marry in 85%, and marriage involved an exchange between the two families (brideservice, brideprice) in 80%. A phylogenetic reconstruction puts arranged marriage at least as far back as the migrations out of Africa. Courtship also existed: 3 of the 8 African forager societies in the sample marry by courtship, so the oldest African pattern is open. How much say the young people had is not coded in any source read; Hadza men and women state clear preferences for a spouse. Among the Tsimane forager-horticulturalists of Bolivia, asked why they married their spouse, women most often answered that they married against their will (37.6%, ext 5850). Among Himba pastoralists of Namibia, 9% of women's first marriages were love matches against 62% of second marriages (ext 5856). Across five non-Western societies, scores for intimacy, passion and commitment did not differ between arranged and free-choice marriages in the pooled data (ext 5859).

#### Marriage system (mostly one spouse at a time, often several over a life, polygyny allowed in most societies)
`EEA-MATE-03 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Most societies in the ethnographic record permitted a man more than one wife (about 85%), but in forager societies most marriages were monogamous. In 87% of 190 forager societies fewer than 20% of men were married polygynously; among tropical African foragers polygyny runs from 3% to 20%; among Savanna Pume foragers 11% of men and 20% of women are ever married polygynously. Men contributing more food went with more monogamy. Serial pairing was common: among the !Kung and Ache, men and women had children with more than one partner. Over a life, many people had several spouses: Ache women in the pre-contact forest period averaged 12.1 husbands and about 63% of Ache children had a secondary father (ext 1730); perhaps 20% of Hadza stay married to the same person for life (ext 1729). Among the Tsimane of Bolivia, forager-horticulturalists, fewer than 20% of marriages ended in divorce (15 of 76 marriages begun more than 20 years before), most often in the first year, and 5 to 10% of men were married polygynously, almost always to two sisters (ext 5849). Of 25 forager populations in a meta-analysis of reproductive skew, 10 had normative monogamy, 13 tolerated but rare polygyny and 2 normative polygyny (ext 3066). Across 142 societies of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, the rule on how many wives a man may have tracked the share of men and of women actually married polygynously (r .76 and .81, ext 5835). Starkweather and Hames identified 53 societies outside the classical Himalayan and Marquesan area that permit polyandry, one woman with more than one husband, and report that it is most common in egalitarian societies (ext 5833). Genetic studies estimate more breeding women than breeding men: a female to male ratio of 2.1 (San) to 12.5 (Basque) in one study of six populations (ext 5841), and 1.33 (East Asians) to 2.63 (Yoruba) in corrected HapMap estimates (ext 5842, 5843). Hammer and colleagues read the excess as polygyny, formal and effective; Labuda and colleagues read their corrected values as within the range of monogamous or serially monogamous societies, while also overlapping with polygynous ones.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - pume_ever_polygynous: men 11%, women 20%
  - forager_polygyny_practised: 3% to 20% (tropical African foragers)
  - societies_permitting_polygyny: about 85%

**Modern default.** Law imposes one spouse at a time in most states (Japan from 1880, China 1953, India 1955, Nepal 1963). Serial pairing through divorce and new partners continues.

**The gap.** Small gap. Forager practice was already mostly one spouse at a time with some polygyny and remarriage; modern law removes the polygynous minority. Forager marriages often ended: across ten forager and forager-farmer populations reported divorce rates run from 18% (Agta) to nearly every couple (north Alaskan Inuit), and the reviewers put the general level at a quarter to a third of marriages or more, with remarriage common (ext 1728). The rates were counted differently in each population, so they cannot be averaged or set against a modern rate. Where polygyny is practised today: in 56 villages of northern Tanzania, within villages polygyny was not significantly related to food security, height-for-age or weight-for-height (ext 5845); among the Dogon of Mali the hazard of death by age 5 was higher in work-eat groups with more married women per married man in villages 1 to 8, and only in village 9, exceptionally large and wealthy, did polygyny show no adverse effect (ext 5848).

#### Rivals and comparison
`EEA-MATE-07 · Mating & reproduction` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** A forager's rivals for a partner were the same-sex people of the camp and the regional network, a few hundred people seen in ordinary life. No study measures this directly; it follows from the size of the pool (EEA-MATE-02).

**Modern default.** Media and feeds show a stream of the most attractive and successful people alive, edited. #### Sexual stimuli (real people vs unlimited images)
`EEA-MATE-08 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Sexual cues came from real people a forager knew and could meet. No study measures this directly. Among the Aka, the Hewletts found no word for masturbation, and men asked about masturbating before marriage or during the post-partum sex taboo all said it did not occur (Hewlett and Hewlett 2010, ext 3844).

**Modern default.** Pornography is the norm: in a nationally representative sample of US adult internet users, about 70% had viewed it and just over half had in the past year. ### Care & development

#### Alloparental care supply (a village, not a dyad)
`EEA-CARE-01 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Ancestral human infants were not raised by a mother alone but by a network of allomothers - fathers, grandmothers, older siblings, and trusted non-kin - who shared both direct care (holding, carrying, soothing) and provisioning (food). Hrdy argues that humans are a cooperatively breeding ape (ext 22): unlike other African apes, whose mothers nurture infants essentially on their own, hominin mothers relied on groupmates to protect, care for, and provision their unusually slow-maturing, costly young. A human child requires on the order of 13 million calories from birth to independence, and humans breed faster (every 3-4 years) than chimpanzees and orangutans (about every 5.5 and 7.7 years; gorillas breed about every 3.8 years, Galdikas and Wood 1990, ext 3519) while producing larger, slower-maturing babies - a life-history paradox Hrdy argues is solvable only by shared provisioning (ext 401). Across foraging ethnography, infants were handled by people other than the mother from shortly after birth: among the Hadza, the mother does roughly 69% of the time children aged four and under were held (ext 2128) and allomothers the rest; !Kung infants have the mother's entire social world available with intense face-to-face interactions from girls; among the Trobriand Islanders, who are horticulturalists, infants are suckled by lactating allomothers. The infant's evolved expectation is a reliable surround of multiple invested caregivers, not a single isolated dyad.

**Modern default.** Hrdy argues that the idealized breadwinner-father / homemaker-mother / biological-children unit is a historical aberration roughly a century old (Victorian at most; the 1950s template in the US), departing from the extended-kin allomaternal norm that held for the vast majority of human history (ext 403). Care that was distributed across many hands is now concentrated on one or two.

**The gap.** The mismatch is between the reliable, present, multi-caregiver surround the infant's attachment and the mother's reproductive psychology evolved to expect, and the thin one- or two-adult household the modern environment supplies. Hrdy argues that maternal commitment is conditional on perceived alloparental support (M9, ext 402). This is a supply mismatch, not a parental-competence failure.

**Resolution unit.** Reliable, present alloparental supply - care plus provisioning actually delivered by invested caregivers - NOT bare kin count or genealogical presence. Four relatives who live far away and do not share the daily load do not satisfy the parameter; what the infant and mother expect is dependable, hands-on, repeated care from a stable surround.

**Serves mechanisms:** M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting

#### Caregiver continuity and reliability (present, not merely countable)
`EEA-CARE-02 · Care & development` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** Attachment is not a single dyadic bond but secure attachment to one or more trusted caretakers, Bowlby's design refined by Hrdy to plural figures, and human infants are equipped with traits (cuteness, prolonged eye contact, social smiling) for recruiting the attention of potential caretakers. Crucially, maternal commitment itself is contingent: for a cooperatively breeding mammal, the capacity to conceive is only part of the equation; the mother must be able to carry through to rearing independence, which she assesses against perceived alloparental support. Touch is part of this continuity. In the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample the mother slept in the same room as her infant in every one of the 173 societies coded, and in the same bed in at least 76 (ext 705). Field argues that the skin is the first and the largest sense organ to develop prenatally (ext 296). The expectation is not a list of relatives but caregivers who are reliably, physically present across time.

**Modern default.** In the United States of the late 1970s infants spent little time in body contact with caregivers, their movements were restricted by playpens, high chairs or cribs, and separate sleeping was regularly recommended (ext 3145); all Euro-American infants observed in Washington, D.C. had their own crib in their own room (ext 3151), and of the 18 US infants, 15 slept near their mothers until 3 to 6 months of age, when most were moved to a separate room (ext 3435). Caregivers may be nominally present (counted on paper) yet not reliably, responsively available in the moment-to-moment way the regulatory system expects.

**The gap.** The mismatch is between caregiver reliability/continuity as an experienced, present regulatory input and a modern environment that often supplies caregivers who are countable but not dependably present. Because maternal investment and infant attachment are both calibrated to perceived caregiver reliability (M9, M15), low continuity is not neutral: it degrades the secure-base scaffolding and the touch-mediated regulation the infant evolved to expect, even when the number of caregivers on paper looks adequate.

**Resolution unit.** Reliable, present caregiver supply - responsive caregivers actually and repeatedly available across time, including continuity of body contact - NOT a count of designated or genealogically-related caregivers. The naive reading (enough named caregivers exist) misleads; what regulates the infant is dependable presence.

**Serves mechanisms:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting

#### Mixed-age child playgroups as developmental input
`EEA-CARE-03 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Ancestral children developed social competence through play with a mixed-age band of other children - younger and older mixed together - rather than under continuous adult supervision or in age-segregated peer cohorts. Rough-and-tumble play during the juvenile period is a dedicated developmental input: in the mammalian design, play is a distinct emotional system (Panksepp's PLAY) and, in rats, play experience physically tunes the maturing prefrontal cortex (ext 212), refining, as Pellis and Pellis propose (ext 216), the circuits that later dampen the amygdala and support emotional regulation. Critically, the AMOUNT and quality of play partners matters: female rats raised with more play partners show progressively greater play experience and corresponding PFC development, and even the untreated playmates of more-playful partners tended to have greater cortical thickness (ext 220) - play is a partnered input, so a richer multi-partner (and, in the human case, multi-age) play environment is the expected calibrating stimulus.

**Modern default.** Modern childhood compresses or eliminates free mixed-age play: age-segregated grade cohorts and daycare, adult-structured and supervised activities, indoor screen-based leisure, reduced unsupervised outdoor roaming, and safety norms that discourage rough-and-tumble.

**The gap.** The mismatch is between mixed-age, self-organized, physically-active play as the evolved calibrating input for the juvenile prefrontal cortex (M4, embedded in the alloparental child-minding network, M9) and a modern environment that supplies age-segregated, adult-structured substitutes. In rats, play with partners shapes the developing cortex (Pellis & Pellis 2009, ext 212, 220). Cor reads reduced free play in children as a missing developmental input. In children, general risky outdoor play (13 studies) and places that allow risky play (21 studies) went with better physical, mental, social and cognitive outcomes in a review of 74 studies (Ramsden et al. 2026, ext 5097), and children whose parents reported more time playing adventurously before the pandemic had slightly fewer internalising symptoms and more positive affect during the first Covid-19 lockdown (Dodd, Nesbit and FitzGibbon 2023, ext 5104).

**Resolution unit.** Self-directed, mixed-age, physically-active free play actually engaged in - partnered play experience - NOT adult-structured activities, solitary screen play, or scheduled lessons that occupy time without delivering the rough-and-tumble, role-reversal, peer-negotiated input the circuits expect.

**Serves mechanisms:** M4 - Social Calibration / Play, M9 - Care / Alloparenting

#### Multigenerational presence (older people inside the band's giving and getting)
`EEA-CARE-04 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Older people kept a place in the band's giving and getting. They gave food (ext 3088), care (ext 2084) and knowledge (ext 3827) where they could, and they were given to (ext 3088, 3665). The project's position: reciprocity runs across the whole life, so a grandmother who gave for a lifetime holds her place when she takes. What older people gave differed by group. Among the Hadza, grandmothers foraged most when their newest grandchild was youngest (ext 2063). Among the Tsimane, net food flowed down the generations: grandmothers gave a net 117 kcal a day to their grandchildren and grandfathers 223, into their 70s (ext 3088). Among the Aka, care by others, largely grandmothers, lowered mothers' work by up to 216 kcal over nine hours (ext 2084). Tsimane adults named grandparents most often as the source of the stories they knew (ext 3827). Among the Ache and Hiwi, older women did not contribute food that eased the deficits of middle-aged couples raising children, and about 10 percent of middle-aged Ache women lived with their own mothers (ext 2301). Agta adults named old age among the needs that decided whom they gave to in an experimental sharing game (ext 3665). The grandmother hypothesis (and Lieberman's active-grandparent corollary) holds that the long human life after the last birth (among 52 wild mammal species, longer than chance only in humans, orcas and short-finned pilot whales, ext 3722) was selected for and made possible by older adults remaining physically active to help rear children, grandchildren, and other young kin, increasing inclusive fitness through kin-selected provisioning. Older people are thus part of the alloparental supply: a band of several generations in which the old kept a place in the exchange and were no absent generation.

**Modern default.** Among US adults aged 25 or over with a living parent or adult child, 13.2% lived with a parent or adult child, 74.8% had the nearest one within 30 miles and 6.8% had the nearest one 500 miles or more away (ext 4204). In ten European countries 58 percent of grandmothers and 49 percent of grandfathers looked after a grandchild in the past year (Hank and Buber 2009, ext 4209).

**The gap.** The mismatch is between older people who keep a place in the daily giving and getting of a band of several generations (M9, M3) and the modern default of grandparents who live apart from it.

**Resolution unit.** Older relatives present and inside the family's daily giving and getting: giving care, food or knowledge where they can, and being provided for. NOT mere grandparental existence or living-relative count. A surviving grandmother several time zones away does not fill the place a band of several generations gave her.

**Serves mechanisms:** M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting

### Touch & contact

#### Affective (C-tactile) contact frequency and skin-to-skin time
`EEA-TOUCH-01 · Touch & contact` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Ancestral infants lived in near-continuous bodily contact: carried on the body, fed at short intervals, and held skin-to-skin for much of the day (ext 2082), with frequent touch from the mother and a wider circle of caregivers. Field argues that the skin is the first and largest sense organ and that the fetus is already continuously stimulated in utero (ext 296). Skin-to-skin/carried contact was the species-typical default, not an intervention.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - note: No defensible single ancestral 'touches-per-hour' number; cross-cultural touch frequency ranges enormously (0-180+ contacts between two people sharing coffee in cafés in four cities per Jourard, ext 295), so the EEA value is a high-contact regime with a floor, not a point estimate
  - qualitative: True
  - infant_contact: near-continuous (carried, co-sleeping, fed at frequent short intervals)
  - caregiver_scope: mother plus alloparents

**Modern default.** In the United States of the late 1970s infants spent little time in body contact with caregivers and their movements were restricted by playpens, high chairs or cribs (ext 3145), and Euro-American infants were held less than Aka and Ngandu infants and were more often alone (ext 3151); personal-space norms and screen-mediated interaction strip casual affective touch from daily adult life.

Critically, affective touch is the one ancestral channel that remote, volumetric, or screen-based technology cannot deliver: it requires physical skin contact.

**Resolution unit.** Reliable supply of skin-to-skin, affective contact, especially in infancy and under stress, and NOT a raw count of any contact. Two kinds of touch carry it: firm, moderate pressure is what helped in the preterm massage studies (ext 293), and slow, gentle stroking is what C-tactile fibres respond to, firing most at stroking speeds of 1 to 10 cm per second that people rate the most pleasant (ext 2147, 2689).

#### Co-sleeping
`EEA-TOUCH-02 · Touch & contact` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** In every one of the 173 societies coded in the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, the mother slept in the same room as her infant, and in at least 76 of them in the same bed (ext 705); the sample spans foragers, farmers, herders and others, not foragers alone. Nighttime proximity meant the physiological co-regulation channels operated across the 24-hour cycle, not just during waking.

**Modern default.** WEIRD norms favor solitary infant sleep in a separate crib or room.

**The gap.** Solitary infant sleep removes the nighttime proximity of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, where the mother slept in the same room as her infant in every one of the 173 societies coded, and in the same bed in at least 76 (ext 705). It removes the nighttime physiological co-regulation measured in cosleeping pairs, where mother and infant induce arousals in one another within seconds (ext 615). The evidence for this co-regulation comes from one lab (McKenna 2014, ext 615, 616).

**Resolution unit.** Reliable presence of nighttime body contact / proximity that preserves the touch and co-regulation channel through the sleep period; the relevant variable is continuity of contact across the 24-hour cycle, not sleep duration.

**Serves mechanisms:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding, M7 - Circadian Regulation

#### Affective grooming/touch from trusted others (adult social touch)
`EEA-TOUCH-03 · Touch & contact` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** Beyond infancy, ancestral adults maintained social bonds through affiliative body contact: the human homolog of primate grooming. Primate grooming is fundamentally about the intimacy of massage, where skin stimulation triggers endorphin release and underwrites social bonding.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - mechanism: skin stimulation -> endorphin release (bonding) and cortisol regulation (stress buffering)
  - qualitative: True
  - reciprocity: bidirectional: giver and receiver both benefit physiologically
  - adult_social_touch: affiliative body contact / grooming-homolog from trusted others

**Modern default.** Adult-to-adult affiliative touch is sharply curtailed in WEIRD societies by personal-space norms, professionalization of touch, and screen-mediated relationships, and remote communication carries none of it.

**The gap.** In 67 women, a neck and shoulder massage from the partner before a stress test went with a smaller rise in cortisol and heart rate than being alone, and verbal support alone was no better than being alone (ext 429). The benefit is bidirectional (ext 430). This channel is physical-contact-only and cannot be delivered by remote or volumetric technology.

**Resolution unit.** Reliable supply of affiliative touch from trusted others (the grooming homolog), present as an ongoing relational channel; not one-off or transactional touch, and not mere co-presence without contact.

**Serves mechanisms:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding

### Circadian & sensory

#### Dawn/dusk light gradient with genuine nocturnal darkness as circadian entrainment signal
`EEA-CIRC-01 · Circadian & sensory` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Across evolutionary history the human circadian pacemaker (suprachiasmatic nucleus) was entrained by a high-amplitude natural light-dark cycle: bright daytime illumination, a dusk dimming, and then real darkness for the duration of the biological night, with only firelight (low) as evening illumination. In wrist-sensor recordings of Hadza, San and Tsimane adults, no group went to sleep near sunset: sleep began on average 3.3 hours after sunset (ext 2224). The operative signal is the timing and spectral content of light at the day-night transition.

**EEA value** (illustrative, not constants):

  - biological_night: genuine darkness
  - typical_sleep_onset: on average 3.3 hours after sunset; no group (Hadza, San, Tsimane) went to sleep near sunset (ext 2224)
  - evening_illumination: firelight (low lux)
  - reference_populations: ["Hadza", "San"]

#### Sleep timing/phase alignment to the biological night (not a fixed duration target)
`EEA-CIRC-02 · Circadian & sensory` - **tested many times**

### Threat & safety

#### Threat temporality (acute, episodic, escapable-by-action)
`EEA-THREAT-01 · Threat & safety` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** Cacioppo and Patrick write that short, life-or-death confrontations drove the evolution of 'fight or flight' (ext 150). The fight-or-flight architecture was selected to spike hard and briefly, then terminate. Threat exposure was thus pulsatile, not continuous.

**Modern default.** Modern stressors are chronic and non-terminating: the overbearing boss, the long commute, financial precarity, abstract status anxiety, always-on connectivity. The same stressor persists hour after hour, year after year, while the sympathetic/parasympathetic architecture is unchanged. The threat system is engaged at a low chronic duty cycle it was never built to sustain, and the beneficial acute response becomes damaging on the chronic timescale.

**The gap.** The mismatch is in the time-course, not the threat machinery itself. The system is calibrated for brief, resolvable spikes; modern life supplies sustained, unresolved activation. Acute stress is adaptive (even enhances immunity); the identical response sustained chronically suppresses immunity and accumulates wear-and-tear. The gap is the absence of an episode boundary that lets the response shut down.

**Resolution unit.** Calibrate to threat TEMPORALITY (episode duration and duty cycle with a terminating standdown), not threat magnitude or count. A single intense-but-resolved spike is ancestral-normal; a mild-but-unending engagement is the mismatch. The unit is 'does the activation episode end and the system return to baseline,' not 'how scary was it.'

#### Threat controllability and predictability (resolvable by own action)
`EEA-THREAT-02 · Threat & safety` - **argued, not yet tested**

**EEA baseline.** The threat system evolved on the expectation that vigilance would be followed by an effective response that resolves the danger.

**Modern default.** Many modern stressors are structurally uncontrollable and unpredictable: algorithmic management and electronic monitoring strip decision latitude, outcomes hinge on systems no single action can move, and dread is open-ended (an unknown deadline, an unbounded threat). Boehm's ethnography of forager gossip describes a band that effectively keeps a running 'dossier' on every individual - provenance was the substrate of trust.

**Modern default.** The shift Tomasello describes - from face-to-face 'I-you' joint intentionality to impersonal 'we-as-group' collective intentionality - is taken to an extreme by mass and digital media, where signals are detached from any reputation-bearing source the receiver can sanction or even locate.

**The gap.** The evolved machinery for weighing information by source reputation and face-to-face accountability is deprived of the provenance cues it was calibrated to use. Receivers must evaluate high-stakes claims from sources they cannot place in any reputational web, and senders bear little reputational cost for deception - removing the gossip/ostracism feedback loop that ancestrally policed signal honesty.

**Resolution unit.** Proportion of consequential information arriving from a known, reputation-bearing, accountable source the receiver can locate and sanction - NOT raw access to information or number of contacts. A person can be flooded with information yet have almost none of it carry verifiable provenance.

#### Gossip and narrative flow as reputation and norm transmission
`EEA-INFO-02 · Information environment` - **tested many times**

**EEA baseline.** Among the Ju/'hoansi, daytime talk was mostly about economic matters, complaints and gossip, while firelight talk at night was mostly stories (ext 720). Gossip and narrative are talk about who behaved how, who is reliable or stingy, what happened, and what one ought to do. This stream did double duty - it transmitted norms, calibrated each person's reputation in the eyes of the group, and deterred free-riding because everyone knew gossip was constantly underway and anyone could become its subject. Boehm documents gossip, ridicule, and ostracism as enforcement against free-riders (bullies, the lazy, feigners, secret meat-eaters); Henrich notes that norm violations meant 'reputational damage, gossip, and a consequent loss of marriage opportunities and allies.'

**Modern default.** The tight loop in which gossip simultaneously informed, judged, and deterred within a single accountable group is broken: reputational narrative is broadcast without the reciprocal accountability that made it self-policing.

**The gap.** The gossip/narrative channel that ancestrally fused information transmission with reputation tracking and norm enforcement is decoupled. People consume reputational narrative about strangers they cannot sanction, and the norm-deterrence function (knowing the group is watching and will talk) is weakened in anonymous or one-to-many settings - leaving the evolved appetite for reputational narrative engaged but its enforcement payload disabled.

**Resolution unit.** Reputation/norm narrative circulating within a bounded group where subjects and audience are mutually known and can reciprocally sanction - NOT raw volume of social content consumed. Doomscrolling celebrity gossip is high-volume but supplies almost no actionable, reciprocal reputational signal.

**Serves mechanisms:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M5 - Status Monitoring

## About this framework

Human systems signal when their conditions are not met, and modern environments withhold those conditions at scale. Preferences are mechanism outputs, not ground truth. The load-bearing claim of every proxy gradient: the cue is identical at every level; only the resolving function changes, so a proxy can fire the cue without supplying what actually resolves it.
