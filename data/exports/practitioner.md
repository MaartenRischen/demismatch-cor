## How to read this document

This document is a working map of human motivation and distress, written for people who sit across from other people and try to help them. Its central claim will reframe a lot of what you see in your office: most of the suffering your clients bring you is not a defect in them. It is accurate biological signaling coming from systems that are working correctly, but being fed the wrong inputs by a modern environment they were never built for.

Put plainly: the feeling is real, the signal is informative, and the problem is usually upstream of the person. When someone feels anxious, flat, restless, ashamed, or empty, the default reading here is not "this person is broken" but "this system is reporting on its input conditions." That is a diagnostic stance, not a romantic one. It does not mean the signal is always literally true, and it does not mean you should withhold symptom relief where it is indicated. It does mean you check the inputs before you conclude the machinery is faulty.

There are well-defined exceptions where the signal stops being a reliable readout: developmental miscalibration carried forward from early life, chronic dysregulation, acute organic disease or state shifts (an infection or autoimmune disease, an endocrine disease such as a thyroid fault, delirium), structural damage, large-effect genetic variants with measurable physical effects, and substance or medication effects. In those cases the signal can be decoupled from current conditions. The line between them is a clear physical defect, found or not yet found: organic disease or state shift, structural damage, a large-effect genetic variant with measurable physical effects (such as the 22q11.2 deletion) and substance or medication effects are physical causes, and the behaviour is a symptom you treat at its cause. A marker that only shows sensitivity is no defect. Developmental miscalibration, chronic dysregulation, sleep loss and the low-grade inflammation that a mismatched life produces are still responses, set early (DA7) or kept running by coupled systems (DA3), and they are not a disease.

### The derivation stack, briefly

The framework is built in layers, each forced by the one beneath it:

- **Frame:** We never get unmediated access to reality. We get evolved interfaces that were selected to keep our ancestors alive and reproducing, not to tell us the truth. And the default reading of a motivational or emotional output is that it is information about conditions, not noise to suppress.
- **Premise:** The loss function evolution optimized is inclusive fitness, one measure: an individual's own reproductive success plus the reproductive success of relatives, weighted by how closely they are related. Survival counts because the dead do not reproduce. That is selection's loss function, not something the organism computes at runtime. The organism is not a general-purpose learner; it is a bundle of specialized, interacting systems. Modern environments routinely push those systems outside the ranges they expect.
- **Properties:** From this it follows that many painful outputs are intelligible defensive signals, that human regulation is built to run on other people, that systems are coupled and can drag each other down, that proxy cues can switch a system on without ever switching it off, that defensive systems are biased toward false alarms, that fixing one system does not generically fix another, and that the tuning of these systems - gains, thresholds, setpoints - varies from person to person within substantially heritable ranges. That last point is a population statistic, not a verdict on any client: it changes where mismatch bites first, not whether changing inputs works.
- **Consequences:** Chronic unresolved activation accumulates as bodily wear (allostatic load). Markets learn to sell proxies for unmet needs. And because cortical override is metabolically expensive and degrades under chronic load, changing the actual inputs is usually more durable than asking the client to think their way out. The environment is a primary intervention layer - and it is the one most institutions underweight.

This last point is the clinical heart of the document. The default leverage is in changing inputs. For medication Cor applies the clear-defect rule: where a defect is the cause the drug may be treating a disease; where none is, the drug gets replaced by the real thing, such as people who need you and give back, and the dose comes down while the real thing comes in. Where stopping suddenly is biologically dangerous, as with certain drugs and with alcohol, never stop suddenly, and do it with a doctor.

### How to read a proxy gradient

This is the single most useful tool here, so read it carefully.

Each system fires in response to a **cue**. The cue is identical at every level of the gradient. What changes as you go down the ladder is the **resolving function** - whether anything that actually satisfies and terminates the system is being supplied.

A proxy is something that fires the cue without supplying what resolves it. At the top of a gradient (FULL), the cue fires and the need is met. As you descend, the cue keeps firing just as strongly, but less and less real resolution arrives, until at the floor the cue fires and nothing is supplied at all. That is the open loop: the system is switched on, the client feels the pull, and it never switches off.

For each mechanism below you will get: what it looks like in a person, the full proxy gradient in applied terms, the mismatch prediction (what specifically goes wrong when a modern environment feeds it proxies), how well-grounded the claim is (evidence-link count and key researchers), and what changing the inputs - rather than patching the person - would mean.

A note on the ladders: most are simple top-to-bottom ordinal ladders. A few systems behave differently. Some (marked Kind B) are not about a false cue being fired but about a real input being withdrawn or a regulatory off-switch being disabled. Where I use level labels (FULL, PARTIAL, MIXED, MINIMAL, ZERO, and the like), I am using only the examples the framework itself supplies.

## M1 - Threat Management

### What it looks like in a person

This is the freeze-fight-flee system, plus the offensive mode of frustration-aggression. In the room, it shows up as panic, hypervigilance, a racing heart, irritability, a client who is "always on," who cannot relax, who startles, who is exhausted by a danger they cannot name. It is mediated by phylogenetically ancient subcortical circuits - amygdala, periaqueductal gray, hypothalamus - that operate pre-attentively and can override cortical processing. Its core defence centre is about 460 to 535 million years old, shared with lampreys; the amygdala circuit is at least about 340 million years old.

What resolves it is simple and physical: safety established, the threat eliminated or avoided.

### The proxy gradient (resolvability axis)

- **FULL:** acute danger you can fight or flee, then escape. The system fires, you act, you get away, it shuts off. Clean resolution.
- **PARTIAL:** a real present threat resolved by a trusted other. Partly resolved - someone helps you handle it.
- **MIXED:** a chronic slow-burn real threat with no terminating action available. The danger is real but there is nothing to fight and nowhere to flee.
- **MINIMAL:** an abstract or anticipated threat with no physical target. The cue fires, but there is nothing to act on, so nothing resolves.
- **ZERO:** engineered supernormal outrage and threat media. The cue is manufactured and fired at you; there is nothing real to resolve at all. (This floor rung is flagged contestable.)

The cue feels identical at every rung - the same dread, the same activation. Only the availability of resolving action drains away as you descend.

### Mismatch prediction

Modern threats - debt, emails, social media, status anxiety - cannot be fought or fled. The system stays on. This is the core problem: M1 was built for threats that end. Modern threats do not end, so the system never gets the "safe now" signal it is built to wait for.

### Cascade

M1 is powerful. It suppresses pursuit (M2), play (M4), reproductive motivation (M14), movement (M10), agency (M6) and the immune response (M8). When threat is high, exploration, play, sex, exercise, and a sense of agency all go offline - which is exactly what you see in a chronically threatened client. M1 is itself suppressed by M3 (social bonding): a trusted other present can turn the alarm down.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching the person means teaching them to override an ancient alarm with expensive cortical effort - which degrades under chronic load. Changing the inputs means asking what unresolvable threat the system is correctly reporting (financial, occupational, relational, informational) and either making it resolvable or removing it. The PARTIAL rung is a clinical lever in itself: a trusted other can resolve a real threat the person cannot resolve alone.

### Evidence

115 evidence links. Key researchers: Panksepp, LeDoux, Mobbs. This mechanism is **forced** by convergence C10.

## M2 - Pursuit / Exploration

### What it looks like in a person

This is the general-purpose SEEKING system - the drive to explore, pursue goals, build skills, and engage with the world. The same wanting system also runs when what is wanted is a partner; what is specific to partners belongs to M14. In a person it looks like drive and curiosity when healthy, and like restless, compulsive, never-satisfied "wanting" when hijacked - the client who scrolls for hours, chases novelty, feels driven but never full.

The key dissociation: wanting (mesolimbic dopamine) is separable from liking (hedonic satisfaction). A proxy can crank up the wanting while delivering none of the liking that normally closes the loop.

What resolves it: engagement with real challenges that produce tangible outcomes.

### The proxy gradient (cue = SEEKING/wanting, constant throughout)

- **FULL:** engaging a real challenge that yields a real outcome.
- **PARTIAL:** knowledge work with a real but abstracted, delayed outcome.
- **MIXED:** a variable-reward app or feed with intermittent real payoff.
- **MINIMAL:** infinite novelty, content scroll, idle browsing - cue fires, nothing resolves.
- **ZERO:** machine gambling engineered for time-on-device with guaranteed net loss. Cue fires; not only does nothing resolve, the player is systematically drained.

The wanting is identical at the top and the bottom. What drains as you descend is the tangible outcome and the consummatory closure that would say "enough." (The framework notes the MIXED-vs-MINIMAL ordering is its most contestable point.)

### Mismatch prediction

Variable-reward apps fire SEEKING without resolution. Open loop. This is the textbook case: the cue is maximal, the resolving function is zero, and the system runs all day.

### Cascade

M2 is suppressed by threat (M1), bonding (M3), circadian regulation (M7), immune regulation (M8), and energy regulation (M13). It interacts with M4 (play) and M14 (reproduction). Practically: a client whose SEEKING looks dead may be downstream of poor sleep, inflammation, threat, or grief - check those before concluding the drive itself is the problem.

### What changing the inputs means

You cannot satisfy a hijacked SEEKING system with more of the proxy - that is the open loop. You resolve it by supplying real challenges with tangible outcomes (the FULL rung) and reducing exposure to the engineered floor rungs. Patching the person with willpower against an infinite feed is the losing strategy.

### Evidence

67 evidence links. Key researchers: Panksepp, Berridge. This mechanism is **forced** by convergence C6.

## M3 - Social Bonding

### What it looks like in a person

This is attachment and co-regulation, and it now also includes grief (absorbed from the old GRIEF system). It runs in two states: security, which enables the person broadly, and grief, which suppresses broadly. In the room: the securely-bonded client recovers from stress faster and explores more; the grieving or isolated client looks shut down across the board. Attachment is about 165 to 200 million years old, as old as the mammals, with 192 evidence links - the most heavily grounded mechanism in the entire atlas.

What resolves it is reliable human co-regulation through physical presence, organized in nested layers: about 5 intimate co-regulators who provide most of the regulation, about 15 in the sympathy group, about 50 close partners, and about 150 coherent face-to-face relationships, the sizes found among foragers and in modern networks. The critical variable at the innermost layer of about 5 is **reliability, not count**.

### The proxy gradient (ordered by real co-regulatory function, not amount of contact)

- **FULL:** a reliable, physically co-present inner circle.
- **PARTIAL:** a real bonded inner circle held at a distance via remote contact - real bond, attenuated presence.
- **MIXED:** a large mediated network that routes only partly to real co-presence.
- **MINIMAL:** one-sided parasocial attachment to a non-reciprocating figure - cue fires, no reciprocal regulation.
- **ZERO:** an AI companion engineered to simulate a reciprocating bond. Cue fires; nothing co-regulates.

Note the off-gradient case: isolated or solo living is not a rung on this ladder - it is empty-shelf deprivation, the absence of input entirely.

### Mismatch prediction

The nuclear family, isolated living, and mediated contact substituting for physical presence. The system expects a reliable physically-present inner circle and a face-to-face network; modern arrangements thin both.

### Cascade

M3 in its security state is the great enabler - it suppresses threat (M1), and it gates pursuit, play, and movement. In its grief state it suppresses them. It interacts with M14. Recall the survival data behind this: strong social relationships confer roughly a 50% survival advantage (Holt-Lunstad).

### What changing the inputs means

This is the highest-leverage cross-cutting input dimension in the framework, because social context scaffolds regulation of nearly everything else. Patching the person means teaching emotion-regulation skills to someone whose architecture expects those skills to be supplied by other bodies in the room. Changing the inputs means building or restoring a reliable physically-present inner circle - prioritizing reliability over count at the innermost layer.

### Evidence

192 evidence links. Key researchers: Bowlby, Panksepp, Coan, Hrdy. This mechanism is **forced** by convergence C5.

## M4 - Social Calibration / Play

### What it looks like in a person

The play system develops social skills through rough-and-tumble. Tellingly, it is absent in threatened or ill animals - so a client (or child) who does not play is telling you something about their threat and health state. In people, especially in development, this shows up as the capacity to read social rules, take turns, recover from minor conflict, and self-direct in a group.

What resolves it: self-directed, unsupervised, physical play with other children. In forager childhoods this is a mixed-age group.

### The proxy gradient (cue = the PLAY/SEEKING social-joy pull; ordered by surviving embodied reciprocity with a real partner)

- **FULL:** self-directed, unsupervised physical play with other children, mixed-age as in forager childhoods.
- **PARTIAL:** adult-structured supervised physical activity, organized sports - real bodies, but adult-directed.
- **MIXED:** screen-mediated multiplayer play with real peers - real partners, mediated bodies.
- **MINIMAL:** solitary single-player screen play, no reciprocating partner - cue fires, no real reciprocity.
- **ZERO:** an engineered companion-AI or supernormal variable-reward "play" product. (Architecture-derived.) Cue fires; no identity-bearing partner at all.

(The framework flags the MIXED-over-MINIMAL ranking - screen play with real peers above solitary scripted games - as its most contestable point.)

### Mismatch prediction

Structured activities replacing free play. Screens replacing bodies. Play deprivation. The system needs self-directed embodied reciprocity, and modern childhood substitutes adult structure and screens.

### Cascade

M4 is suppressed by threat (M1) and bonding's grief state (M3) - consistent with its absence in threatened animals - and it interacts with pursuit (M2). Social play is at least about 60 million years old (every primate plays); play in reptiles and fish arose separately.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means trying to teach social skills didactically to someone the system expected to learn them through unsupervised peer play. Changing the inputs means restoring access to self-directed, unsupervised physical play with other children, older and younger included - and, given the cascade, reducing the threat load that switches play off in the first place.

### Evidence

55 evidence links. Key researchers: Panksepp, Pellis & Pellis, Gray. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C2, C4, and C12.

## M5 - Status Monitoring

### What it looks like in a person

Rank regulation: shame, pride, submission, defeat, and continuous rank vigilance. This is not optional and it does not turn off - it runs continuously in the background. In the room it presents as chronic comparison, shame spirals, defeat states, and the felt sense of always coming up short. Importantly, the framework distinguishes **earned prestige** (preferred) from **coerced dominance** (Henrich & Gil-White).

What resolves it: an acceptable position within a reference group of about 150, ideally through earned prestige rather than coerced dominance.

### The proxy gradient (cue = constant comparison/ranking signal; ordered by real standing supplied in a real, knowable group)

- **FULL:** earned prestige in a small face-to-face group.
- **PARTIAL:** a coerced or unstable dominance rank in a real group. Real standing, lower quality.
- **MIXED:** status-striving in a large impersonal society.
- **MINIMAL:** global mediated comparison by follower and like counts. (Architecture-derived.) Cue fires, no real resolvable standing.
- **ZERO:** purchased status signals, luxury-brand display. (Architecture-derived.) Cue fires; nothing real is earned.

(The floor ordering - purchased signals vs social-media comparison - is flagged as the most contestable point.)

### Mismatch prediction

Global comparison via social media. Millions of "peers." Status-defeat cascade. The system was built to track standing in a group of about 150; it now reads itself against millions, and a person can lose every comparison.

### Cascade

No recorded suppression in or out; it interacts with M14 (reproduction). What matters clinically is the link to physiology: this mechanism is grounded partly in allostatic-load and primate-stress work (Sapolsky's link between hierarchy type and cortisol), so chronic status defeat is a bodily-wear pathway, not just a mood.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means trying to talk a client out of comparisons that a continuously-running, non-optional system keeps generating. Changing the inputs means relocating their status competition into a real, knowable, small group where earned prestige is achievable - and reducing exposure to global mediated comparison that the system cannot win.

### Evidence

88 evidence links. Key researchers: Gilbert, Price, Mazur, Sapolsky, Marmot. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C2, C7, C9, and C13.

## M6 - Controllability / Agency

### What it looks like in a person

This is the vmPFC-DRN controllability circuit. The crucial clinical fact: **learned helplessness is the default; agency is the override.** Passivity is the baseline state the brain falls back to; the sense that your actions matter has to be actively established and maintained. This makes M6 critical for breaking cascades - it is often the system you work through to get the others moving. In a person it looks like passivity, helplessness, "why bother," or, healthily, a felt sense that effort registers.

What resolves it: genuine action-outcome feedback and real decision latitude.

### The proxy gradient (cue = detection of response-outcome contingency)

- **FULL:** decision latitude over consequential outcomes that visibly feed back.
- **PARTIAL:** high demand with reduced but still real decision latitude.
- **MIXED:** abstract, surveilled, or screen-mediated work where effort does not visibly register.
- **MINIMAL:** a chronically uncontrollable real environment producing learned helplessness. The cue fires, no real contingency is supplied.
- **ZERO:** an engineered simulated-agency loop - a slot machine, game, or dashboard - firing the contingency cue with zero real consequence. (Architecture-derived.)

This is a dual-axis gradient: from PARTIAL down to MINIMAL, real contingency is progressively withdrawn; ZERO substitutes a false one. (The MINIMAL-vs-ZERO ordering is the contestable point.)

### Mismatch prediction

Abstract work where effort does not visibly register. Rigid hierarchies. Bureaucratic systems. The system needs to see that what it does changes outcomes; modern work often hides that feedback.

### Cascade

M6 is suppressed by threat (M1). Because helplessness is the default and agency the override, and because chronic defensive activation degrades the cortical override machinery, this system is fragile under load - and restoring it is often the wedge that lets other cascades reverse.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means cognitively reframing helplessness in a client whose actual environment supplies no contingency - asking them to feel agentic in a world where their effort genuinely does not register. Changing the inputs means restoring real decision latitude and visible action-outcome feedback. Beware the ZERO rung: simulated-agency loops (games, gamified dashboards) can fire the contingency cue and feel satisfying while supplying zero real consequence.

### Evidence

38 evidence links. Key researchers: Maier & Seligman. No convergence is linked to this mechanism.

## M7 - Circadian Regulation

### What it looks like in a person

The SCN master clock gates metabolism, immunity, cognition, and mood. Its clock genes are more than 500 million years old, shared by insects and vertebrates; cyanobacteria have older daily clocks, but those evolved separately. The key clinical insight: the modern mismatch is usually about **timing and quality, not duration**. A client may be "getting eight hours" and still be circadian-disrupted. Presentations include mood disturbance, cognitive fog, metabolic and immune problems - all downstream of a mis-set clock.

What resolves it: dark-cued sleep onset, bright daylight exposure, and a consistent rhythm.

### The proxy gradient (Kind B - the entraining light signal itself degrades and is finally inverted)

- **FULL:** an outdoor light-dark cycle with dark-cued sleep. (Resolution met.)
- **ATTENUATED:** indoor daytime light with a mostly-dark, dimly-lit night. (Resolution still met.)
- **PARTIAL:** a correctly-phased clock overridden by a misaligned social schedule - social jetlag.
- **MINIMAL:** shift work and always-on connectivity, with the dark cue largely gone. Cue degraded, little resolution.
- **DEFEATED:** evening blue-light screens actively mis-setting the clock - false daylight at biological night.

This is a Kind B gradient: it is not a false cue being fired but the real entraining signal being degraded and finally inverted. (Contestable points: the social-jetlag-vs-shift-work ordering, and whether screen signal-inversion is truly the floor.)

### Mismatch prediction

Electric light, blue screens, shift work, caffeine. These do not just shorten sleep; they corrupt the timing signal the whole organism synchronizes to.

### Cascade

Functionally this clock gates metabolism, immunity, cognition, and mood, so its disruption shows up everywhere. Always screen circadian inputs in a client with diffuse, cross-domain complaints.

### What changing the inputs means

This is a near-pure inputs problem. Patching means treating the downstream mood or cognitive symptom while the clock stays mis-set. Changing the inputs means restoring the light-dark signal: bright daylight, dark-cued sleep onset, consistent timing, and removing the false daylight of evening screens.

### Evidence

20 evidence links. Key researchers: Walker, Roenneberg, Czeisler. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C3, C9, and C12.

## M8 - Immune Regulation

### What it looks like in a person

This is the cytokine signaling system that produces sickness behavior - and crucially, **it overrides voluntary intention.** It is also the hub connecting inflammation to low mood via cross-system cascade. In the room: fatigue, withdrawal, low mood, anhedonia, "I just can't" - the same profile as depression, because inflammation produces it. A client cannot willpower their way out of cytokine-driven sickness behavior.

What resolves it: pathogen cleared, inflammation resolved.

### The proxy gradient (Kind B - the trigger drifts from a clearable pathogen toward psychosocial/lifestyle signals with nothing to clear, and the off-switch degrades)

- **FULL:** acute infection or injury, fought off and fully resolved.
- **ATTENUATED:** recurrent acute stress that resolves each time but with incomplete recovery.
- **PARTIAL:** sustained lifestyle drivers - sedentarism, poor diet, sleep loss - keeping inflammation simmering.
- **MINIMAL:** chronic social isolation and status stress defeating the inflammatory off-switch. Cue fires, nothing to clear.
- **DEFEATED:** self-sustaining chronic low-grade inflammation, allostatic-load collapse.

(The lifestyle-drivers-over-social-isolation ordering is contestable and could legitimately swap or collapse.)

### Mismatch prediction

Chronic low-grade inflammation from diet, sedentarism, and social isolation. The system was built to fire at a pathogen and shut off when it cleared; modern drivers keep it firing with nothing to clear.

### Cascade

This is described as the **hub** connecting inflammation to low mood across the wider system. When you see a low-mood picture, inflammation is a candidate input - and the relevant inputs (diet, movement, sleep, social connection) sit in M13, M10, M7, and M3.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means treating the resulting low mood as a primary mood disorder while the inflammatory drivers continue. Changing the inputs means addressing the lifestyle and social signals - diet, sedentarism, sleep, isolation, status stress - that keep the off-switch from engaging. Note that this is partly reversible by environmental correction.

### Evidence

37 evidence links. Key researchers: Dantzer, Danese, Irwin. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C3, C7, and C9.

## M9 - Care / Alloparenting

### What it looks like in a person

The parental and alloparental care system, running on oxytocin and prolactin circuits. The defining claim: **the organism expects a village, not solo parenting.** In the room this is the burned-out parent - the client who loves their child and is nonetheless depleted past function, often blaming themselves. The framework reframes that: the architecture expected distributed care that is not being supplied.

What resolves it: a distributed care network rather than solo or dyadic parenting. Traditional-society data typically shows multiple non-parent caregivers (about 4 or more). The critical resolving variable is the presence and continuity of **real alloparental supply** - care plus provisioning - not bare kinship.

### Two separate scales

This mechanism has two distinct axes. They must be read separately, never merged.

**Axis A - real alloparental supply** (a deprivation axis, ordered by caregiver count times continuity, not kinship):

- **FULL:** a distributed network of about 4 or more kin and community caregivers.
- **REDUCED:** multiple reliable caregivers, including high-quality paid care. (Resolution still met.)
- **THIN:** a single intermittent helper, or high-turnover institutional care. (Partly resolved.)
- **EMPTY SHELF:** a solo or dyadic parent with no network. This is the deprivation floor - an absence of input, not a proxy.

**Axis B - fake-fix proxy** (real alloparental function is essentially zero, ordered by how completely the caregiver-other is counterfeited):

- **ZERO:** a screen or app babysitter, escalating to an AI or robot "caregiver." (Architecture-derived.) The cue of "a caregiver is present" fires; no real alloparental function is supplied.

### Mismatch prediction

The nuclear family without an alloparental network. Parent burnout cascade. This is the empty-shelf case at scale: the input the system requires has simply been removed from the modern environment.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means treating parental burnout as an individual stress-management or resilience deficit. Changing the inputs means building real, continuous alloparental supply - care and provisioning from multiple reliable caregivers, including high-quality paid care, which sits on the resolution side of Axis A. The thing to avoid is mistaking the Axis B counterfeit (a screen, an app, an AI "caregiver") for a fix; it fires the cue and supplies no care.

### Evidence

64 evidence links. Key researchers: Hrdy, Panksepp, Kenkel. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C1, C4, C5, and C12.

## M10 - Movement

### What it looks like in a person

Physical activity functions as a zeitgeber, an anti-inflammatory, and a mood regulator. The reframe: **movement isn't just exercise - it activates repair mechanisms throughout the organism.** In a person, deficit shows up as low mood, poor sleep timing, inflammation, and dysregulation that no amount of "exercise" fully fixes - because the system expects all-day movement, not a session.

What resolves it: movement woven into daily life rather than set aside as "exercise." Hadza men walk about 11 km a day and women about 6 km, as a side effect of foraging.

### The proxy gradient (Kind B - the real input is withdrawn, not a false cue fired; ordered by how much of the all-day muscle-activity distribution is supplied)

- **FULL:** all-day incidental movement of foraging life - locomotion plus active rest postures.
- **ATTENUATED:** a physically active job - farming, manual labor, a walking-heavy trade. (Resolution still met.)
- **PARTIAL:** a mostly sedentary day broken up by walking, standing, and fidgeting - incidental NEAT.
- **MINIMAL:** a sedentary day plus a discrete gym or exercise session - a partial re-supply, not a false cue.
- **DEFEATED:** chair-bound sedentarism with essentially no movement.

(The contestable point: the gym is ranked below all-day incidental NEAT here because the axis is the movement distribution, not fitness; on a fitness or mortality axis it would swap.)

### Mismatch prediction

Sedentarism, with gym sessions as partial compensation. The framework is explicit that a workout is a partial re-supply of a withdrawn input - useful, but not the same as the all-day distribution the system expects.

### Cascade

M10 is suppressed by threat (M1) and bonding's grief state (M3) - so a sedentary, withdrawn client may be downstream of threat or grief, not "unmotivated."

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means prescribing "exercise" as a standalone behavior. Changing the inputs means restoring incidental, all-day movement to how the person lives - and recognizing that a discrete gym session, while genuinely beneficial, is a MINIMAL-rung partial re-supply, not full resolution.

### Evidence

29 evidence links. Key researchers: Pontzer, Lieberman, Ratey. This mechanism is grounded in convergence C3.

## M11 - Cooperation / Alliance

### What it looks like in a person

Reciprocity, cheater detection, norm enforcement, and coalitional identity, combined into one system. Convergence C11 forces M11. Three bodies of tests support it (Fehr & Gächter, Boehm, Tomasello), with Trivers 1971 as the theory behind them. In a person, deficit shows up as the corrosive sense of dealing with strangers who can defect without consequence, of belonging to no team, of effort and fairness going untracked and unrewarded.

What resolves it: a trusted reciprocal network with norm enforcement, within a group small enough for direct reputation tracking - about 150, the size found among foragers and in modern networks. Norm enforcement requires repeated direct interaction where defectors can be identified, remembered, and sanctioned. Beyond about 150, cooperation requires institutional scaffolding - formal rules, third-party enforcement, shared fictions - rather than direct reciprocity.

### The proxy gradient (cue = constant alliance/reciprocity machinery)

- **FULL:** a trusted reciprocal network under about 150, with direct reputation tracking and norm enforcement.
- **PARTIAL:** institutionally-scaffolded cooperation beyond about 150 - formal rules, third-party enforcement, shared fictions. This is resolution-via-prosthesis: institutions re-supply the same function at scale.
- **MIXED:** market exchange among strangers backed by gameable reputation systems. Proxy failure begins here.
- **MINIMAL:** coalition-signaling and parasocial alliance via symbolic in-group markers. Cue fires, no real reciprocal function reaches a real counterparty.
- **ZERO:** anonymous one-shot transactions and AI "allies" with no reputation and no shared narrative. (Architecture-derived.)

Note the unusual structure: FULL to PARTIAL is genuine resolution (institutions are a working prosthesis), so proxy failure does not begin until MIXED. (The PARTIAL/MIXED boundary - exactly where proxy failure starts - is the contestable point.)

### Mismatch prediction

Anonymous transactions, ghosting, no shared narrative. The system needs reputation that sticks and norms that are enforced; modern interaction strips both.

### Cascade

Reciprocity is at least about 25 to 34 million years old, shared with Old World monkeys; human norm enforcement arose within the last 10 million years.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means treating relational cynicism or alienation as a cognitive distortion. Changing the inputs means embedding the person in a trusted reciprocal network under about 150 where reputation is tracked and defection is sanctioned - or, where scale demands it, ensuring real institutional scaffolding rather than the gameable or anonymous floor rungs.

73 evidence links. Key researchers: Trivers, Boehm, Fehr & Gächter, Tomasello. This mechanism is **forced** by convergence C11.

## M12 - Contamination Avoidance

### What it looks like in a person

Three kinds of disgust - pathogen, sexual, and moral - functioning as a behavioral immune system; how the moral kind began is left open. It carries the lowest evidence count in the atlas (12 links). In a person, the pathogen domain shows up as contamination fear and avoidance; the moral domain shows up as purity-coded social aversion.

What resolves it: clean food, a clean social environment, and moral disgust contained.

### The proxy gradient (a 3-rung pathogen-disgust gradient)

- **FULL:** clean food and a clean social environment, with disgust firing on real pathogen cues.
- **PARTIAL:** vulnerability-calibrated over-firing - for example in pregnancy or immunosuppression. Partly resolved; the system raises its threshold appropriately.
- **MIXED:** behavioral-immune smoke-detector false positives on harmless people. This is the floor.

### Moral disgust: no rungs

Moral disgust, the third kind, is **not a rung on the ladder**: purity-coded social aversion plus weaponized moral disgust and purity spirals. How the moral kind began is left open. Tybur and colleagues treat moral disgust as a separate adaptation that shares mainly its facial and vocal expression with pathogen disgust, and a meta-analysis of 50 studies finds that incidental disgust does not amplify moral judgment once publication bias is corrected.

### Mismatch prediction

Moral disgust weaponized in politics, purity spirals. Again - this prediction sits on the demoted, weakest-evidence branch.

### What changing the inputs means

For the well-supported pathogen domain, patching means treating contamination over-firing as a standalone obsessive symptom while ignoring that the system is a false-positive-biased smoke detector by design. Changing the inputs means clean food and a clean social environment, and recognizing the smoke-detector false positives on harmless people for what they are. For the moral domain, the framework's caution is itself the clinical guidance: the evidence is thin.

### Evidence

12 evidence links. Key researchers: Rozin, Curtis, Schaller. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C2 and C8.

## M13 - Energy Regulation

### What it looks like in a person

Hunger, satiety, and constrained total energy expenditure, framed through Pontzer's constrained energy model - the insight that energy is **redistributed, not simply burned.** In a person, the visible problem is intake that does not stop when it should: the client who eats and is not satisfied, who craves engineered food, whose satiety signal seems broken. The framework's claim is that it is not broken - it is being defeated.

What resolves it: effortful, diverse, seasonal whole foods, and metabolic variety.

### The proxy gradient (Kind B - the satiety/intake-termination signal is progressively disabled then actively defeated; scale = food-processing intensity)

- **FULL:** effortful, diverse, seasonal whole foods.
- **ATTENUATED:** minimally processed traditional whole foods. (Resolution still met.)
- **PARTIAL:** conventionally processed, energy-dense food.
- **MINIMAL:** energy-dense palatable food optimized for crave. Cue fires, satiety begins to fail.
- **DEFEATED:** ultra-processed bliss-point food, an engineered supernormal stimulus. The off-switch is actively defeated.

The middle three rungs are architecture-derived. (The contestable point: whether the ATTENUATED/PARTIAL/MINIMAL middle is three real tiers or just a bimodal whole-versus-ultra-processed split.)

### Mismatch prediction

Ultra-processed foods defeating satiety signals. This is the engineered supernormal stimulus case: the food is built to fire the crave cue while disabling the signal that would say "stop."

### Cascade

Recall that M13 is among the systems whose state can suppress pursuit (M2), and inflammation (M8) is fed by poor diet, so energy regulation is wired into the wider load picture.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means asking a client to out-discipline a food specifically engineered to defeat their satiety signal - a losing position, because the regulatory off-switch is the thing being attacked. Changing the inputs means shifting the food environment toward effortful, diverse, seasonal whole foods, where the termination signal can actually function.

### Evidence

34 evidence links. Key researchers: Pontzer, Keys. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C3, C6, and C9.

## M14 - Reproductive Motivation

### What it looks like in a person

Built on Fisher's three-system model - desire, attraction, and pair bonding - which can each operate independently of one another. This system runs partly on the HPG axis, not only on brain circuits. The framework notes it shapes far more of human behavior than reproduction itself. In a person, deficit and hijack show up as compulsive use of sexual or romantic proxies alongside an absence of real pair-bonded fulfillment.

What resolves it: sexual fulfillment within a pair bond, plus pair-bond stability, plus reproductive success.

### The proxy gradient

- **FULL:** a real partner.
- **PARTIAL:** an attenuated real relationship, e.g. a long-distance relationship. Real bond, reduced presence.
- **MIXED:** a partial proxy - a dating app that leads to a real meeting.
- **MINIMAL:** a pure soft proxy - romance fiction, parasocial attachment. Cue fires, no real resolution.
- **ZERO:** a pure hard proxy - pornography, AI sexual products. Cue fires; nothing in the resolution conditions is supplied.

### Mismatch prediction

Pornography, dating apps, delayed reproduction, and social-media mate-value comparison. These fire desire, attraction, or bonding cues without supplying fulfillment within a stable pair bond.

### Cascade

M14 is suppressed by threat (M1) and care (M9). It interacts with bonding (M3), status (M5), and pursuit (M2). Clinically: high threat or active caregiving load can each suppress reproductive motivation - so low desire may be a downstream signal, not a primary problem.

### What changing the inputs means

Patching means treating compulsive proxy use as a standalone behavioral addiction in isolation. Changing the inputs means addressing what is suppressing or failing to resolve the system - threat, the absence of a stable pair bond - and recognizing that the hard proxies at the floor fire the cue maximally while supplying none of the pair-bond stability and fulfillment that resolve it.

### Evidence

58 evidence links. Key researchers: Panksepp, Fisher, Buss, Trivers, Hrdy. This mechanism is **forced** by convergence C14.

## M15 - Touch

### What it looks like in a person

M15 is touch, an input to social bonding (M3), carried by C-tactile afferents. The framework flags it as contested - the premise that its cue can be separated from its function is itself disputed. In a person, the deficit is touch starvation contributing to the dysregulation you would otherwise attribute purely to M3.

What resolves it: regular physical contact with trusted others.

### The proxy gradient (contested; ordered by remaining relational/bond function)

- **FULL:** affectionate touch from a trusted, reciprocating other.
- **PARTIAL:** affective touch from a non-intimate caregiver, or massage.
- **MIXED:** incidental, non-affective everyday contact.
- **MINIMAL:** a weighted or deep-pressure substitute, no living other. (Speculative/future-conditional.)
- **ZERO:** synthetic, haptic, or AI C-tactile stimulation, with zero relational embedding. (Speculative/future-conditional.)

Note this gradient is not strictly monotonic: MINIMAL and ZERO both have zero relational function and are split only by a secondary cue-magnitude axis. The two floor rungs are explicitly speculative.

### Mismatch prediction

Touch-starved modern environments. The most striking evidence here is the kangaroo-care finding: a 51% reduction in neonatal mortality for infants under 2000 g given skin contact, in pooled randomised trials - a vivid illustration that physical touch is a load-bearing input, not a luxury.

### What changing the inputs means

Because M15 feeds M3, patching the person is largely beside the point - this is an input channel. Changing the inputs means restoring regular affectionate physical contact with trusted, reciprocating others. The speculative floor rungs (weighted substitutes, synthetic haptics) supply some cue magnitude but, by definition, zero relational embedding - so they cannot substitute for the bonded contact at the top of the ladder, and the framework holds them with appropriate caution.

### Evidence

18 evidence links. Key researchers: McGlone, Field. This mechanism is grounded in convergences C4 and C5.

### Social structure

#### Social network layers (~5/15/50/150)
`EEA-SOC-01 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** The ancestral social world was structured as a series of hierarchically inclusive, nested relationship layers, each roughly three times the size of the one inside it: an innermost support clique of ~5 close confidants who provided emotional and physical support, a sympathy group of ~15 (contacted at least monthly), an affinity/band layer of ~50 (the typical overnight foraging camp), and an active personal network of ~150 (the outer ceiling of stable, individually-known relationships). Each layer carried a qualitatively different grade of intimacy and contact frequency, with the heaviest investment concentrated in the inner ~5-15. These sizes are found among foragers: across 339 forager societies, aggregated residential groups averaged 54 people and periodic multi-group gatherings 165 (ext 1048). Measured residential camps are often smaller: the median residential group across 263 forager societies was 27 people (ext 754), and Hadza camps center near 30 individuals (ext 603). They are found again in modern networks (modern side below). This was the full architecture within which status was monitored and alliances tracked: a personally-known reference group bounded at ~150, not an anonymous mass. Human social groups cluster at characteristic values of approximately 5, 12, 35, 150, 500, and 2,000 (ext 490). Forager populations were organized in nested levels, with each level about three to four times larger than the one below it, from families through camps to regional groups (ext 685).

**Now (modern default):** The modern environment delivers a radically inverted network shape: nominal friend counts that for a small minority run to many hundreds or thousands (ext 4624), while the layers that actually carry support and intimacy stay under the same ceiling. Online networks reproduce the same ~5/15 inner structure when genuine ties are measured (support clique ~4, sympathy group ~14, not significantly different from offline values), and mean platform friend counts hover near ~150-190 (across all 721 million Facebook users of May 2011 the mean was about 190 and the median 99; Ugander et al. 2011, ext 4624), but the bulk of nominal connections are not considered close friends (only ~27.6% of Facebook friends are considered 'genuine' (i.e. close) friends, ext 508). Dunbar proposes that face-to-face meetings are needed from time to time to keep friendships from sliding down the network layers until they become mere acquaintances, and that social media may slow this decay and cannot replace in-person contact (ext 509). The result is a wide, shallow periphery of weak or fictive ties layered on top of a core that the larger network does not enlarge: among 117 adults, more time on social media went with more online friends and with no larger or emotionally closer offline network (Pollet, Roberts and Dunbar 2011).

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is between the number of ties the modern environment presents (a mean of about 190 Facebook friends, and many hundreds or thousands for a small minority, ext 4624) and the number of people a person actually knows and invests in, found among foragers and in modern networks (~150, with real support concentrated in the inner ~5-15). People with large networks had on average weaker ties, measured as time spent calling (ext 4349). Status monitoring (M5) and alliance/reputation tracking (M11), evolved for a ~150 personally-known reference group, are now flooded by reference groups, while the trust-bearing inner layers remain capped and are under-fed when face-to-face contact is displaced.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding, M5 - Status Monitoring  ·  evidence links: 27

#### Lifetime known-individual pool (~1,000)
`EEA-SOC-02 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** Across a foraging lifetime an individual accumulated a bounded pool of personally known others. Estimates put this cumulative lifetime roster at roughly 1,000+ distinct individuals (Hill et al. 2014, Hadza/Ache), much greater than the predicted 150 significant social interactants (Dunbar's number) that was extrapolated from primate brain by social group size regressions. This pool was not a single contemporaneous group: it accreted over decades through camp reshuffling, regional fission-fusion, and durable cross-camp partnerships (e.g. Ju/'hoansi hxaro). The active network of about 150 people (EEA-SOC-01) was the layer within this lifetime pool that a person kept up with at any one time.

**Now (modern default):** Facebook friend counts run to many hundreds or thousands for a small minority of users (ext 4624), and only ~27.6% of Facebook friends register as genuine close friends (ext 508).

**The gap to work with:** EEA delivered a finite, slowly-accreting lifetime roster (~1,000+).

#### Band / camp size (a few dozen people)
`EEA-SOC-03 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral humans lived in small, residentially co-present foraging bands (camps): a single overnight camp typically held on the order of a few dozen people - a median residential group of 27 people across 263 forager societies (ext 754), and about 30 individuals in Hadza camps (ext 603) - a scale that sits within the Dunbar 'band' layer (~35) and 'overnight camp' layer (~50) of the nested social network. At this scale every co-resident is a personally known, named individual whose behaviour can be directly observed and tracked over time.

**Now (modern default):** Modern humans do not live in a single bounded, personally-known residential band. Daily life is partitioned across many large, partly anonymous groupings - workplaces, apartment buildings, transit systems, schools, and online networks. The small, stable, fully-known co-resident group of a few dozen people has no direct modern equivalent; its functions are split across institutions.

**The gap to work with:** The ancestral social field was a small set of a few dozen personally-known people sharing a camp, sitting within the band/overnight-camp Dunbar layer. The modern default replaces this with chronic immersion in groupings far larger than the band scale. The delta is a mismatch between the scale at which our cooperation, status-monitoring, and bonding machinery was calibrated (a bounded, fully-known band/camp of a few dozen people) and the scale we actually inhabit (large, partly anonymous aggregations plus a thin set of disposable ties), so the band-sized layer that once carried daily social life is no longer delivered by any single modern structure.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding  ·  evidence links: 10

#### Within-camp kin fraction (low; campmates mostly unrelated)
`EEA-SOC-04 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** A person's residential camp/band was NOT a clan of close relatives. Across forager societies the typical residential group (~28 adults) was composed mostly of individuals to whom the focal person was not closely related: primary/first-order kin (parents, siblings, offspring) made up only a small minority of co-residents (under 10% of a residential camp; Apicella et al. 2012, ext 601), and most campmates were unrelated or only distantly related. An agent-based model suggests that, even if all individuals seek to live with as many kin as possible, within-camp relatedness is reduced if men and women have equal influence in selecting camp members (Dyble et al. 2015, ext 687).

**Now (modern default):** Modern social environments invert the ancestral pattern in two opposite directions at once. (1) The family one is related to is, for about two in three US adults aged 25 or over with a living parent or adult child, not all within 30 miles (three in four have one within 30 miles, about a third have all of them that near, and 13 percent share a home with one; Choi et al. 2020, ext 4204). (2) The larger groups one spends daily life inside (workplace, neighborhood, online networks, institutions) are NOT self-assembled through mutual choice, reciprocity, and reputation tracking; membership is assigned by employment, address, or platform. The result is that 'who you live and work among' is no longer the output of a relationship-selection process; it is administratively or commercially determined.

**The gap to work with:** The ancestral signal was: 'the group around me is mostly unrelated people I (or my band) actively chose and continuously vetted through reciprocity and reputation; kinship is not what holds us together, the relationships are.' The modern signal is degraded on the selection axis: the unrelated people around us are still mostly unrelated (that part matches), but they are assigned rather than chosen, and the reciprocity/reputation machinery that turned unrelated campmates into durable trusted allies is largely absent or shallow. Mechanisms M3 (bonding) and M11 (cooperation/alliance) evolved expecting that low kin-relatedness would be compensated by self-selected, reciprocal, reputation-anchored dyads. Modernity delivers the low relatedness WITHOUT the compensating chosen-and-vetted dyadic structure, so the mechanisms fire in a world of unrelated-but-also-unbonded co-residents, which is a key part of why being surrounded by people can still register as social isolation.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding  ·  evidence links: 6

#### Membership turnover (camp dissolves and reconstitutes; most campmates change between survey waves)
`EEA-SOC-05 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** The residential group (camp/band) was a fission-fusion structure that continuously dissolved and reconstituted rather than a fixed-membership unit. Among the Hadza, entire camps relocated roughly every 4-8 weeks and individuals reshuffled across camps such that only a minority of campmates carried over from one survey wave to the next (Smith et al. 2018, Hadza waves 1 to 3 years apart; the exact proportion is unverified, see eea_value). Crucially, despite this high residential churn, assortment on cooperation persisted (cooperators clustered together across reshuffles). Group composition was fluid.

**Now (modern default):** Modern social environments invert the structure: the GROUP labels are stable and durable (a workplace, a school cohort, a neighborhood, a social-media platform or feed) while the actual dyadic relationships within them are shallow, interchangeable, and disposable. People remain nominal members of named, persistent groups, yet cycle through co-members with little individual reciprocal investment, and there is no institution analogous to a delayed-reciprocity partnership (e.g. hxaro) that conserves a specific bond once physical proximity ends. In a Dutch panel followed over seven years, a lack of opportunities to meet (in contexts such as work, family, sports clubs, voluntary associations and the neighbourhood) was an important reason why many relationships ended (Mollenhorst, Volker and Flap 2014, ext 2649).

**The gap to work with:** The ancestral and modern environments are inverted on which layer is stable. Ancestrally the GROUP churned (most campmates changed between survey waves). Modernity delivers the opposite: stable, durable group labels (workplace, cohort, feed) paired with shallow, disposable dyads and no mechanism (no hxaro-equivalent) for conserving a specific bond once proximity ends. The mismatch lies in where turnover lands: on the relationships, which evolved to persist, instead of on group membership, which evolved to be fluid. That leaves the co-regulating, reputation-tracking reciprocal dyad chronically unmet.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** The durable reciprocal dyad - a specific, individually-known partner with whom reciprocity, co-regulation, and reputation are tracked over years. What needs conserving across membership turnover is not the group or the amount of contact but the persistence of particular bonded relationships; the bond, not the band, is the conserved unit.

#### Selection rule / "shuffle algorithm" (choice-driven; homophily + cooperation + reciprocity)
`EEA-SOC-06 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** Who you lived alongside was produced by an ongoing, choice-driven assortment process operating across frequent residential reshuffling. In Hadza camps this "algorithm" had recurring structure: (1) homophily - in both the campmate and gift networks, social ties were more likely between people similar in age, height, weight, body fat, and handgrip strength; (2) strong reciprocity - in the campmate network an ego was roughly 37-51x (point ~44x) more likely to name an alter who named them; (3) cooperator assortment - cooperators clustered with cooperators, an association extending out to two degrees of separation in gift networks; plus transitivity and geographic decay. The output was a camp continually re-composed of preferred, reciprocating, similarly-cooperative partners.

**Now (modern default):** Where modern matching exists (dating apps, professional networks, recommender feeds), the optimization target is engagement, novelty, or proximity-of-the-moment, not durable mutual reciprocity, demonstrated cooperativeness, or transitive trust within a personally-known group.

**The gap to work with:** The EEA delivered a continuous, agency-rich sorting process that filtered one's immediate social world toward reciprocating, cooperative, vouched-for partners. The modern environment inverts this: the explicit matching systems that do exist optimize for engagement/novelty rather than reciprocity and demonstrated cooperation. The result is reduced control over WHO populates one's social field, weaker assortment on actual cooperativeness, and loss of the transitive-reputation signal that let ancestral humans cluster with trustworthy reciprocators out to two degrees. The mismatch is a broken selection rule: the inputs the ancestral algorithm ran on (mutual choice, observed reciprocity, cooperator reputation) are no longer the primary determinants of who one lives and works among.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M3 - Social Bonding  ·  evidence links: 4

#### Relationship longevity & maintenance (durable dyads; maintenance institutions like hxaro conserve bonds across distance)
`EEA-SOC-07 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** In ancestral foraging life the load-bearing social unit was the durable, reciprocal DYAD, not the group. Groups continuously reshuffled (entire camps shifted location every 4 to 8 weeks, with most campmates changing from one survey wave to the next). Among the Ju/'hoansi, hxaro was a formal delayed-reciprocity gift-partnership system with ties passed down across generations (ext 2272), spanning dispersed bands up to ~200 km apart; the average person spent 4.4 months a year living with hxaro partners (ext 752). Trivers's conditions for the evolution of reciprocal altruism describe exactly this regime: a long lifetime, low dispersal, and repeated interaction with the same small set of individuals, so that an investment in a specific partner reliably paid back over time.

**Now (modern default):** The modern environment inverts the ancestral pattern: it supplies stable GROUP labels (a workplace, a school cohort, a social-media feed, a city) while the dyads inside them are shallow and disposable, and there is no institutional equivalent of hxaro that conserves a specific bond once physical proximity ends. When two people stop sharing a context, the relationship is simply allowed to decay; nothing structurally obligates either party to keep servicing it. Dunbar proposes that friendships require recurring face-to-face contact to avoid sliding down through the network layers and eventually dropping out of the personal network entirely, and that online media at best slows that decay rather than preventing it (ext 509). Sutcliffe and colleagues propose that trust is a function of the time invested in a relationship, and that the costs of maintaining a relationship (most obviously, time) are roughly linear with the number of relationships (ext 503, 505).

**The gap to work with:** The specific missing piece is a maintenance institution: the EEA had hxaro-type structures (formalized, inheritable, distance-spanning, long-term delayed reciprocity) that actively preserved a named dyad, whereas modernity has no hxaro-equivalent and instead relies on shared proximity/context as the only glue. When that proximity ends (job change, move, leaving school), the bond is not migrated to an institution that keeps it alive; it decays. The result is many stable-looking memberships sitting on top of disposable, shallow ties.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** The durable, spatially-dispersed reciprocal DYAD plus the maintenance institutions that conserve it across distance and time, i.e., a specific named partner whose bond is actively serviced (face-to-face when possible, and via hxaro-type delayed-reciprocity exchange when apart) so the relationship persists and survives the reshuffling of any particular group. The resolution requires migrating a bond to an institution that keeps it alive once proximity ends, not merely accumulating more low-investment ties.

#### Novel-stranger frequency
`EEA-SOC-08 · Social structure` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Social cognition operated over a bounded pool: a concurrent active network capped at about 150 people, nested in ~5/15/50/150 layers of decreasing intimacy. Within that architecture almost every face encountered was already known, placed, and reputationally tracked. A genuinely novel stranger, an individual who could not be located anywhere in one's known network or in the known networks of one's allies, was therefore a comparatively rare event. Because the bounded pool had no slot for such a person, novelty itself was diagnostic: a never-before-seen individual could not be vouched for, had no shared reputational history, and could not be assumed to be bound by the local web of reciprocity and norm enforcement. Encounters with unplaceable strangers were thus both infrequent and highly salient, and the threat-management system (M1) treated unfamiliarity as a plausible danger cue to be resolved (placed, vouched-for, or avoided) rather than as a routine background condition. This is an inference from the bounded-pool architecture plus M1, not a directly measured ancestral rate.

**Now (modern default):** The M1 machinery still treats unplaceable others as potential threats.

**The gap to work with:** The delta is one of base rate and resolvability. Ancestrally, novel strangers were rare, salient, and resolvable (place them in the known web, or treat unfamiliarity as an actionable threat signal); modernity inverts this to constant, non-diagnostic, unresolvable novelty. The threat-management system inherited from the rare-stranger regime cannot down-regulate to the new base rate: it either over-fires (chronic low-grade vigilance, stranger-directed wariness in crowds and online) or, where it habituates, leaves people transacting with a stream of others who carry none of the reputational accountability the system implicitly assumes. The conserved unit being eroded is not the count of strangers but the durable, reputationally-tracked dyad against which any new face used to be evaluated; once almost everyone is a stranger, the bounded-pool premise that made novelty meaningful no longer holds.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** The durable, reputationally-tracked reciprocal dyad: ancestrally every new face was evaluated against a finite web of known, vouched-for relationships, and it was that web of bonds - not the count of strangers per se - that gave novelty its diagnostic and protective meaning. The mismatch resolves only insofar as new encounters can be anchored back into durable, accountable relationships rather than left as anonymous one-off contacts.

#### Behavioral observability / reputation visibility
`EEA-SOC-09 · Social structure` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** In a band/camp embedded in a personally-known reference group, a person's relevant behavior was directly observable by, or transmitted through gossip to, people who knew them by name and knew their history. Reputation was a shared, continuously-updated, distributed record: as Boehm puts it, "the band keeps a dossier on every individual, noting positive and negative points." Where campmates differed, they knew it: asked one by one in 12 Hadza camps, they agreed more on who hunts best (r 0.23) and a little more on who works hardest (r 0.08). They barely agreed on who shares the most food, who tells the fewest lies and who has the best heart (ext 2795). Project position (21 Nov 2026): anyone still in camp shared and was honest enough to be kept there, so on those points there was little left to rank, and such labels sort strangers more than campmates. The study's authors read the low agreement as moral behaviour that changes across time and situations to fit local circumstances (ext 2796). Observability had teeth: those who deviated (theft, deception, stinginess, free-riding) were subject to gossip, ridicule, ostracism, and ultimately exclusion, and everyone knew this monitoring was constant, so it functioned as a deterrent and most foragers tried to exert self-control accordingly. Tomasello argues that because foraging was obligately collaborative, individuals had to evaluate partners and track how they themselves were being evaluated (ext 239). The net result: visibility was embodied, hard to fake over time, attached to a single durable identity, and backed by real consequences.

**Now (modern default):** The visibility that does exist is mediated, curated, and quantitatively inflated. On average, UK respondents considered only 27.6% of their Facebook friends genuine close friends (with a strong modal value between 0 and 10%), so networks are padded with weakly-accountable acquaintances rather than people tracking one's actual conduct. Mediated contact cannot substitute for the embodied, face-to-face observation the bonding/reputation system evolved for: Dunbar proposes that without periodic in-person contact, ties decay through the network layers into mere acquaintance, and that social media may slow this decay (ext 509). Visibility is now selectively self-presented (gameable), fragmented across multiple personas and contexts, and policed (if at all) by distant institutions or algorithms rather than by named peers with whom one shares a future. Reputation is either absent (anonymity), sortable away (exit/blocking, fresh accounts), or captured by platform metrics.

**The gap to work with:** The ancestral environment delivered embodied, hard-to-fake, single-identity behavioral observability inside a reference group whose members knew one's history and held real enforcement power. The modern environment delivers visibility that is mediated, curated, gameable, fragmented across exitable contexts, padded with non-accountable weak ties, and largely stripped of peer-enforced consequences. The reputation-tracking machinery (M11) and status-position monitoring (M5) evolved to run on dense, durable, accountable observation now operate on thin, performative, low-accountability signals, producing both reputational impunity for defection among strangers and chronic exposure of curated personas to vast unfamiliar audiences with no reciprocal knowledge of the person behind them.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M5 - Status Monitoring  ·  evidence links: 11

#### Leadership and dominance (no rulers; prestige; exit available)
`EEA-SOC-10 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Mobile hunter-gatherer bands had no rulers. Decisions that affected the group were reached by discussion toward consensus. Influence came from prestige. Anyone who tried to dominate was mocked, shunned or expelled, and in extreme cases killed. People could also leave a camp for another one, and that option checked anyone who tried to take control.

**Now (modern default):** Rulers are chosen at state scale. The polity is in practice impossible to leave.

**The gap to work with:** The forager checks on dominance (ridicule, shunning, walking away, a voice in a face-to-face decision) have no reach over rulers. One person's say shrinks from a share of a camp consensus to one vote.

#### Status reference group (ranked against people who know you)
`EEA-SOC-11 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** A forager's standing was judged by people who knew them. Daily life ran in a camp of about 30 (Hadza) inside a lifetime pool of roughly 1,000 individually known people (Ache, Hadza). The status monitor weighs this local audience most: across 15 small-scale communities (899 people), the shame people expected to feel over an act tracked how much their own community would think less of them (mean r 0.84) more closely than how other communities would (0.69). In four US studies, respect in one's face-to-face groups went with well-being more strongly than income or education did, and well-being rose and fell with that standing.

**Now (modern default):** Rank can be compared against people who will never know you: celebrities, strangers' edited lives, follower and like counts. No study in Cor measures how many people a person compares themselves with online. The measured link between social media use and low mood is small (r about 0.1 in meta-analyses of adolescents) and in time-use diaries the links between digital-screen engagement and adolescent well-being were smaller than a preregistered cutoff for a practically significant effect (ext 102).

**The gap to work with:** The audience that sets a person's standing grows from a group that knows them to an open-ended field of strangers whose judgement the person cannot meet or change. Hours online are not the measure (DA4): the link between time on social media and low mood is small, and the cost showed where Facebook arrived at US colleges in 2004 to 2006, most for students more likely to compare badly with their peers (ext 2031, 2032).

#### How standing is earned (prestige and dominance)
`EEA-SOC-12 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Two routes to rank exist: dominance, through fear, and prestige, through skill that others choose to defer to. In experiments with strangers both routes work. Among the Chabu prestige was the more important route to leader status, and among egalitarian foragers leadership depends on individual ability and demonstrated success in activities the group values and is often specific to the context. Authority from skill stays inside the activity: Mbendjele BaYaka specialists are consulted on elephant hunting, healing or ritual and can oblige no one, and Tsimane fishing-trip organisers are spread fairly evenly across households and take no more fish than others. Respect is different: in one Tsimane village of 57 men, fighting ability, influence and respect sat with the same few men, and among the Chabu most leadership traits formed one general leadership and respect factor.

**Now (modern default):** Standing is earned in large organisations and markets through titles, pay and audience size. Formal authority carries across domains: a manager commands in every part of the job, and wealth or fame buys influence outside the skill that earned it.

**The gap to work with:** In the forager record, command stayed inside the activity and could be refused; modern rank carries command across domains and is hard to refuse or leave. The concentration of respect in a few people is the part that may be old: it shows in one Tsimane village and among the Chabu.

#### What standing buys (children)
`EEA-SOC-13 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Across 33 non-industrial societies, higher-status men had greater reproductive success (meta-analysis of 288 results, overall r 0.19). The effect in foragers (Zr 0.18) was about the same as in farmers and herders. Status went with men's fertility and mating success more than with lower child mortality, and the effect on surviving children was about a third smaller where marriage was monogamous. Better hunters had higher age-corrected reproductive success than other men in their group in five societies (Ache, Hadza, !Kung, Lamalera, Meriam). Among the Tsimane both dominance and prestige went with higher fertility.

**Now (modern default):** In industrial societies the link between men's income and number of children is somewhat weaker than in pre-industrial societies, and the sexes differ: men with higher incomes still have more children, mainly because poor men more often stay childless, while higher income in women and more education in both sexes typically go with fewer children (ext 5072). Richerson and Boyd argue that professional status-seeking takes the time and money needed for reproduction (ext 410).

**The gap to work with:** Status-seeking was tied to children; the motive still runs while the payoff in children has weakened or reversed. Across industrial populations, wealth, measured mostly as income, went with higher fertility in 8 of 12 longitudinal findings, and income went with more children in men and fewer in women (Stulp and Barrett 2016); in three industrial samples the link between men's wealth and their number of children was the weakest of eleven human samples, though still positive (ext 3657), and in Britain higher-income men had more children only because low-income men were more often childless, while women's own income went with fewer children (ext 3658).

#### Wealth gaps (low to moderate in foragers)
`EEA-SOC-14 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Forager wealth inequality was low to moderate. Weighted wealth Gini coefficients were 0.25 for hunter-gatherers and 0.27 for horticulturalists, against 0.42 for pastoralists and 0.48 for farmers, where land and livestock can be owned and inherited (21 small-scale populations). Across eleven kinds of forager wealth most Ginis fell between 0.2 and 0.5 when body weight is left out; the two material measures averaged 0.36. House sizes at 63 archaeological sites give the same order: median Gini 0.17 at hunter-gatherer sites, 0.35 at agricultural sites.

**Now (modern default):** Wealth Gini coefficients in modern states are far higher: about 0.80 for the USA in 2000 and 0.73 for China, figures Kohler et al. cite from other studies. Among the Ju/'hoansi, possession data from 1974 (/Kae/kae, Botswana) and 2017 to 2018 (Nyae Nyae, Namibia) show that cash income brought large differences in material wealth, even between neighbours (ext 1034).

**The gap to work with:** Wealth gaps went from small, mostly in body and skill, to very large, mostly in owned and inherited property. Ju/'hoansi social and political equality held for decades after cash arrived, so a wealth gap does not by itself bring rule by the wealthy.

#### Famous strangers (one-way bonds)
`EEA-SOC-15 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** Everyone a forager heard about repeatedly in detailed stories could possibly be met. The lifetime pool of individually known people was about 1,000, and the people one heard about in stories were tied to people one knew. A person you saw and heard often knew you back.

**Now (modern default):** Media supply one-way bonds with people who do not know you exist. In a quota sample of 2,028 Hungarian adults, 39% had a favourite celebrity, and on the authors' scale 4.5% of adults and 8.5% of 18 to 34 year olds had a high level of admiration. Of 151 US early adolescents, 61% thought of their favourite media figure as a relationship partner: girls mostly as a friend, boys more often as a mentor.

**The gap to work with:** The bonding and status systems meet familiar, admired faces that never return the bond and never rank you. In a preregistered experiment, thinking about a favourite media figure lowered negative mood about as much as thinking about a friend, and did not lower loneliness, which thinking about a friend did (ext 1371); De Backer et al. 2007 read celebrity interest as learning from models plus mistaking celebrities for members of one's own network (argued).

#### Size of "us" (the language group)
`EEA-SOC-16 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** A forager lived in a camp of a few dozen inside a wider group that shared a language and intermarried. Across 339 forager societies the regional ethnic unit had a geometric mean of about 840 people, with a wide spread (one standard deviation on the log scale runs from roughly 240 to 2,900). In 2012 the Eastern Hadza numbered about 950 people in 49 named bands sharing one language; the Ache tribe was defined by one language, had no tribe-wide leaders, and never gathered as a whole.

**Now (modern default):** People identify with nations, parties, religions and fan bases.

**The gap to work with:** The largest group a forager counted as 'us' held hundreds to a few thousand people, linked by marriage. Modern 'us' groups are held together by labels and media.

#### Contact between groups (visiting and exchange alongside hostility)
`EEA-SOC-17 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** Forager bands were linked. Among the Hadza and Ache, adults who were in-laws or ritual partners interacted across bands more often, and in-law ties mattered about as much as blood ties. In 1974 Ju/'hoansi adults at /Kae/kae kept on average 15 gift-exchange partners living 2 to 200 km away and spent on average 4.4 months a year living with them. Humans form peaceful ties between groups, which chimpanzees do not. Hostility was also part of the record: the Ache and Hadza had hostile relations with neighbouring ethnic groups and no long-distance trade, and in 20 of 21 mobile forager societies about 15% of recorded killings were between groups.

**Now (modern default):** Contact between large groups runs through trade, media and institutions.

**The gap to work with:** Forager groups were tied by marriage, visits and gifts that ran through people one knew, alongside real hostility toward some neighbours.

#### What marks someone as "them" (alliance cues)
`EEA-SOC-19 · Social structure` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Foragers marked groups by language and dialect, and, Kurzban, Tooby and Cosmides argue, rarely met people genetically distant enough to look like another race (ext 1052). The tested part: the mind sorts people by alliance, and the sorting can be redrawn within minutes. When team membership cut across race, US participants sorted people by race less (a preregistered replication with 463 people found the drop at a smaller size; race was not erased). Arbitrary labels produce favouritism toward one's own group; a meta-analysis of cooperation experiments gives d 0.32, larger when outcomes depend on each other, and driven more by favouring 'us' than by harming 'them'.

**Now (modern default):** Large labelled groups (parties, nations, teams) supply alliance cues without any shared life. In the US, the gap between feelings toward one's own and the other party grew from 22.64 to 40.87 points on a 0 to 100 scale between 1978 and 2016, driven by colder feelings toward the other side. The rise is not general: across 12 OECD democracies six saw it fall. Posts about the political out-group are shared more (each out-group word about 67% higher odds, US media and Congress). US students claim a team's win as their own and say 'we' nearly twice as often after a win.

**The gap to work with:** The alliance-sorting system now runs on labels, with no shared life and no repeated contact, and media reward posts about 'them' (Rathje et al. 2021, eight datasets). Whether moralised posts in general spread more is contested: Brady et al. 2017 found it in 563,312 tweets, and a re-test on six other Twitter corpora gave inconsistent results (Burton, Cruz and Hahn 2021).

#### Food shared and eaten in company
`EEA-SOC-20 · Social structure` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** A review of 45 small-scale groups reports food transfers beyond the household throughout, and in all 10 groups where it was checked big game reached more families than small game. Among the Hadza of Tanzania at least 90% of the meat of large animals left the hunter's household. Among the Aka of the Congo a cook and her children ate about 20% of the stew she made, about 75% of the stew a woman ate came from other women's pots, serving was done in plain view, and the men usually ate together at the men's shelter. Among the Ju/'hoansi of Botswana each family ate the evening meal at its own fire and people then gathered at shared fires. Among the Great Andamanese a pig brought home whole was roasted at a public cooking place and each family ate at its own hut. Batek camps in Malaysia, about 34 people, shared food at once, stored none and ate almost nothing out of camp. Hadza men ate on the foray, out of camp, about 90 percent of what they are estimated to spend in a day, most of it honey (ext 3162). No record shows a whole camp eating as one table every day. Sharing with the whole band was the exception and went with bands under 40 to 50 people.

**Now (modern default):** In the United States 26% of adults ate every meal of the previous day alone in 2023, more than half again above the 2003 level; among people living alone it was about 70%. In a UK poll of 2,000 adults nearly 70% had never had a meal with a neighbour. Shared meals vary widely between countries today: 11.7 lunches and dinners a week with someone known in Senegal, 2.7 in Bangladesh and Estonia. Food now comes from a shop, and a household's food does not pass through other households.

**The gap to work with:** A forager ate food that others in the camp had caught, carried or cooked, and others ate theirs, among people who knew who gave what. A person who eats alone from a shop gets the calories without the giving and receiving. Project position: the missing exchange is what lowers mood. Among 37,193 older Japanese adults followed for three years, men living alone who usually ate alone had 2.36 times the rate of new depressive symptoms of men living alone who ate with others, after adjustment for income, health, nutrition and other factors (ext 3684); in a cross-sectional study, people who lived with their families yet ate alone had the highest odds of depressive symptoms (ext 3583).

### Mating & reproduction

#### Who picks the partner (kin-arranged marriage)
`EEA-MATE-01 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** In most forager societies parents or close kin arranged marriages. In Apostolou's coding of 190 forager societies, arrangement by parents or close kin was the main way to marry in 85%, and marriage involved an exchange between the two families (brideservice, brideprice) in 80%. A phylogenetic reconstruction puts arranged marriage at least as far back as the migrations out of Africa. Courtship also existed: 3 of the 8 African forager societies in the sample marry by courtship, so the oldest African pattern is open. How much say the young people had is not coded in any source read; Hadza men and women state clear preferences for a spouse. Among the Tsimane forager-horticulturalists of Bolivia, asked why they married their spouse, women most often answered that they married against their will (37.6%, ext 5850). Among the Ju/'hoansi, more than 60% of brides said they had been mildly or strongly reluctant about their first marriage, wives started 90% of the divorces in first marriages, and after a divorce women had much more say in choosing the next husband (Wiessner 2009 as reported by Scelza 2013, ext 5855). Among Himba pastoralists of Namibia, 9% of women's first marriages were love matches against 62% of second marriages (ext 5856). Across five non-Western societies, scores for intimacy, passion and commitment did not differ between arranged and free-choice marriages in the pooled data (ext 5859).

**Now (modern default):** Individuals choose, and increasingly choose strangers. Among US heterosexual couples, 39% of those who met in 2017 met online, against 2% in 1995; meeting through friends fell from 33% to 20% and through family from 15% to 7%. In 2017, 89% of couples who met online had no personal connection before.

**The gap to work with:** Partner choice moved from families and kin, who knew both people, to individuals searching alone among strangers. The people who once filtered and vouched for a partner are now mostly out of the decision.

#### The pool of possible partners
`EEA-MATE-02 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** A forager chose from a small pool of people who were known before anything started. Among the Aka, the area a person explores over a lifetime, looking for a mate, visiting kin and hunting, reaches about 50 km and holds about 700 people they can meet and get to know (ext 841); half of the places on the researchers' list that an Aka adult had visited lay within 30 km of camp (ext 3204). The lifetime pool of individually known people was about 1,000 in the Ache and Hadza. Possible partners came with a known family and a reputation.

**Now (modern default):** Online, the pool is effectively unbounded and made of strangers. On one large US dating site, attention was very unequal: most users got a handful of first messages at most while a few got hundreds (one woman in New York got 1,504 in a month). Men and women mostly wrote to people about 25% more desirable than themselves, and men writing to more desirable women got replies at most 21% of the time.

**The gap to work with:** The pool grew from hundreds of known people to an open-ended list of strangers judged from a profile. Attention concentrates on a few, and most people's approaches go unanswered.

#### Marriage system (mostly one spouse at a time, often several over a life, polygyny allowed in most societies)
`EEA-MATE-03 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Most societies in the ethnographic record permitted a man more than one wife (about 85%), but in forager societies most marriages were monogamous. In 87% of 190 forager societies fewer than 20% of men were married polygynously; among tropical African foragers polygyny runs from 3% to 20%; among Savanna Pume foragers 11% of men and 20% of women are ever married polygynously. Men contributing more food went with more monogamy. Serial pairing was common: among the !Kung and Ache, men and women had children with more than one partner. Over a life, many people had several spouses: Ache women in the pre-contact forest period averaged 12.1 husbands and about 63% of Ache children had a secondary father (ext 1730); perhaps 20% of Hadza stay married to the same person for life (ext 1729). Among the Tsimane of Bolivia, forager-horticulturalists, fewer than 20% of marriages ended in divorce (15 of 76 marriages begun more than 20 years before), most often in the first year, and 5 to 10% of men were married polygynously, almost always to two sisters (ext 5849). Of 25 forager populations in a meta-analysis of reproductive skew, 10 had normative monogamy, 13 tolerated but rare polygyny and 2 normative polygyny (ext 3066). Across 142 societies of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, the rule on how many wives a man may have tracked the share of men and of women actually married polygynously (r .76 and .81, ext 5835). Starkweather and Hames identified 53 societies outside the classical Himalayan and Marquesan area that permit polyandry, one woman with more than one husband, and report that it is most common in egalitarian societies (ext 5833). Genetic studies estimate more breeding women than breeding men: a female to male ratio of 2.1 (San) to 12.5 (Basque) in one study of six populations (ext 5841), and 1.33 (East Asians) to 2.63 (Yoruba) in corrected HapMap estimates (ext 5842, 5843). Hammer and colleagues read the excess as polygyny, formal and effective; Labuda and colleagues read their corrected values as within the range of monogamous or serially monogamous societies, while also overlapping with polygynous ones.

**Now (modern default):** Law imposes one spouse at a time in most states (Japan from 1880, China 1953, India 1955, Nepal 1963). Serial pairing through divorce and new partners continues.

**The gap to work with:** Small gap. Forager practice was already mostly one spouse at a time with some polygyny and remarriage; modern law removes the polygynous minority. Forager marriages often ended: across ten forager and forager-farmer populations reported divorce rates run from 18% (Agta) to nearly every couple (north Alaskan Inuit), and the reviewers put the general level at a quarter to a third of marriages or more, with remarriage common (ext 1728). The rates were counted differently in each population, so they cannot be averaged or set against a modern rate. Where polygyny is practised today: in 56 villages of northern Tanzania, within villages polygyny was not significantly related to food security, height-for-age or weight-for-height (ext 5845); among the Dogon of Mali the hazard of death by age 5 was higher in work-eat groups with more married women per married man in villages 1 to 8, and only in village 9, exceptionally large and wealthy, did polygyny show no adverse effect (ext 5848).

#### Timing: puberty to first birth
`EEA-MATE-04 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Forager women had their first child at about 19 to 20 in five groups (Hadza 18.8, !Kung 19.2, Ache 19.5, Hiwi 20.5, ext 844; Agta 20.2, ext 845) and at about 15.4 among the Pume (Kramer 2008, ext 3816). Mean age at first menstruation ranged from 12.6 to 18.4 across 22 natural-fertility societies, and first birth followed about four years later. Last births came near 39.

**Now (modern default):** US median age at first menstruation was 11.9 in 2013 to 2017. US mean age at first birth rose from 21.4 in 1970 to 25.0 in 2006 and 27.5 in 2023; in 2006 it was 29.4 in Switzerland, and it rose in every rich country compared.

**The gap to work with:** The years between being able to reproduce and doing it grew from about four to about fifteen (US: menarche near 12, first birth near 27.5). Gluckman and Hanson argue that biological maturity now comes well before social maturity for the first time.

#### Number of children and spacing
`EEA-MATE-05 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Forager women who lived through their reproductive years had about six births: Ache 8.2, Agta 6.9, Hadza 6.2, Hiwi 5.5, Ju/'hoansi 4.3; the mean across 75 natural-fertility societies was 5.98. Births were spaced about 3 to 4 years apart (hunter-gatherer average 3.7 years). About a third of forager women survived to complete their fertility. With pregnancy and long breastfeeding, menstrual cycles were few: among the Dogon, natural-fertility farmers, a woman had about 100 menstruations in her life.

**Now (modern default):** World births per woman fell from 4.84 in 1950 to 2.23 in 2021, and fell in every country. Among European women born in 1960, 10% (Greece, Spain) to 20% (Germany) had no child, a level also seen in women born around 1900. A US woman with three births has about three times the Dogon count of menstruations.

**The gap to work with:** Fewer children, fewer pregnancies and far more menstrual cycles. Each birth lowered the relative risk of breast cancer by 7.0 percent and each 12 months of breastfeeding by a further 4.3 percent across 47 studies in 30 countries, and the authors estimate that the births and breastfeeding usual in developing countries until recently would cut the cumulative risk by age 70 in developed countries from 6.3 to 2.7 per 100 women (Collaborative Group on Hormonal Factors in Breast Cancer 2002). Women in the top quarter of lifetime ovulatory cycles had 1.81 times the odds of ovarian cancer of women in the bottom quarter (Pelucchi et al. 2007). Childlessness at 10 to 20% is high, but it is no new level for Europe. No forager source on the share of adults who never reproduce was found.

#### What gets a person chosen, and how unequal men are
`EEA-MATE-06 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** In five societies (Ache, Hadza, !Kung, Lamalera, Meriam), men who hunted better had higher age-corrected reproductive success than other men in their group, and Hadza men with deeper voices and stronger upper bodies had more children. Hadza women put great weight on a man's foraging, and men on a woman working hard. Men's number of children varied more than women's: the male to female variance ratio was 1.66 among the Aka, 1.77 among the !Kung, 1.93 among the Hadza and 4.22 among the Ache. How large the gap is depends on the marriage system; in monogamous populations it is about 1.

**Now (modern default):** Stated preferences keep the same sex differences across 45 countries (14,399 people): men weight looks and youth more, women earning prospects and an older partner. On dating sites attention concentrates on a few. In the US, the share of men aged 18 to 24 with no sex in the past year rose from 18.9% (2000 to 2002) to 30.9% (2016 to 2018), and men with lower income and no full-time work were more often sexually inactive.

**The gap to work with:** Choice used to run on skill and character seen over years in a small group. On apps it runs on photos and stated traits, and the sex differences in stated preferences barely show up in whom people like when they meet. Whether the rise in sexless young men comes from this shift is untested.

#### Rivals and comparison
`EEA-MATE-07 · Mating & reproduction` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** A forager's rivals for a partner were the same-sex people of the camp and the regional network, a few hundred people seen in ordinary life. No study measures this directly; it follows from the size of the pool (EEA-MATE-02).

**Now (modern default):** Media and feeds show a stream of the most attractive and successful people alive, edited. In an experiment, women who saw highly attractive women rated their own value as a marriage partner lower, and men who saw socially dominant men did the same. Across 77 studies, exposure to thin-ideal media images went with more body dissatisfaction in women (d -0.28). A later meta-analysis of 204 studies found the effect minimal for most women and present mainly in women already dissatisfied with their bodies (ext 3180). Across 24 experiments, appearance-ideal images on social media lowered body image moderately against images unrelated to appearance (Hedges g -0.61), and across 14 experiments more than other appearance images did (g -0.68; de Valle et al. 2021).

**The gap to work with:** The comparison set grew from people one knew to the top of a global field, chosen and edited for display. Whether this lowers self-rated mate value outside the lab is untested; the one tested contrast effect from the same lab, for erotica, failed to replicate.

#### Sexual stimuli (real people vs unlimited images)
`EEA-MATE-08 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Sexual cues came from real people a forager knew and could meet. No study measures this directly. Among the Aka, the Hewletts found no word for masturbation, and men asked about masturbating before marriage or during the post-partum sex taboo all said it did not occur (Hewlett and Hewlett 2010, ext 3844).

**Now (modern default):** Pornography is the norm: in a nationally representative sample of US adult internet users, about 70% had viewed it and just over half had in the past year. About 11% of men and 3% of women agreed at least somewhat that they were addicted to it. Across 50 studies, use went with lower sexual and relationship satisfaction in men; most studies are cross-sectional. In a US probability survey, use went with rough sex such as choking.

**The gap to work with:** The sexual incentive system now meets unlimited images of strangers chosen to be maximally arousing. In three preregistered replications, viewing the images did not lower attraction or love for a partner, against the one 1989 experiment that reported it. In two US panels, husbands who used them more reported lower marital quality six years later (among wives the link ran the other way), and starting use went with divorce by the next survey wave in women and men. Feeling addicted tracks moral conflict about one's own use as well as how much one uses.

#### Sex and conception coupled
`EEA-MATE-09 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** For foragers, sex led to pregnancy unless breastfeeding or low energy suppressed ovulation. Birth spacing of 3 to 4 years came from breastfeeding and the energy cost of lactation (EEA-MATE-05).

**Now (modern default):** Contraception separates sex from conception. In 2019, 47.7% of all women aged 15 to 49 worldwide used a modern method, 20.1 points more than in 1970, from 1.9% in South Sudan to 87.9% in Norway. In the US, the pill sped up the fall in marital fertility in the 1960s.

**The gap to work with:** Sex no longer signals possible children. The motivation system that rewards sex runs as before, while the number of children is now set by a separate decision.

#### Paternity and affairs (rates vary between societies)
`EEA-MATE-10 · Mating & reproduction` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Rates of children fathered outside the pair vary a lot between societies. In genetic studies of mostly European-descent populations, and in the Dogon of Mali, the rate is about 1 to 2%; across 67 studies the rate for men confident of their paternity was 1.9%. Within one European population over 500 years, the rate among married couples ranged from 0.4 to 5.9 percent, highest in poor families in crowded late-19th-century cities (Larmuseau et al. 2019). Among Himba pastoralists in Namibia it was 48%, and the community knew which children these were. In 53 of 76 lowland South American societies with data, people believe a child can have more than one father. No genetic paternity rate for a forager population was found. In the ethnographic codes of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, 54% of societies condone men's extramarital sex and 11% condone women's, and women's extramarital sex is reported at a moderate or universal level in 55% (ext 5854) or 57% (ext 997) of societies.

**Now (modern default):** Modern Western samples give the same 1 to 2%. Paternity can now be tested genetically.

**The gap to work with:** No gap is shown. Cor has no forager genetic paternity rate to compare with, and the modern Western figures match the ones that exist. A page cannot claim that affairs are more or less common than in the past.

### Care & development

#### Alloparental care supply (a village, not a dyad)
`EEA-CARE-01 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral human infants were not raised by a mother alone but by a network of allomothers - fathers, grandmothers, older siblings, and trusted non-kin - who shared both direct care (holding, carrying, soothing) and provisioning (food). Hrdy argues that humans are a cooperatively breeding ape (ext 22): unlike other African apes, whose mothers nurture infants essentially on their own, hominin mothers relied on groupmates to protect, care for, and provision their unusually slow-maturing, costly young. A human child requires on the order of 13 million calories from birth to independence, and humans breed faster (every 3-4 years) than chimpanzees and orangutans (about every 5.5 and 7.7 years; gorillas breed about every 3.8 years, Galdikas and Wood 1990, ext 3519) while producing larger, slower-maturing babies - a life-history paradox Hrdy argues is solvable only by shared provisioning (ext 401). Across foraging ethnography, infants were handled by people other than the mother from shortly after birth: among the Hadza, the mother does roughly 69% of the time children aged four and under were held (ext 2128) and allomothers the rest; !Kung infants have the mother's entire social world available with intense face-to-face interactions from girls; among the Trobriand Islanders, who are horticulturalists, infants are suckled by lactating allomothers. The infant's evolved expectation is a reliable surround of multiple invested caregivers, not a single isolated dyad.

**Now (modern default):** Hrdy argues that the idealized breadwinner-father / homemaker-mother / biological-children unit is a historical aberration roughly a century old (Victorian at most; the 1950s template in the US), departing from the extended-kin allomaternal norm that held for the vast majority of human history (ext 403). Care that was distributed across many hands is now concentrated on one or two.

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is between the reliable, present, multi-caregiver surround the infant's attachment and the mother's reproductive psychology evolved to expect, and the thin one- or two-adult household the modern environment supplies. Hrdy argues that maternal commitment is conditional on perceived alloparental support (M9, ext 402). This is a supply mismatch, not a parental-competence failure.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliable, present alloparental supply - care plus provisioning actually delivered by invested caregivers - NOT bare kin count or genealogical presence. Four relatives who live far away and do not share the daily load do not satisfy the parameter; what the infant and mother expect is dependable, hands-on, repeated care from a stable surround.

**Serves:** M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting  ·  evidence links: 27

#### Caregiver continuity and reliability (present, not merely countable)
`EEA-CARE-02 · Care & development` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Attachment is not a single dyadic bond but secure attachment to one or more trusted caretakers, Bowlby's design refined by Hrdy to plural figures, and human infants are equipped with traits (cuteness, prolonged eye contact, social smiling) for recruiting the attention of potential caretakers. Crucially, maternal commitment itself is contingent: for a cooperatively breeding mammal, the capacity to conceive is only part of the equation; the mother must be able to carry through to rearing independence, which she assesses against perceived alloparental support. Touch is part of this continuity. In the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample the mother slept in the same room as her infant in every one of the 173 societies coded, and in the same bed in at least 76 (ext 705). Field argues that the skin is the first and the largest sense organ to develop prenatally (ext 296). The expectation is not a list of relatives but caregivers who are reliably, physically present across time.

**Now (modern default):** In the United States of the late 1970s infants spent little time in body contact with caregivers, their movements were restricted by playpens, high chairs or cribs, and separate sleeping was regularly recommended (ext 3145); all Euro-American infants observed in Washington, D.C. had their own crib in their own room (ext 3151), and of the 18 US infants, 15 slept near their mothers until 3 to 6 months of age, when most were moved to a separate room (ext 3435). Caregivers may be nominally present (counted on paper) yet not reliably, responsively available in the moment-to-moment way the regulatory system expects.

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is between caregiver reliability/continuity as an experienced, present regulatory input and a modern environment that often supplies caregivers who are countable but not dependably present. Because maternal investment and infant attachment are both calibrated to perceived caregiver reliability (M9, M15), low continuity is not neutral: it degrades the secure-base scaffolding and the touch-mediated regulation the infant evolved to expect, even when the number of caregivers on paper looks adequate.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliable, present caregiver supply - responsive caregivers actually and repeatedly available across time, including continuity of body contact - NOT a count of designated or genealogically-related caregivers. The naive reading (enough named caregivers exist) misleads; what regulates the infant is dependable presence.

**Serves:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting  ·  evidence links: 14

#### Mixed-age child playgroups as developmental input
`EEA-CARE-03 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral children developed social competence through play with a mixed-age band of other children - younger and older mixed together - rather than under continuous adult supervision or in age-segregated peer cohorts. Rough-and-tumble play during the juvenile period is a dedicated developmental input: in the mammalian design, play is a distinct emotional system (Panksepp's PLAY) and, in rats, play experience physically tunes the maturing prefrontal cortex (ext 212), refining, as Pellis and Pellis propose (ext 216), the circuits that later dampen the amygdala and support emotional regulation. Critically, the AMOUNT and quality of play partners matters: female rats raised with more play partners show progressively greater play experience and corresponding PFC development, and even the untreated playmates of more-playful partners tended to have greater cortical thickness (ext 220) - play is a partnered input, so a richer multi-partner (and, in the human case, multi-age) play environment is the expected calibrating stimulus.

**Now (modern default):** Modern childhood compresses or eliminates free mixed-age play: age-segregated grade cohorts and daycare, adult-structured and supervised activities, indoor screen-based leisure, reduced unsupervised outdoor roaming, and safety norms that discourage rough-and-tumble.

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is between mixed-age, self-organized, physically-active play as the evolved calibrating input for the juvenile prefrontal cortex (M4, embedded in the alloparental child-minding network, M9) and a modern environment that supplies age-segregated, adult-structured substitutes. In rats, play with partners shapes the developing cortex (Pellis & Pellis 2009, ext 212, 220). Cor reads reduced free play in children as a missing developmental input. In children, general risky outdoor play (13 studies) and places that allow risky play (21 studies) went with better physical, mental, social and cognitive outcomes in a review of 74 studies (Ramsden et al. 2026, ext 5097), and children whose parents reported more time playing adventurously before the pandemic had slightly fewer internalising symptoms and more positive affect during the first Covid-19 lockdown (Dodd, Nesbit and FitzGibbon 2023, ext 5104).

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Self-directed, mixed-age, physically-active free play actually engaged in - partnered play experience - NOT adult-structured activities, solitary screen play, or scheduled lessons that occupy time without delivering the rough-and-tumble, role-reversal, peer-negotiated input the circuits expect.

**Serves:** M4 - Social Calibration / Play, M9 - Care / Alloparenting  ·  evidence links: 18

#### Multigenerational presence (older people inside the band's giving and getting)
`EEA-CARE-04 · Care & development` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Older people kept a place in the band's giving and getting. They gave food (ext 3088), care (ext 2084) and knowledge (ext 3827) where they could, and they were given to (ext 3088, 3665). The project's position: reciprocity runs across the whole life, so a grandmother who gave for a lifetime holds her place when she takes. What older people gave differed by group. Among the Hadza, grandmothers foraged most when their newest grandchild was youngest (ext 2063). Among the Tsimane, net food flowed down the generations: grandmothers gave a net 117 kcal a day to their grandchildren and grandfathers 223, into their 70s (ext 3088). Among the Aka, care by others, largely grandmothers, lowered mothers' work by up to 216 kcal over nine hours (ext 2084). Tsimane adults named grandparents most often as the source of the stories they knew (ext 3827). Among the Ache and Hiwi, older women did not contribute food that eased the deficits of middle-aged couples raising children, and about 10 percent of middle-aged Ache women lived with their own mothers (ext 2301). Agta adults named old age among the needs that decided whom they gave to in an experimental sharing game (ext 3665). The grandmother hypothesis (and Lieberman's active-grandparent corollary) holds that the long human life after the last birth (among 52 wild mammal species, longer than chance only in humans, orcas and short-finned pilot whales, ext 3722) was selected for and made possible by older adults remaining physically active to help rear children, grandchildren, and other young kin, increasing inclusive fitness through kin-selected provisioning. Older people are thus part of the alloparental supply: a band of several generations in which the old kept a place in the exchange and were no absent generation.

**Now (modern default):** Among US adults aged 25 or over with a living parent or adult child, 13.2% lived with a parent or adult child, 74.8% had the nearest one within 30 miles and 6.8% had the nearest one 500 miles or more away (ext 4204). In ten European countries 58 percent of grandmothers and 49 percent of grandfathers looked after a grandchild in the past year (Hank and Buber 2009, ext 4209).

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is between older people who keep a place in the daily giving and getting of a band of several generations (M9, M3) and the modern default of grandparents who live apart from it.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Older relatives present and inside the family's daily giving and getting: giving care, food or knowledge where they can, and being provided for. NOT mere grandparental existence or living-relative count. A surviving grandmother several time zones away does not fill the place a band of several generations gave her.

**Serves:** M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting  ·  evidence links: 23


### Touch & contact

#### Affective (C-tactile) contact frequency and skin-to-skin time
`EEA-TOUCH-01 · Touch & contact` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral infants lived in near-continuous bodily contact: carried on the body, fed at short intervals, and held skin-to-skin for much of the day (ext 2082), with frequent touch from the mother and a wider circle of caregivers. Field argues that the skin is the first and largest sense organ and that the fetus is already continuously stimulated in utero (ext 296). Skin-to-skin/carried contact was the species-typical default, not an intervention.

**Now (modern default):** In the United States of the late 1970s infants spent little time in body contact with caregivers and their movements were restricted by playpens, high chairs or cribs (ext 3145), and Euro-American infants were held less than Aka and Ngandu infants and were more often alone (ext 3151); personal-space norms and screen-mediated interaction strip casual affective touch from daily adult life.

**The gap to work with:** Touch deprivation produces growth hormone suppression in rat pups and immune breakdown in infant monkeys (ext 3508, 294); even in non-extreme modern life the loss of affective touch, firm and gentle, removes a direct oxytocin/vagal/cortisol-regulating input. Critically, affective touch is the one ancestral channel that remote, volumetric, or screen-based technology cannot deliver: it requires physical skin contact.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliable supply of skin-to-skin, affective contact, especially in infancy and under stress, and NOT a raw count of any contact. Two kinds of touch carry it: firm, moderate pressure is what helped in the preterm massage studies (ext 293), and slow, gentle stroking is what C-tactile fibres respond to, firing most at stroking speeds of 1 to 10 cm per second that people rate the most pleasant (ext 2147, 2689).

**Serves:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding, M9 - Care / Alloparenting  ·  evidence links: 21

#### Co-sleeping
`EEA-TOUCH-02 · Touch & contact` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** In every one of the 173 societies coded in the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, the mother slept in the same room as her infant, and in at least 76 of them in the same bed (ext 705); the sample spans foragers, farmers, herders and others, not foragers alone. Nighttime proximity meant the physiological co-regulation channels operated across the 24-hour cycle, not just during waking.

**Now (modern default):** WEIRD norms favor solitary infant sleep in a separate crib or room.

**The gap to work with:** Solitary infant sleep removes the nighttime proximity of the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample, where the mother slept in the same room as her infant in every one of the 173 societies coded, and in the same bed in at least 76 (ext 705). It removes the nighttime physiological co-regulation measured in cosleeping pairs, where mother and infant induce arousals in one another within seconds (ext 615). The evidence for this co-regulation comes from one lab (McKenna 2014, ext 615, 616).

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliable presence of nighttime body contact / proximity that preserves the touch and co-regulation channel through the sleep period; the relevant variable is continuity of contact across the 24-hour cycle, not sleep duration.

**Serves:** M15 - Touch, M3 - Social Bonding, M7 - Circadian Regulation  ·  evidence links: 10

#### Affective grooming/touch from trusted others (adult social touch)
`EEA-TOUCH-03 · Touch & contact` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Beyond infancy, ancestral adults maintained social bonds through affiliative body contact: the human homolog of primate grooming. Primate grooming is fundamentally about the intimacy of massage, where skin stimulation triggers endorphin release and underwrites social bonding.

**Now (modern default):** Adult-to-adult affiliative touch is sharply curtailed in WEIRD societies by personal-space norms, professionalization of touch, and screen-mediated relationships, and remote communication carries none of it.

**The gap to work with:** In 67 women, a neck and shoulder massage from the partner before a stress test went with a smaller rise in cortisol and heart rate than being alone, and verbal support alone was no better than being alone (ext 429). The benefit is bidirectional (ext 430). This channel is physical-contact-only and cannot be delivered by remote or volumetric technology.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliable supply of affiliative touch from trusted others (the grooming homolog), present as an ongoing relational channel; not one-off or transactional touch, and not mere co-presence without contact.

### Circadian & sensory

#### Dawn/dusk light gradient with genuine nocturnal darkness as circadian entrainment signal
`EEA-CIRC-01 · Circadian & sensory` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Across evolutionary history the human circadian pacemaker (suprachiasmatic nucleus) was entrained by a high-amplitude natural light-dark cycle: bright daytime illumination, a dusk dimming, and then real darkness for the duration of the biological night, with only firelight (low) as evening illumination. In wrist-sensor recordings of Hadza, San and Tsimane adults, no group went to sleep near sunset: sleep began on average 3.3 hours after sunset (ext 2224). The operative signal is the timing and spectral content of light at the day-night transition.

**Now (modern default):** Indoor electric and screen light keeps evening illumination bright and short-wavelength (blue-enriched, ~450nm) well past dusk, suppressing melatonin and delaying its onset by 1.5-3 hours; daytime indoor light is far dimmer than outdoor light, flattening the day-night amplitude.

**The gap to work with:** The modern light environment inverts the ancestral pattern on both ends: too little bright light by day, too much blue-enriched light at night. This delays circadian phase, compresses the biological night, and decouples internal time from external solar time.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Spectral timing of light exposure at the day-night transition (dusk dimming + nocturnal darkness).

**Serves:** M7 - Circadian Regulation  ·  evidence links: 17

#### Sleep timing/phase alignment to the biological night (not a fixed duration target)
`EEA-CIRC-02 · Circadian & sensory` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestrally, sleep occurred within a phase relationship to the solar/circadian night: onset about three hours after sunset (3.3 hours on average among Hadza, San and Tsimane, ext 2224; about three hours in rural Haiti without electricity, ext 3903), offset near or before dawn, with the sleep episode anchored to internal circadian time. Field data from three preindustrial societies (Hadza, San, Tsimane) place habitual sleep within a window in the rough range of ~6-7 hours of nocturnal sleep; subsistence horticulturalists on Tanna, Vanuatu, slept about 7.4 to 7.9 hours (ext 2813). The ancestral feature that counts is the alignment of the sleep episode to circadian phase; the hour count differed between groups.

**Now (modern default):** Industrial schedules set wake times by clock and alarm rather than by internal time, producing chronic 'social jetlag' - a standing offset between the endogenous circadian phase and the socially imposed sleep window. In a large Central-European dataset ~69% experience >=1 hour of social jetlag and about one-third >=2 hours; ~80% of workers wake by alarm; population chronotype has drifted later while workday sleep has shortened.

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is primarily one of phase, not amount. Independently of sleep duration, misalignment between biological and social clocks is associated with metabolic harm (e.g. social jetlag raises overweight odds and predicts BMI in the overweight, with an effect more than half that of sleep duration, after controlling for duration itself). Across 43 studies of 231,648 people, social jetlag went with higher body mass index and 1.2 times the odds of overweight (Arab et al. 2024), and in the high-quality studies of a review of 68 with higher body mass index, waist, systolic blood pressure and HbA1c (Bouman et al. 2023). In 60,977 UK adults, regular sleep and wake times predicted lower mortality more strongly than sleep duration did (Windred et al. 2024). Chasing a duration number while ignoring phase misses the operative variable.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Sleep timing/phase - the alignment of the sleep episode to internal circadian night - NOT sleep duration.

**Serves:** M7 - Circadian Regulation  ·  evidence links: 22

#### Sleep in the nightly period of falling temperature
`EEA-CIRC-03 · Circadian & sensory` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** In Hadza, San and Tsimane, the sleep period consistently occurred during the nighttime period of falling environmental temperature and ended near the nadir of daily ambient temperature (ext 745). Yetish and colleagues read it this way: the daily cycle of temperature change, largely eliminated from modern sleep environments, may be a potent natural regulator of sleep (Yetish et al. 2015, ext 745).

**Now (modern default):** Climate-controlled indoor environments hold ambient temperature roughly constant across the 24-hour cycle, removing the nighttime period of falling environmental temperature (ext 745).

**The gap to work with:** Modern sleep environments have largely eliminated the daily cycle of temperature change (Yetish et al. 2015, ext 745). In Hadza, San and Tsimane the sleep period consistently occurred during the nighttime period of falling environmental temperature (ext 745).

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Direction and timing of the nighttime ambient-temperature change (falling during the sleep period, ext 745), not absolute thermostat setpoint or daytime comfort.

### Subsistence & time-use

#### Daily incidental movement load
`EEA-SUBS-01 · Subsistence & time-use` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Across a foraging day, adults covered substantial ground on foot as a structural side-effect of acquiring food: Hadza men walk about 11.4 km a day and women about 5.8 km (Pontzer et al. 2012, GPS; ext 2430), and the women also dig for several hours (Lieberman 2021, ext 196). Activity was modest in intensity and embedded: light chores, sit-to-stand transitions (~50 a day, similar to ~38 for European adults; ext 289), and active resting postures (squatting, kneeling) that sustain low-level lower-limb muscle activity. Hunter-gatherer PAL is only modestly above a Western desk worker's (~1.8-1.9 vs ~1.6), so the EEA baseline is not 'extreme exertion'. It is 'movement is the unavoidable medium of subsistence.'

**Now (modern default):** Movement has been extracted from subsistence and re-inserted, if at all, as discretionary 'exercise': set-aside, planned, dose-prescribed (e.g. 150 min/week guidelines), and separated from the rest of a chair-bound, motor-transported, indoor day. Hadza women took about twice and Hadza men about three times the daily steps of smartphone users aged 18 to 75 in a 111-nation sample from middle- and high-income countries (ext 3418).

**The gap to work with:** The gap is not a calorie or PAL gap (these are surprisingly close). Hadza adults spent 134.92 minutes a day in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (ext 2233). Average sedentary bout duration is similar in Hadza and industrialized adults (~15 min), and Hadza spend about 18% of nonambulatory time squatting and 12.5% kneeling; squatting elicits higher muscle activity than chair-sitting (ext 289). Ancestral movement was obligatory; modern movement is optional, bunched, and easily skipped. The mismatch is in HOW the day's muscle activity is spread, not in gross energy turnover.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Incidental all-day movement woven into the fabric of living (errands, carrying, standing, walking between activities, active rest postures) - NOT a set-aside 'exercise' session. The functional target is the continuous distribution of low-level activity across waking hours, not weekly minutes of vigorous training.

**Serves:** M10 - Movement  ·  evidence links: 13

#### Effort-to-outcome contingency (closed-loop, self-attributable)
`EEA-SUBS-02 · Subsistence & time-use` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Van Vugt et al. (2024) argue that psychological adaptations for work evolved in small networks of hunter-gatherers where effort-outcome links were visible and physical (ext 583). In animals, the vmPFC detects response-outcome contingency and, when contingency is present, inhibits the dorsal-raphe passivity default, and experiences of control immunize against later helplessness (ext 66, 68, 69).

**Now (modern default):** In 13 European cohort studies, 15% of 197,473 employed men and women reported job strain (high demands with low control) (ext 817).

**The gap to work with:** Because passivity, in animals, is the unlearned default that only switches OFF when controllability is detected, environments that obscure the effort-outcome link don't feel neutral - they default the organism toward helplessness-type physiology. The gap is the loss of routinely detectable, self-attributable contingency, which population health data link to elevated disease risk (low job control predicts coronary disease independent of grade).

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reliably PRESENT and DETECTABLE response-outcome contingency that the actor can attribute to their own effort - not mere busyness, throughput, or nominal 'autonomy.' What must be restored is the closed, legible loop between action and observed result, not the quantity of work.

#### Task diversity within the subsistence day
`EEA-SUBS-03 · Subsistence & time-use` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** The evolved exploratory-pursuit substrate (mesolimbic-dopamine SEEKING) is built for persistent inquisitive engagement with a varied environment, not for indefinitely repeating one narrow operation.

**Now (modern default):** Industrial and much digital work narrows the day to repetitive, specialized, often single-task operation - high repetition, low task variety, minimal novel problem-solving - with the SEEKING system left chronically under-stimulated by the work itself and frequently redirected onto engineered supernormal substitutes (feeds, games, variable-ratio rewards) off the clock.

**The gap to work with:** The gap is the collapse from a high-variety, skill-diverse subsistence portfolio to narrow repetitive specialization, under-engaging the exploratory-pursuit system whose canonical evolved function is varied engagement with a changing world. No study in Cor measures forager task diversity.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Genuine variety of self-directed, skill-recruiting tasks across the day that engage exploration and problem-solving - not nominal 'multitasking' (rapid switching among equally shallow operations) and not enrichment proxies bolted on outside the core work.

#### Daily subsistence work-hours
`EEA-SUBS-04 · Subsistence & time-use` - **tested once**

**Ancestrally:** No single daily work-hour figure is supported. The 'original affluent society' position (Sahlins, ext 714) reads the data as short food-quest hours and ample leisure. ### Diet & metabolic

#### Food diversity and seasonality (whole-food, micronutrient-dense, omnivorous variety)
`EEA-DIET-01 · Diet & metabolic` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral intake came from a wide, seasonally rotating array of wild plant and animal foods eaten in their whole, minimally altered form. Diet composition shifted with geography and season (ext 763); Eaton and Konner argue that it also shifted with period and kept the broad versatility typical of an omnivore (ext 38). Macronutrient and micronutrient profiles diverged sharply from the modern Western diet in nearly every measurable dimension at once: higher protein share, dramatically less refined carbohydrate and added sugar, lower sodium, higher potassium, higher micronutrient density, and near-zero industrially processed food.

**Now (modern default):** Seasonality is erased by global supply chains; micronutrient density falls while energy density rises; in US adults, ultra-processed food rose from 53.5% to 57.0% of daily calories between 2001-2002 and 2017-2018 (ext 4851).

**The gap to work with:** The modern food environment collapses a seasonally varied, micronutrient-dense, whole-food omnivory into a year-round monotony of refined, energy-dense, nutrient-diluted products, removing both the variety and the whole-food matrix the metabolic and digestive systems were calibrated to.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Whole-food matrix and dietary variety, not any single 'good' or 'bad' nutrient: the mismatch is in the wholesale shift to refined, processed forms across the whole diet, not in one macronutrient ratio.

**Serves:** M13 - Energy Regulation  ·  evidence links: 10

#### Satiety-honest fiber and whole-food matrix (food that signals fullness truthfully)
`EEA-DIET-02 · Diet & metabolic` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Ancestral foods were fibrous, so the volume and macronutrient signals reaching satiety circuits tracked the actual caloric load. Reconstructions place ancestral fiber intake far above modern levels (on the order of 100 g/day or somewhat more, Eaton 2006, ext 3605, vs ~20 g today, Eaton and Konner 1985, ext 39). Sweetness, fat, and salt arrived bundled inside a whole-food matrix, so the reward-system's responses to those cues were honest proxies for nutritional value. For the Hadza, honey is a favourite food and a substantial calorie share (Marlowe et al. 2014, ext 730).

**Now (modern default):** Ultra-processed, hyperpalatable products engineered to the 'bliss point', the mathematically optimized concentration of sugar, fat, and salt that maximizes craving. These foods decouple palatability from nutritional value and deliver calories faster, so the evolved fullness/reward proxies no longer track intake. Ultra-processed hyperpalatability is the modern proxy that defeats satiety.

**The gap to work with:** Industrial formulation deliberately separates the reward cues (sweet/fat/salt) from the satiety cues (fiber, bulk, slow digestion) the two were ancestrally bundled together, so modern hyperpalatable food drives consumption past the point the body would otherwise stop, defeating the honest-signal logic of appetite regulation.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Satiety-honesty of food (does palatability track nutritional/caloric load?), not calorie count alone: the failure is the engineered decoupling of reward from fullness, not merely high energy density.

**Serves:** M13 - Energy Regulation, M2 - Pursuit / Exploration  ·  evidence links: 10

#### Acquisition effort (calories are physically worked for)
`EEA-DIET-03 · Diet & metabolic` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Food had to be physically acquired: walked to, foraged, dug, hunted, and processed. Among the Hadza, GPS-measured daily walking distances average about 11.4 km for men and 5.8 km for women (Pontzer et al. 2012, 30 adults over 11 days) and, in a larger sample of 179 people over 2,078 person-days, 12.9 km for men and 7.6 km for women (Wood et al. 2021, ext 3418), and women also spend several hours digging. This embeds substantial obligate movement and a real energetic and time cost between hunger and calories. Note: the absolute activity premium over sedentary moderns is modest (hunter-gatherer PAL ~1.8-1.9 vs ~1.6 for desk workers, ~15% higher), so the baseline is 'embedded all-day effort,' not extreme athleticism.

**Now (modern default):** Calories require essentially zero acquisition effort: hyperpalatable, energy-dense food is available on demand, pre-processed, with no movement, foraging, or processing cost between impulse and ingestion. The effort gate that historically bounded intake is gone.

**The gap to work with:** Removing the work between hunger and food eliminates a natural brake on consumption and decouples eating from the obligate daily movement it was bundled with - calories are now frictionless to obtain and detached from the physical activity that ancestrally accompanied acquisition.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Incidental, embedded, all-day acquisition movement (walking, foraging, carrying, digging) - NOT 'exercise' as a discrete bout. The mismatch is the loss of effort woven through daily food-getting, not a missing gym session. Work hours: see EEA-SUBS-04.

**Serves:** M10 - Movement, M13 - Energy Regulation  ·  evidence links: 7

#### Energy regulation is intake-bounded
`EEA-DIET-04 · Diet & metabolic` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Total daily energy expenditure (TEE) increases with physical activity at low activity levels but plateaus at higher levels, after adjusting for body size and composition (ext 283). The authors' model has the body adapt to maintain TEE within a narrow range (ext 283). Pontzer and colleagues hypothesize that human daily energy expenditure may be an evolved physiological trait (ext 287), and write that population comparisons suggest it is an evolved, species-specific trait (ext 284). Hunter-gatherers and subsistence populations expend about the same total daily energy as industrialized adults of the same body size despite substantial differences in physical activity; the same wild-vs-captive constancy appears across mammals. Ancestrally, the binding constraint on energy balance was therefore food INTAKE (how much could be acquired and its composition), not how many calories activity could burn off.

**Now (modern default):** The lay model treats body weight as 'calories in minus calories out' with exercise as the lever for the 'out' side. Total expenditure rises with activity at low activity levels and plateaus at higher levels (ext 283): above moderate activity, each added ~100 counts per minute per day went with less than 50 kcal/day more expenditure. Above moderate activity this model fails: cranking up activity adds little to expenditure, while the intake side, driven by hyperpalatable, energy-dense, effortless food, is what has changed and what dysregulates energy balance.

**The gap to work with:** Modern environments leave intake unbounded (cheap, hyperpalatable, effortless calories) while the body's expenditure stays homeostatically capped, so the one variable the system relied on as the binding constraint (limited, worked-for food) is exactly the one industrialization removed, and the popular 'just exercise it off' remedy targets the wrong, buffered side. Total energy expenditure increases with physical activity at low activity levels but plateaus at higher activity levels (ext 283).

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** The binding constraint on energy balance: ancestrally INTAKE (food availability/composition), not expenditure. The mismatch is unbounded intake, not insufficient calorie-burning; exercise is a weak proxy for the metabolic problem.

### Threat & safety

#### Threat temporality (acute, episodic, escapable-by-action)
`EEA-THREAT-01 · Threat & safety` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Cacioppo and Patrick write that short, life-or-death confrontations drove the evolution of 'fight or flight' (ext 150). The fight-or-flight architecture was selected to spike hard and briefly, then terminate. Threat exposure was thus pulsatile, not continuous.

**Now (modern default):** Modern stressors are chronic and non-terminating: the overbearing boss, the long commute, financial precarity, abstract status anxiety, always-on connectivity. The same stressor persists hour after hour, year after year, while the sympathetic/parasympathetic architecture is unchanged. The threat system is engaged at a low chronic duty cycle it was never built to sustain, and the beneficial acute response becomes damaging on the chronic timescale.

**The gap to work with:** The mismatch is in the time-course, not the threat machinery itself. The system is calibrated for brief, resolvable spikes; modern life supplies sustained, unresolved activation. Acute stress is adaptive (even enhances immunity); the identical response sustained chronically suppresses immunity and accumulates wear-and-tear. The gap is the absence of an episode boundary that lets the response shut down.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Calibrate to threat TEMPORALITY (episode duration and duty cycle with a terminating standdown), not threat magnitude or count. A single intense-but-resolved spike is ancestral-normal; a mild-but-unending engagement is the mismatch. The unit is 'does the activation episode end and the system return to baseline,' not 'how scary was it.'

#### Threat controllability and predictability (resolvable by own action)
`EEA-THREAT-02 · Threat & safety` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** The threat system evolved on the expectation that vigilance would be followed by an effective response that resolves the danger.

**Now (modern default):** Many modern stressors are structurally uncontrollable and unpredictable: algorithmic management and electronic monitoring strip decision latitude, outcomes hinge on systems no single action can move, and dread is open-ended (an unknown deadline, an unbounded threat). No behavior reliably terminates the stressor, producing the conditions for learned helplessness and chronic anticipatory dread.

**The gap to work with:** The harm comes from uncontrollability and unpredictability, the absence of an action that resolves the danger, more than from threat itself. In a lab study of 32 adults waiting for shocks whose delay was shown in advance, nine preferred more voltage sooner to less voltage later (ext 4397), and Maier and Seligman write that learned helplessness in the laboratory, combining animal and human results, produced eight of the nine symptoms the DSM lists for depression (ext 442). Modern environments systematically supply unresolvable, action-proof stressors - the mismatch.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Calibrate to CONTROLLABILITY = whether a behavioral response reliably terminates or averts the threat, not to mere familiarity or low intensity. A predictable-but-uncontrollable stressor (you see it coming, nothing you do helps) is the toxic case; an unfamiliar-but-action-resolvable one is ancestral-normal.

**Serves:** M1 - Threat Management, M6 - Controllability / Agency  ·  evidence links: 6

#### Real-vs-perceived threat ratio and all-clear resolution
`EEA-THREAT-03 · Threat & safety` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** The smoke-detector logic produces many false alarms (a 1000:1 cost asymmetry makes ~999 false alarms per real escape optimal).

**Now (modern default):** The smoke-detector system keeps firing on cues it cannot resolve; social all-clear is attenuated by isolation. Activation that should be transient becomes chronic, shifting the body budget toward sustained inflammation and allostatic load.

**The gap to work with:** Chronically unbalanced body budgeting and unbridled inflammation are implicated in chronic stress, in persistent anxious and low states (what Western scales score as anxiety and depression), and in physical disease (Barrett; McEwen allostatic load). The mismatch is the missing terminating action and missing social all-clear, not the false-alarm rate itself.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Calibrate to RESOLVABILITY: a present threat that a behavioral all-clear (and a social all-clear via proximity/touch/reunion) can terminate - NOT bare threat count or alarm frequency. The ancestral system tolerated abundant false alarms; what it did not tolerate, and what modernity supplies, is alarms with no all-clear. The unit is 'can this activation be closed,' not 'how many alarms occur.'

#### Killing between groups
`EEA-THREAT-04 · Threat & safety` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Lethal raids between groups occurred among foragers, and in some populations they were frequent. Killing between groups was part of the ancestral setting. Bowles compiled 23 sources and found war deaths averaged 14% of all deaths (archaeological 12%, ethnographic 16%; single sites 0% to 46%), in samples that include sedentary and contact-period groups, so his 14% is no forager default. Fry and Soderberg found that in 21 mobile forager band societies most of 148 recorded killings had one killer and one victim; about a third were between groups, but 38 of those 50 came from one society, the Tiwi, and in the other 20 societies about 15% were. A phylogenetic estimate puts all killing by other humans at about 2% of deaths.

**Now (modern default):** Killing between groups is done by states with armies.

**The gap to work with:** The two headline numbers measure different things (share of all deaths in war, share of killings between groups) and cannot be averaged. A page can say lethal raids between groups occurred and were sometimes frequent; it cannot give one ancestral percentage.

**Serves:** M1 - Threat Management, M11 - Cooperation / Alliance  ·  evidence links: 9


### Information environment

#### Signal provenance and accountability (information from known, reputation-bearing, face-to-face sources)
`EEA-INFO-01 · Information environment` - **argued, not yet tested**

**Ancestrally:** Barkow lists mass communication and media and the ubiquity of strangers among the features of modern industrial societies that could not have existed in the Pleistocene (ext 2648). Boehm's ethnography of forager gossip describes a band that effectively keeps a running 'dossier' on every individual - provenance was the substrate of trust.

**Now (modern default):** The shift Tomasello describes - from face-to-face 'I-you' joint intentionality to impersonal 'we-as-group' collective intentionality - is taken to an extreme by mass and digital media, where signals are detached from any reputation-bearing source the receiver can sanction or even locate.

**The gap to work with:** The evolved machinery for weighing information by source reputation and face-to-face accountability is deprived of the provenance cues it was calibrated to use. Receivers must evaluate high-stakes claims from sources they cannot place in any reputational web, and senders bear little reputational cost for deception - removing the gossip/ostracism feedback loop that ancestrally policed signal honesty.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Proportion of consequential information arriving from a known, reputation-bearing, accountable source the receiver can locate and sanction - NOT raw access to information or number of contacts. A person can be flooded with information yet have almost none of it carry verifiable provenance.

#### Gossip and narrative flow as reputation and norm transmission
`EEA-INFO-02 · Information environment` - **tested many times**

**Ancestrally:** Among the Ju/'hoansi, daytime talk was mostly about economic matters, complaints and gossip, while firelight talk at night was mostly stories (ext 720). Gossip and narrative are talk about who behaved how, who is reliable or stingy, what happened, and what one ought to do. This stream did double duty - it transmitted norms, calibrated each person's reputation in the eyes of the group, and deterred free-riding because everyone knew gossip was constantly underway and anyone could become its subject. Boehm documents gossip, ridicule, and ostracism as enforcement against free-riders (bullies, the lazy, feigners, secret meat-eaters); Henrich notes that norm violations meant 'reputational damage, gossip, and a consequent loss of marriage opportunities and allies.'

**Now (modern default):** The tight loop in which gossip simultaneously informed, judged, and deterred within a single accountable group is broken: reputational narrative is broadcast without the reciprocal accountability that made it self-policing.

**The gap to work with:** The gossip/narrative channel that ancestrally fused information transmission with reputation tracking and norm enforcement is decoupled. People consume reputational narrative about strangers they cannot sanction, and the norm-deterrence function (knowing the group is watching and will talk) is weakened in anonymous or one-to-many settings - leaving the evolved appetite for reputational narrative engaged but its enforcement payload disabled.

**What actually has to be present (resolution unit):** Reputation/norm narrative circulating within a bounded group where subjects and audience are mutually known and can reciprocally sanction - NOT raw volume of social content consumed. Doomscrolling celebrity gossip is high-volume but supplies almost no actionable, reciprocal reputational signal.

**Serves:** M11 - Cooperation / Alliance, M5 - Status Monitoring  ·  evidence links: 10

## About this framework

Human systems signal when their conditions are not met, and modern environments withhold those conditions at scale. Preferences are mechanism outputs, not ground truth. The load-bearing claim of every proxy gradient: the cue is identical at every level; only the resolving function changes, so a proxy can fire the cue without supplying what actually resolves it.
