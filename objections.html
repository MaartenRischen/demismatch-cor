Work is built on top of Cor, so the strongest attacks on its whole approach belong on the record, next to the claims they hit. Each entry below states the attack at full strength, in the attacker's own words, with the source. It names the parts of the atlas the attack hits, says where Cor stands, and gives a verdict.

The verdicts mean this. Answered: Cor holds evidence that meets the attack. Partly answered: some of it is met and the rest is open. Open: Cor does not hold the evidence to meet it, and the entry says what is missing. Conceded: the attack is right, and the entry says what follows. An attack whose source could not be read in full gets no verdict. Recording an objection changes no position in the atlas. Answers cite only records that are in Cor; replies published by others are attributed to their authors.

Evolutionary psychology

Attacks on the evolutionary approach itself.

These target evolutionary psychology as a field. Each one reaches Cor because Cor reads human signals against an ancestral baseline (P2, P3).

OBJ-01 The matching problem: nobody can show today's mechanisms are the ancestral ones

Verdict: Partly answered

The attack

To explain a present-day mental mechanism by evolution, you have to show three things: that it matches a mechanism selected for in the ancestral environment, that it descends from that mechanism, and that it still does the same job. The tools biologists use for this (experiments, comparison with related species, fossils, optimality models) are mostly unavailable for human psychology. Smith concludes that evolutionary psychology as it stands is impossible. Maung applies the same argument to evolutionary psychiatry, mismatch accounts included: the data cannot decide whether a disorder is a breakdown, a mismatch, a persisting adaptation, a by-product, or simply a product of development in a modern setting, and reading ancestral function off modern symptoms is circular.

"Evolutionary psychological claims, therefore, fail unless practitioners can show that mental structures underpinning present-day behaviors are structures that evolved in prehistory for the performance of adaptive tasks that it is still their function to perform. This is the matching problem."

Subrena E. Smith (2020), "Why Evolutionary Psychology (Probably) Isn't Possible", This View of Life, source, paragraph 9. Not a Cor record: a web essay with no DOI. It restates the argument of Smith's paper (Biological Theory, 2020, ext 1067), which is closed access and is in Cor from its abstract only.

"If there is no fact of the matter about how psychological mechanisms are to be individuated, then there is no fact of the matter about how they are to be matched."

Subrena E. Smith (2020), "Why Evolutionary Psychology (Probably) Isn't Possible", This View of Life, source, paragraph 11. Not a Cor record: as above.

"However, for many mental disorders, evolutionary psychiatry lacks the methodological resources to demonstrate these conditions. Therefore, many evolutionary psychiatry hypotheses are empirically untestable and remain indefinitely underdetermined by data."

Maung HH (2024), The matching problem for evolutionary psychiatry, doi:10.1080/09515089.2024.2354437, Abstract, ext 1077

"This results in circular reasoning, whereby the properties of modern minds are assumed to have also been possessed by ancient minds and then these assumed properties of ancient minds are invoked to explain the properties of modern minds, without any independent evidence that ancient minds actually possessed these properties"

Maung HH (2024), The matching problem for evolutionary psychiatry, doi:10.1080/09515089.2024.2354437, Underdetermination of theory by data, paragraph 7, ext 1079

Where it hits

P2 P3 DA1 OF2 C2 C3 C13

Where Cor stands

For the old affective systems the attack is met the way it is met in animal biology: the subcortical systems behind fear, seeking, separation distress, care, play and lust are conserved across mammals (C4, DA8, Jaak Panksepp and Lucy Biven 2012, ext 8). Cor's mismatch claim then needs only that the cues a mechanism responds to still vary today, so its response can be tested now, which is the move Egeland and colleagues make in reply (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1069).

Maung's rivals can be tested. A breakdown, in Cor, is a clear physical defect, found or not yet found, and the case is a disease (DA1). A response fixed by early history should change little when present conditions change, and in adults it changed. Distress rose with job loss and was gone after re-employment, and earlier mood did not predict who found work (Kessler RC et al. 1989, ext 4659); in 15 of 18 longitudinal studies, returning to work went with better health (Rueda S et al. 2012, ext 1710). One sibling study stands on the side of rearing: adopted-away siblings of a parent diagnosed with depression were diagnosed with it 23 percent less often (Kendler KS et al. 2020, ext 2878).

Smith's individuation point applies to every functional taxonomy in biology. Each mismatch reading is tested on its own resolution condition (P2), so no test depends on the count of fifteen.

One part stays open. Cooperation and norm enforcement (M11) is human-specific, its convergence has no foundational parent (C11), and Cor holds no comparative case for its descent.

Replies published by others

Egeland, Kennair and Kleppestø (2026) reply that the routes biologists use to support homology in animals are open to evolutionary psychology too, so Smith's bar would also sink animal behaviour research (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1068); that a mechanism needs continuity only in its own input conditions (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1069); and that the amygdala and disgust meet the standard Smith accepts for the corneal reflex (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1070). They also concede that the evidence leaves competing hypotheses underdetermined (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1071). Their replies do not address individuation, and they postdate Maung without answering him separately. Hagen (2020) replied to Smith on This View of Life; that reply is not yet read for Cor.

What would settle it

Per mechanism: comparative evidence that its circuit is homologous across related species, which Cor holds for the conserved systems and needs for the human-specific ones. For mismatch readings of a diagnosis: the signal tracks current input and fades when the resolution condition is supplied, in adults too. Cor holds this for work (Kessler RC et al. 1989, ext 4659, Rueda S et al. 2012, ext 1710) and needs it mechanism by mechanism.

On record in Cor: G10 (only 5 of 15 mechanisms forced), G6, falsifier 1 on the research programme page.

OBJ-02 Distinctively human thinking is learned from culture, not inherited

Verdict: Partly answered

The attack

Heyes argues that the mechanisms that make human thinking distinctive (imitation, mindreading, language, selective social learning) get their design from cultural evolution. Each person builds them during development with general learning abilities, from information other people supply, the way literacy is built. On this view humans are not stuck with Stone Age minds: human nature changes over historical time.

"Cognitive Gadgets opens up a third way. It suggests that distinctively human cognitive mechanisms are adaptive because they are shaped primarily, not by nature or nurture, but by culture."

Heyes C (2019), Précis of Cognitive Gadgets: The Cultural Evolution of Thinking, doi:10.1017/S0140525X18002145, Section 9, final paragraph, ext 1073

"On a broader scale, cultural evolutionary psychology implies that human minds are more agile, but also more fragile, than was previously thought. We are not stuck in the Pleistocene past with Stone Age minds"

Heyes C (2019), Précis of Cognitive Gadgets: The Cultural Evolution of Thinking, doi:10.1017/S0140525X18002145, Section 9, 'Human nature', ext 1074

Where it hits

P2 DA7 M11 M4

Where Cor stands

Heyes limits her own attack. She leaves emotion, motivation, social motivation and attention to genetic evolution, as a starter kit shared in large part with other apes (Heyes C 2019, ext 1075). Cor's mechanisms are motivational-emotional systems, the part she places in that kit, and Cor holds that a matched setting includes cultural scaffolding (P3, Henrich and Joseph 2016, ext 120). Play (M4) is one of the systems conserved across mammals (C4); the rules children learn through it are learned, as Cor says.

The piece inside her target is cheater detection, which Cor names in C11. Cor holds three tests of it. Nonliterate Shiwiar villagers caught cheaters as often as Harvard students (Sugiyama LS et al. 2002, ext 2797). With one rule held constant, detection was 68 percent when the violators were possible cheaters and 27 percent when a breach was an innocent mistake (Cosmides L et al. 2010, ext 2996). A patient with limbic damage solved 38.9 percent of social-contract problems against 70 percent of matched precaution problems (Stone VE et al. 2002, ext 4877). The skill is specialised and appears without schooling. Open: these tests do not tell an inherited skill from one every human setting teaches, and Heyes's own test would (Heyes C 2019, ext 1076).

Replies published by others

Egeland and colleagues (2026) grant that learned design, with reading as the case, shows design can appear without inherited homology (Egeland J et al. 2026, ext 1071). The open peer commentaries on Heyes's précis in Behavioral and Brain Sciences are not yet read for Cor.

What would settle it

Heyes's own test (Heyes C 2019, ext 1076): compare what a mechanism can do with how much usable information the developmental setting supplies. Applied to cheater detection and norm enforcement in M11, too little information points to inherited design; enough points to learning or culture.

On record in Cor: C11 (no foundational parent), G7.

OBJ-03 Development builds the mind, so there are no pre-set programs to mismatch

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Genes cannot specify brain wiring in detail. Development, epigenetic marks, learning and the physical and social setting build the mind in each lifetime, and separating causes into fixed genes and variable environment cannot be defended. Buller adds the sharpest version for a mismatch account: the same genes developing in a new environment can produce different psychological traits, so there is no reason to think every evolved trait is still the trait our ancestors had.

"From this viewpoint, the human mind does not consist of pre-specified programmes, but is built via a constant interplay between the individual and its environment"

Bolhuis JJ et al. (2011), Darwin in Mind: New Opportunities for Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001109, Reassessing the Major Tenets: Universalism, para 4, ext 1084

"Simply put, human capacities are neither genetically nor environmentally specified, but emerge within processes of development"

Narvaez D et al. (2022), Evolving evolutionary psychology, doi:10.1037/amp0000849, Pillar I: NEP and the Conceptual Agenda of Neo-Darwinism, ext 1088

"Any Pleistocene-selected genes we possess will interact with these new environments to produce psychological traits that may differ in important ways from those of our Pleistocene ancestors. So there is no good reason to think that all of our evolved psychological characteristics remain adapted to the lifestyle of Pleistocene hunter-gatherers."

Buller DJ (2009), Four Fallacies of Pop Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1038/scientificamerican0109-74, Fallacy 3, final paragraph, ext 1105

Where it hits

P2 DA7 DA10 C12

Where Cor stands

Most of this attack is aimed at a genetic determinism Cor does not hold. Cor's architecture calibrates to developmental input within evolved ranges (DA7, C12), with evidence from cross-fostering in rats that reverses epigenetic programming (Weaver ICG et al. 2004, ext 99) and from Bowlby on behavioural systems whose adult form depends on rearing (John Bowlby 1969, ext 13). Narvaez and colleagues' mammalian developmental niche, used as the baseline for species-typical development, is close to Cor's and is recorded as support (Narvaez D et al. 2022, ext 1092).

Buller's version asks whether the adult system built in a modern setting still answers its resolution condition. In adults it does. Distress that rose with job loss was gone after re-employment (Kessler RC et al. 1989, ext 4659), and in 15 of 18 longitudinal studies returning to work went with better health (Rueda S et al. 2012, ext 1710). When a charity paid off poor households' debts, the share with symptoms of generalised anxiety fell from 78 to 53 percent in three months (Ong Q et al. 2019, ext 1712). In rats, stress-driven shrinkage of hippocampal neurons reversed within days or weeks after the stress ended (McEwen et al. 2012, ext 129). Cor states the limit: deprivation far outside anything a child evolved for leaves a lasting mark (C12, Sonuga-Barke EJS et al. 2017, ext 2903).

Replies published by others

Egeland and colleagues (2026, Table 2) give the field's standard reply: evolutionary psychology holds that every mechanism develops through gene-environment interaction, and that the processes the critics name are not neglected.

What would settle it

Reversibility in adults raised in mismatched settings: when the resolution condition is supplied, does the signal fade? Cor holds this for work (Kessler RC et al. 1989, ext 4659, Rueda S et al. 2012, ext 1710), for an ended threat (Ong Q et al. 2019, ext 1712) and, in rats, for control (Maier SF and Seligman MEP 2016, ext 69). Cor needs it for its other mechanisms.

On record in Cor: The deprivation limit in DA7 and OF2; G6.

OBJ-04 Adaptation keeps up: recent selection and niche construction

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Human genetic evolution sped up in the last 80,000 years, and selection in many species can shift a trait within a few hundred years. Humans also build their own environments to suit their adaptations and fix self-made problems with culture. Together these predict much less lag between human design and the modern world than evolutionary psychology assumes, and they undercut the picture of a Stone Age mind in a modern world.

"If humans exhibit equivalent rates, then significant genetic evolution would occur over the course of a few hundred years. While fast evolution is far from inevitable, there is nonetheless strong evidence that it has frequently occurred in humans. EP has yet to come to terms with the possibility of recent, rapid genetic changes with their potential for associated neural rewiring."

Bolhuis JJ et al. (2011), Darwin in Mind: New Opportunities for Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001109, Reassessing the Major Tenets: The Environment of Evolutionary Adaptedness and Gradualism, para 3, ext 1081

"The rate of adaptive evolution in human populations has indeed accelerated within the past 80,000 years. The results above demonstrate the extent of acceleration: the recent rate must be one to two orders of magnitude higher than the long-term rate to explain the genomewide pattern."

Hawks J et al. (2007), Recent acceleration of human adaptive evolution, doi:10.1073/pnas.0707650104, Results: Population Growth, para 1, ext 1093

"While adaptiveness is far from guaranteed, from this theoretical perspective humans are expected to experience far less adaptive lag than anticipated by EP"

Bolhuis JJ et al. (2011), Darwin in Mind: New Opportunities for Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001109, Towards a New Science of the Evolution of the Mind, point (ii), ext 1083

"The idea that we are stuck with a Pleistocene-adapted psychology greatly underestimates the rate at which natural and sexual selection can drive evolutionary change."

Buller DJ (2009), Four Fallacies of Pop Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1038/scientificamerican0109-74, Fallacy 3, paragraph 6, ext 1104

Where it hits

P3 C3 DA8 M8 M13

Cor itself holds the claim this attacks: Eaton and Konner's statement that the human genome has changed little since agriculture (Eaton SB and Konner M 1985, ext 38).

Where Cor stands

Recent selection is real, and its clearest examples fall in Cor's own territory: disease resistance and diet (Hawks J et al. 2007, ext 1094), which bear on immune regulation (M8) and energy regulation (M13). Hawks and colleagues measure genes and say nothing about psychology (Hawks J et al. 2007, ext 1093); the step from their result to minds is Bolhuis and colleagues' inference (Bolhuis JJ et al. 2011, ext 1081).

Two facts limit the attack. Cor's readings for the affective systems rest on circuits conserved across mammals (C4), and selection since farming would have to rewrite function shared with rats and monkeys. And the settings Cor calls most mismatched are one to five generations old: electric light at night (Walker M 2017, ext 77), and feeds, which reached US colleges between 2004 and 2006 (Braghieri L et al. 2022, ext 2031). That is faster than the 25 generations Bolhuis and colleagues give for selection to shift a trait by one standard deviation (Bolhuis JJ et al. 2011, ext 1081). Niche construction cuts both ways: people build settings to suit themselves, and markets build settings that exploit unmet needs (DC2).

Replies published by others

None found in what was read. Egeland and colleagues (2026, Table 2) list recent Holocene selection under the human-nature objection and reply that the same species-typical mechanisms produce different behaviour in different ecologies.

What would settle it

Genome scans showing selection since farming on genes for the circuits Cor names (opioid, oxytocin, stress-axis, circadian) would weaken the conserved-system defence for those mechanisms. Cor needs a check of the scans for those genes. Per parameter: evidence that a modern setting has existed long enough, and pressed hard enough on fitness, for selection to act.

On record in Cor: Proposal only: a caveat on ext 38 pointing to ext 1093.

OBJ-05 There was no single ancestral environment

Verdict: Answered

The attack

The environment of evolutionary adaptedness is not one place or time. The Pleistocene climate swung and its pattern of swings kept changing; early humans lived in many habitats; different traits arose at different times, under different conditions; and later settled farming life may itself be part of human evolutionary history. Buller adds that the adaptive problems that shaped the mind are mostly unknowable, because the evidence about ancestral social life is lost.

"Reference to the chronological and phylogenetic context for the evolution of human behaviour shows that the EEA is by no means unitary. Different elements have different time depths, and by implication different mechanisms."

Foley RA (1996), An evolutionary and chronological framework for human social behaviour, ISBN 9780197261644, Section 'The EEA and the chronology of human social evolution', pp. 110-111, ext 1109

"Even if we consider the selection pressures that acted on ancestral human populations during the Pleistocene epoch (approximately 1.7 million to 10,000 years ago), the abstract concept of stable selection pressures in the EEA is challenged by recent evidence from paleoecology and paleoanthropology."

Bolhuis JJ et al. (2011), Darwin in Mind: New Opportunities for Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001109, Reassessing the Major Tenets: The Environment of Evolutionary Adaptedness and Gradualism, para 4, ext 1082

"these descriptions are purely speculative because we have little evidence of the conditions under which early human evolution occurred. The paleontological record provides a few clues about some aspects of early human life, but it is largely silent regarding the social interactions that would have been of principal importance in human psychological evolution."

Buller DJ (2009), Four Fallacies of Pop Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1038/scientificamerican0109-74, Fallacy 1, paragraph 3, ext 1103

Where it hits

P3 C3 DA8

Where Cor stands

Cor defines a matched setting as one within the range humans have lived in, culturally scaffolded, and never defined by an ancestral surface form (P3). Foley's point that different elements have different time depths fits that frame: each system's baseline is the period it was shaped in, and the old affective systems predate the Pleistocene (DA8, C4).

Buller says ancestral social life cannot be known. Cor's baseline rows rest on measured values. Across 32 forager societies the band the average person lived in held 28.2 adults (Hill KR et al. 2011, ext 2116). Across 263 forager societies the median residential group was 27 people (Marlowe FW 2005, ext 754). In all 45 small-scale groups in one review, food moved between households (Gurven M 2004, ext 1577). Living foragers are people living inside the range P3 names, so their values measure it directly. Cor's gap register says the criterion for which cultural settings count as matched is not yet operational (G7). Public copy that says "the EEA" in the singular overstates this: Cor's baseline is a range (P3).

Replies published by others

None found in what was read.

What would settle it

For each mechanism, a stated time depth and the observed range of its input conditions across that period, which is the sharper criterion G7 asks for.

On record in Cor: G7; Foley (1995) is in Cor from its opening paragraph only (Foley R 1995, ext 1111).

OBJ-06 There is no universal human nature to be mismatched

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Cultural variation shapes psychological traits, human populations differ genetically partly through recent selection, and so there is no uniform human genetic program. Buller argues that evolutionary psychology's doctrine of a universal human nature is one of the reasons its celebrated findings have not held up.

"From this perspective, cultural practices are likely to have influenced selection pressures on the human brain, raising the possibility that genetic variation could lead to biases in the human cognitive processing between, as well as within, populations. In summary, there is no uniform human genetic program."

Bolhuis JJ et al. (2011), Darwin in Mind: New Opportunities for Evolutionary Psychology, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001109, Reassessing the Major Tenets: Universalism, para 6, ext 1085

"Evolutionary Psychology’s failure to produce solid empirical discoveries, I suggest, stems from problems with its theoretical framework"

Buller DJ (2005), Evolutionary psychology: the emperor's new paradigm, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2005.04.003, Conclusion, p. 282, ext 1107

Where it hits

P2 DA10 M14 M5

Where Cor stands

Cor claims species-typical mechanisms whose settings vary between individuals within heritable ranges (DA10, C15), and it builds cultural scaffolding into what counts as matched (P3). It claims no uniform genetic programme, so genetic differences between populations (Bolhuis JJ et al. 2011, ext 1085) are heritable variation of the kind DA10 describes.

For the sex-differentiated claims in reproductive motivation (M14), the largest test Cor holds found the differences across cultures: in 14,399 people in 45 countries, the sex differences in stated mate preferences held (Walter KV et al. 2020, ext 695). National gender equality was not robustly related to them, except for the gap in partner age and one index for financial prospects (Walter KV et al. 2020, ext 999). Cor's baseline row records as contested whether stated preferences predict whom people like once they meet (Eastwick PW et al. 2014, ext 757), and Cor makes no claim about a sex difference in jealousy (Harris CR 2003, ext 758).

Replies published by others

Egeland and colleagues (2026, Table 2) reply that many universal adaptations remain under cultural variation and that the same mechanisms produce different behaviour in different ecologies.

What would settle it

Cross-cultural tests of the specific M14 claims in small-scale societies, with behaviour measured directly, and studies that link the female and male neural substrates to these preferences, which G2 lists as missing.

On record in Cor: G2; the earlier challenge records Zentner M and Mitura K 2012, ext 1009 and Wood W and Eagly AH 2002, ext 805 are met by the 45-country test (Walter KV et al. 2020, ext 695, Walter KV et al. 2020, ext 999).

OBJ-07 Just-so stories: an adaptive story is always available, so the account cannot fail

Verdict: Partly answered

The attack

Gould and Lewontin argue that adaptive explanations cannot be refuted in practice: when one story fails, another replaces it, and plausibility is taken as proof. A good fit between organism and environment does not show selection made it; drift, by-products of development and later reuse of parts can all produce fit. They would object far less if an adaptive explanation could be rejected after failing an explicit test.

"Since the range of adaptive stories is as wide as our minds are fertile, new stories can always be postulated."

Gould SJ and Lewontin RC (1979), The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme, doi:10.1098/rspb.1979.0086, Section 3, Telling stories, p. 587, ext 1099

"Often, evolutionists use consistency with natural selection as the sole criterion and consider their work done when they concoct a plausible story. But plausible stories can always be told."

Gould SJ and Lewontin RC (1979), The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme, doi:10.1098/rspb.1979.0086, Section 3, Telling stories, pp. 587-588, ext 1100

"We would not object so strenuously to the adaptationist programme if its invocation, in any particular case, could lead in principle to its rejection for want of evidence."

Gould SJ and Lewontin RC (1979), The spandrels of San Marco and the Panglossian paradigm: a critique of the adaptationist programme, doi:10.1098/rspb.1979.0086, Section 3, Telling stories, p. 587, ext 1102

Where it hits

P1 P2 DA1 OF2

Where Cor stands

Cor's grading rule answers the core of this. A mismatch reading is graded by whether its modern prediction was tested and held, and the grade covers the tested prediction, never the ancestral story behind it. Each reading can fail in two ways. A clear physical defect found behind a case moves it to the disease side (DA1). And each reading predicts that the signal fades when its resolution condition is supplied (DC3): in adults who found work again, the distress of job loss was gone (Kessler RC et al. 1989, ext 4659), and had it stayed, the reading would have failed. The research programme lists six results that would refute Cor.

The wording point is conceded. The research programme's second hard-core claim says every drive, emotion and preference exists because it once helped propagate the genes that built it. That is the strong adaptationism Gould and Lewontin attack, and it leaves no room for by-products or drift in particular traits. Falsifier 2's wording ("weakened, not refuted") protects the core in the same way.

Replies published by others

Egeland and colleagues (2026, Table 2) reply that adaptationist explanations are offered only for traits with evidence of functional design, and that many hypotheses in the field generate testable predictions, some of which have been falsified.

What would settle it

For each mismatch reading, a modern prediction written down before the data are in, which could fail. Proposal only: restate hard-core claim 2 at the level of motivational architecture as a whole, leaving room for by-products and drift in particular traits, and state what would refute falsifier 2's claim outright.

On record in Cor: Falsifiers 2 and 6 on the research programme page.

OBJ-08 Values read into evolution: "what a human needs" smuggles in a norm

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Egeland and colleagues say Narvaez and colleagues risk committing the moralistic fallacy for saying natural selection requires thriving. Unless thriving just means differential fitness, it has nothing to do with selection: evolution is amoral and indifferent to human ideas of the good. The same charge reaches any account that moves from what selection built to what people should have.

"However, unless “thriving” is understood reductively, for example in terms of differential fitness, its relation to natural selection is orthogonal. Evolution is an amoral process that operates by forces unconcerned with human notions of what is good or right"

Egeland J et al. (2026), Explaining Human Psychology Evolutionarily: Recent Criticisms and New Lines of Defense, doi:10.1007/s12110-026-09540-1, Neglected Processes of Evolutionary Change, final paragraph, ext 1072

Where it hits

P1 OF2 DA9 DC3

Where Cor stands

Cor separates fitness from what is good for the organism: inclusive fitness is the loss function of selection, never of the organism's own computation (P1). A need, in Cor, is a resolution condition, the input that ends a signal, and the ancestral baseline diagnoses a gap without prescribing an ancestral form of life (P3).

The applied step rests on a value Cor states in DC3: one question decides whether a change counts, did it make the person less mismatched, and a little less mismatch is better than none. OF2 adds that reading the signal as information is no argument against relieving it. The value is stated, and nothing in it is derived from what selection favoured, so the moralistic fallacy does not apply.

Replies published by others

Egeland and colleagues (2026, Table 2) reply to the related charge of unethical implications that it commits either the naturalistic or the moralistic fallacy, because facts about what is do not fix what ought to be.

What would settle it

Settled by the premise DC3 states. Evidence that undoing a mismatch raises wellbeing as people themselves report it tests the applied claims; Cor holds this for work (Rueda S et al. 2012, ext 1710) and for debt (Ong Q et al. 2019, ext 1712).

On record in Cor: DC3, OF2.

OBJ-09 "Modular", "general-purpose" and "domain-sensitive" mix levels of analysis

Verdict: Partly answered

The attack

Pietraszewski and Wertz, themselves evolutionary psychologists, argue that the forty-year debate over modular versus general-purpose minds is confused. Fodor's modules are defined by what lies outside a central self's control; evolutionary psychology's are defined by function. Mixing the two levels makes the debate ill-posed, and the unmarked word "modularity" should be dropped. Bolhuis and colleagues add that domain-general learning mechanisms plainly exist across species.

"In this article we argue that the entirety of this debate, and the very notion of massive modularity itself, is ill-posed and confused. In particular, it is based on a confusion about the level of analysis (or reduction) at which one is approaching the mind."

Pietraszewski D and Wertz AE (2022), Why Evolutionary Psychology Should Abandon Modularity, doi:10.1177/1745691621997113, Abstract, ext 1096

"Given the profound confusion created by the term “modularity,” we would suggest abandoning any unmarked use of the term going forward."

OBJ-15 Addiction neuroscience: drugs change the brain in substance-specific ways

Verdict: Partly answered

The attack

Evolutionary researchers add a second attack: most drug use is not the pathological hijack a mismatch account predicts, and children do not take drugs as readily as the hijack account says they should.

"All known addictive drugs activate reward regions in the brain by causing sharp increases in the release of dopamine."

Volkow ND et al. (2016), Neurobiologic Advances from the Brain Disease Model of Addiction, doi:10.1056/NEJMra1511480, Section on the neurobiology of addiction, first paragraphs, ext 969

"the person with addiction transitions from taking drugs simply to feel pleasure, or to “get high,” to taking them to obtain transient relief from dysphoria"

Volkow ND et al. (2016), Neurobiologic Advances from the Brain Disease Model of Addiction, doi:10.1056/NEJMra1511480, Section on the antireward system and Figure 1, ext 970

"These mundane substances do not have an immediate hedonistic"

Sullivan RJ and Hagen EH (2002), Psychotropic substance-seeking: evolutionary pathology or adaptation?, doi:10.1046/j.1360-0443.2002.00024.x, Section: First experiences of commonly used drugs are often aversive, p. 393, ext 901

Where it hits

DA4 OF2 M2

Where Cor stands

Cor agrees with the first half of the attack and rejects its conclusion. A drug changes the body: tolerance, withdrawal, and wanting set off by the places and people of use (Volkow ND et al. 2016, ext 969, Volkow ND et al. 2016, ext 970, Robinson TE and Berridge KC 1993, ext 973). That change is a physical effect of the substance, and Cor's frame puts it on the physical side (OF2). It is real, and it comes down: in methamphetamine users who stayed off the drug, dopamine transporters rose 16 to 19 percent in about a year (Volkow ND et al. 2001, ext 2855). It does not decide whether the habit lasts: with the same body, use rose and fell with the setting.

One in five US soldiers became dependent on heroin in Vietnam (Robins LN et al. 1974, ext 2814). Of the men addicted there, 12 percent were addicted at any time in the three years after return (Robins LN 1994, ext 2820), with heroin easy to get at home (Hall W and Weier M 2017, ext 941). Long-term opioid users from San Antonio were abstinent 54 percent of the time while living elsewhere and 12 percent of the time at home, and 81 percent of those who came back abstinent were using again within a month (Maddux JF and Desmond DP 1982, ext 2833, Maddux JF and Desmond DP 1982, ext 2834). Neither study measured what the men lived among, so they show that the body's dependence does not decide, and nothing more. What the person comes back to decides (DC3): that is Cor's position, and it means people who know and need them, and a role among them. Rats trained on heroin or methamphetamine chose another rat over the drug almost every time, at any dose, and in rats scored as addicted (Venniro M et al. 2018, ext 925). Rat Park failed one repeat (Petrie BF 1996, ext 923) and held in a better independent one: isolated rats drank more morphine, and an hour a day of contact with another rat removed the difference (Raz S and Berger BD 2010, ext 5123). A habit can also start with the drug alone: of pain patients given high doses of opioids for 91 days or more, 6.1 percent got a diagnosis of opioid abuse or dependence within a year, against 0.004 percent of those given none (Edlund MJ et al. 2014, ext 2860). That is the supply half of Cor's hijack claim (DA4).

Two records stand against the position. In rats given free alcohol for nine months and then none for nine, intake doubled, and housing and rank no longer changed it (Wolffgramm J and Heyne A 1991, ext 933). Across 237 prospective studies, craving set off by cues went with later drug use or relapse, with the odds more than doubled (Vafaie N and Kober H 2022, ext 2854).

The second attack is answered. Cor's hijack claim covers a drug that is purified, that reaches the brain fast, or that is supplied without limit (DA4). Sullivan and Hagen draw the same line: they write that drugs with enhanced potency, such as heroin made from morphine, were absent in the past (Sullivan RJ and Hagen EH 2002, ext 902). Plant drugs at plant strength are old (Sullivan RJ and Hagen EH 2002, ext 899, Duke D et al. 2021, ext 2518) and are not the hijack. The children in the argument were tested on nicotine and coffee (Hagen EH et al. 2013, ext 919). Betel shows the supply part: where it is sold ready-made for a few cents a quid, 86 percent of current chewers met the authors' criteria for a use disorder (Lee CH et al. 2018, ext 2514), and among chewers of tobacco-free quid, 23.3 to 95.6 percent met the dependence criteria when slaked lime, which frees the drug, was added, against 4.0 percent without it (Lee CH et al. 2014, ext 2515). Pituri was chewed with wood ash, an alkali that raises the release of nicotine (Ratsch A et al. 2010, ext 2519), which puts it under the same line.

A job counts by what it supplies. Changing the environment means undoing the mismatch: moving a person's conditions toward what humans evolved for (DC3), and a job, money or a move counts by how far it does that. Women in drug treatment in Baltimore who were given paid work, kept on condition of drug-free urine, gave cocaine-negative samples 54 percent of the time over three years, against 28 percent in usual care (Silverman K et al. 2002, ext 2454). A flat of one's own (Baxter AJ et al. 2019, ext 2842) and 33 years in the setting of the habit (Hser YI et al. 2001, ext 2835) brought the person nobody who knows and needs them, and are not counted against Cor's position.

Replies published by others

None beyond the records cited.

A study that takes long-term users out of the setting of their use and puts them among people who need them, against usual treatment, with drug use and deaths as outcomes; an analysis of the Vietnam returnees by the home they came back to (Robins LN 1994, ext 2820); and the choice test of Venniro M et al. 2018, ext 925 run after long access of the kind in Wolffgramm J and Heyne A 1991, ext 933. What chewers of pituri gave up for it was never measured (Ratsch A et al. 2010, ext 2520), and Cor needs it.

Kendler makes the general point: sorting conditions into those with a visible brain finding and those without is a leftover of nineteenth-century neuropathology.

"Overall, six patients were initially evaluated by psychiatrists and five admitted to psychiatric units."

Dalmau J et al. (2007), Paraneoplastic anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor encephalitis associated with ovarian teratoma, doi:10.1002/ana.21050, Results (clinical features); Discussion, last paragraph, ext 1786

"77 patients presented with prominent psychiatric symptoms, including anxiety, agitation, bizarre behaviour, delusional or paranoid thoughts, and visual or auditory hallucinations."

Dalmau J et al. (2008), Anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis: case series and analysis of the effects of antibodies, doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(08)70224-2, Results (clinical features), table 1; outcome paragraph, ext 1788

"The approach is fallacious and only makes sense if you assume a Cartesian worldview and you are looking for where to ‘split’ the HMBS."

Kendler KS (2012), The dappled nature of causes of psychiatric illness: replacing the organic-functional/hardware-software dichotomy with empirically based pluralism, doi:10.1038/mp.2011.182, Concerns and comments, ext 1112

Where it hits

DA1 OF2

Where Cor stands

Cor's rule is not "nothing found, no disease". Only a clear physical defect is a disease, whether a test has found it yet or not (DA1). The encephalitis patients had a clear defect, antibodies that strip a receptor from brain cells (Dalmau J et al. 2008, ext 1789), before anyone could test for it. By Cor's rule they were sick all along and the behaviour was a symptom. What the rule refuses is the step from a marker to a defect: a receptor, a gene variant or a scan pattern that shows how sensitive a person is to stimuli and to mismatch is no defect and no cause, and measured activation can itself sit downstream of mismatch.

The rule has a cost and Cor states it. In one person with nothing found, nobody can tell a defect not yet found from no defect. Cor's default there is the response reading (OF2), and for some people that default will be wrong. That is why the search for a physical defect comes first in Cor's clinical application. In the encephalitis series it would not have stopped at the psychiatric picture: within three weeks 76 of the 100 had seizures and 88 lost responsiveness (Dalmau J et al. 2008, ext 1788).

Replies published by others

Kapur, Phillips and Insel (2012) on why biological differences have not become clinical tests (Kapur S et al. 2012, ext 1115).

What would settle it

Case by case: a defect shown to be the cause moves that case, or that part of a label, to the physical side. A count of how many people given a psychiatric label carry such a defect would show how often the default is wrong. The series Cor holds give no such figure.

On record in Cor: Pollak TA et al. 2020, ext 837; OBJ-18 states Kendler's wider attack on the split itself.

OBJ-25 Psychosis does not work like a healthy signal

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Loneliness points at something and stops when it is answered. Psychosis, on this attack, does neither. Nesse, whose work Cor leans on for the signal reading of low mood and anxiety, puts psychotic depression and mania in a separate class: mood regulation that is broken. Children later diagnosed with schizophrenia already score lower on reasoning tests at age 7. Many people in a psychotic state do not agree that anything is wrong, and that goes with higher symptom ratings. A signal the person cannot read, and that tracks nothing in the room, is a fault.

"There is nothing normal or useful about psychotic depression or mania. Both are serious diseases that result from broken mood regulation mechanisms."

Randolph M. Nesse (2019), Good Reasons for Bad Feelings: Insights from the Frontier of Evolutionary Psychiatry, source lines 4513-4514, ext 358

"The average childhood IQ was 94 for cohort members who met diagnostic criteria for schizophrenia as an adult, 98 for those who met criteria for recurrent depression as an adult, and 101 for healthy comparison subjects"

Reichenberg A et al. (2010), Static and dynamic cognitive deficits in childhood preceding adult schizophrenia: a 30-year study, doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2009.09040574, Abstract; Results, ext 1816

"This research indicates that poor insight predicts poorer treatment adherence and therapeutic alliance, higher symptom severity and more impaired community function, while good insight predicts a higher frequency of depression and demoralization, especially when coupled with stigma and social disadvantage."

Lysaker PH et al. (2018), Insight in schizophrenia spectrum disorders: relationship with behavior, mood and perceived quality of life, underlying causes and emerging treatments, doi:10.1002/wps.20508, Abstract; sections on behaviour and mood; Conclusions, ext 1817

Where it hits

DA1 OF2 DA10

Where Cor stands

Cor's position: psychosis where no defect is the cause is a signal with no resolution, turned all the way up (DA1). In a mismatched world most signals get no resolution, so they keep rising and stop looking like healthy signals. Where a signal gets its answer, as in the world humans lived in for most of their history, it is resolved long before it reaches that level. A signal that has run that far no longer looks like loneliness, and the attack takes that as proof of a fault.

A withheld need does this to healthy people. In 21 studies of 760 healthy people kept from sleep, perceptual distortions came within one to two days, hallucinations after two to four, and delusions after three, and normal sleep ended the symptoms in many cases (Waters F et al. 2018, ext 4020). The diagnosis also follows social conditions. People who report childhood adversity have about 2.8 times the odds of the diagnosis (Varese F et al. 2012, ext 1818). Migrants and their children are diagnosed about twice as often (Selten JP et al. 2020, ext 1822). The rate is lower where a person lives among their own group (Selten JP et al. 2013, ext 1819) and about 2.4 times higher in the most urban setting than in the most rural (Vassos E et al. 2012, ext 1821).

Nesse places psychotic depression and mania in a class of broken mood regulation and names no defect behind it (Randolph M. Nesse 2019, ext 358). The early lag on reasoning tests names none either (Reichenberg A et al. 2010, ext 1816). Where a defect is the cause, as with antibodies against the brain, the case is a disease by Cor's rule (DA1).

What would settle it

A study of people in a first psychotic state with no defect found, whose situation is answered (their own people around them, a place in a group, the exclusion ended), against usual care. If the state does not resolve where the answer is real, the reading fails.

On record in Cor: Nesse's boundary is recorded in DA1; Selten JP et al. 2013, ext 838, Cantor-Graae E and Selten JP 2005, ext 839, Vassos E et al. 2012, ext 840.

OBJ-26 Trials show more relapse when people come off the drug

Verdict: Answered

The attack

In the RADAR trial 253 adults with a recurrent psychosis diagnosis were randomised to a gradual dose reduction over months or to staying on the drug. Within two years 25 percent of the reduction group had a severe relapse against 13 percent on maintenance. Across 75 trials, 24 percent relapsed within a year on an antipsychotic and 61 percent on placebo, and it made no clear difference whether the drug was stopped at once or tapered. Psychiatric admissions about doubled in the two years after people stopped lithium. If relapse were only withdrawal, a slow taper would prevent it. It does not, so on this attack the drug was holding something back.

"By 24 months, 32 participants (25%) in the reduction group had at least one severe relapse compared with 17 (13%) in the maintenance group"

Moncrieff J et al. (2023), Antipsychotic dose reduction and discontinuation versus maintenance treatment in people with schizophrenia and other recurrent psychotic disorders in England (the RADAR trial): an open, parallel-group, randomised controlled trial, doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(23)00258-4, Results (relapse paragraph) and Summary; table 2, ext 1778

"There was no clear difference between studies in which antipsychotics were abruptly withdrawn (drug 27% versus placebo 62%, 18 RCTs, n = 2348, RR 0.43, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.53) or slowly tapered (drug 18% versus placebo 56%, 11 RCTs, n = 1765, RR 0.33, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.44)"

Ceraso A et al. (2020), Maintenance treatment with antipsychotic drugs for schizophrenia, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD008016.pub3, Results, subgroup analyses 2.1 and 2.4; Discussion 4.3; Implications for practice, ext 1782

"For the whole sample, the mean number of admissions/patient/review period doubled from 0.44 to 0.95 (P<0.001) after lithium discontinuation."

Ohlund L et al. (2019), Clinical course and need for hospital admission after lithium discontinuation in patients with bipolar disorder type I or II: mirror-image study based on the LiSIE retrospective cohort, doi:10.1192/bjo.2019.83, Abstract; Results, Table 2 and Kaplan-Meier section, ext 1461

Where it hits

DC3 OF2 DA1

Where Cor stands

Cor's position is its medication line, whole. Where no defect is the cause: the drug gets replaced by the real thing, such as people who need you and give back; the real thing is hard to build under the influence, so the drug comes down while the real thing comes in; tapering is not a rule for every medication; where stopping suddenly is biologically dangerous, as with certain drugs and with alcohol, never stop suddenly, and do it with a doctor. On this line the only imbalance is the one the drug makes, and what is called relapse is the original signal returning plus withdrawal. Where a defect is the cause, the drug may be treating a disease.

The trials do not test that line. Each one took the drug away and put nothing real in its place: in RADAR both arms had usual care, and its authors name added social support as untested (Moncrieff J et al. 2023, ext 1780). A signal whose cause is still there comes back, taper or no taper, so the finding that tapering made no clear difference (Ceraso A et al. 2020, ext 1782) is what the line predicts for a withdrawal with no replacement. It also means relapse is more than withdrawal, and Cor says so: it is the original signal returning as well.

The nearest studies put something in the drug's place, and none put a person's own people there. Soteria used a house with paid staff (Bola JR and Mosher LR 2003, ext 1802). Open Dialogue used staff teams that meet the family and network (Bergström T et al. 2018, ext 1806). One trial tapered antidepressants alongside a therapist-led class and found relapse no different from staying on the drug (Kuyken W et al. 2015, ext 1815). The long follow-ups point both ways: seven years after early dose reduction more first-episode patients had recovered, 40 against 18 percent (Wunderink L et al. 2013, ext 1784); ten years after a year on placebo more had a poor outcome, 39 against 21 percent (Hui CLM et al. 2018, ext 1785). No trial has tested the replacement line. A search of ClinicalTrials.gov and of the published trial protocols on 21 November 2026 found none, finished or running: the reduction trials bring the dose down under usual care, with a diary and a list of warning signs (Begemann MJH et al. 2020, ext 2500), and the one trial with a job programme gives it to both arms (Weller A et al. 2019, ext 2501). The newest reduction trial repeats the pattern of the older ones: more relapse in the first year, better functioning at three and four years (Sommer IE et al. 2026, ext 2498), and five deaths against one (Sommer IE et al. 2026, ext 2499).

Around the trials the evidence on a person's own people points one way. Warmth from the family goes with fewer relapses and criticism with more (Ma CF et al. 2021, ext 2481). Working with the family cut relapse by almost half, in trials that kept the drug (Pharoah F et al. 2010, ext 2482, Pharoah F et al. 2010, ext 2483).

What would settle it

A randomised trial in which the dose comes down while a person's own people are put in place, people who need them and give back, against maintenance, with relapse and daily functioning as outcomes.

On record in Cor: Lewis G et al. 2021, ext 1306, Van Leeuwen E et al. 2021, ext 1365, Tondo L and Baldessarini RJ 2020, ext 1463, Ceraso A et al. 2020, ext 1781, Calton T et al. 2008, ext 1804.

OBJ-27 A reader on medication will take the page as a reason to stop

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Raised by two test readers of the project's own manifesto in November 2026, and by the trials. A page that says "where no defect is the cause there is no disease" will be read by people on lithium, antipsychotics, antidepressants and benzodiazepines. Some will stop. In one blinded trial half of the patients switched abruptly from lithium to placebo had a manic relapse within weeks. Abrupt benzodiazepine withdrawal can bring life-threatening seizures. Across 75 trials, people switched from an antipsychotic to placebo relapsed far more often within a year. On this attack the page does harm whatever its intent, so the position should be softened or left off.

"The relapses started 13-19 days after placebo substitution."

Mander AJ and Loudon JB (1988), Rapid recurrence of mania following abrupt discontinuation of lithium, doi:10.1016/s0140-6736(88)92947-9, Abstract, ext 1470

"can cause life-threatening seizures so the dose should be gradually reduced"

Brett J and Murnion B (2015), Management of benzodiazepine misuse and dependence, doi:10.18773/austprescr.2015.055, Key points; Withdrawal section; Box (withdrawal clinical features), ext 984

"Antipsychotic drugs were more effective than placebo in preventing relapse at seven to 12 months (primary outcome; drug 24% versus placebo 61%, 30 RCTs, n = 4249, RR 0.38, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.45"

Ceraso A et al. (2020), Maintenance treatment with antipsychotic drugs for schizophrenia, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD008016.pub3, Abstract, Main results, ext 1781

Where it hits

DA1 DC3

Where Cor stands

Cor does not soften the position and does not leave it off. A person who replaces a drug that treats no disease with the real thing is the outcome the medication line aims at. What the attack describes is half of the line taken alone, which is why the line always travels whole: the drug gets replaced by the real thing, such as people who need you and give back; the real thing is hard to build under the influence, so the drug comes down while the real thing comes in; tapering is not a rule for every medication; where stopping suddenly is biologically dangerous, as with certain drugs and with alcohol, never stop suddenly, and do it with a doctor. The warning sits in the text next to the position wherever Cor mentions medication, and where a defect is the cause the line says the drug may be treating a disease.

The trials the attack cites show what sudden stopping does (Mander AJ and Loudon JB 1988, ext 1470, Brett J and Murnion B 2015, ext 984), and the line forbids sudden stopping where it is dangerous. Cor weighs a second harm that the attack leaves out: a person whose reaction is a normal response to a mismatched world is given a lifelong diagnosis and a drug for life.

What would settle it

A follow-up of readers on medication: how many changed a dose, how fast, with whom, and what followed. A follow-up of readers given the response reading: what they changed in their lives. Cor needs both. And the trial named under OBJ-26.

On record in Cor: Tondo L and Baldessarini RJ 2020, ext 1463, Kalfas M et al. 2025, ext 1361.

OBJ-28 A wrong reading of a first psychotic episode does damage, so psychosis should be left out

Verdict: Answered

The attack

The longer a first psychotic episode goes without treatment, the worse the person does years later: across 33 samples a longer delay went with worse symptom ratings and poorer social functioning at a mean of eight years. A family told that psychosis is a signal may wait, and waiting costs. Psychosis is the one label, on this attack, where being wrong is too expensive, so the project should carve it out.

"Long DUP correlated statistically significantly with poor general symptomatic outcome, more severe positive and negative symptoms, lesser likelihood of remission and poor social functioning and global outcome"

Penttilä M et al. (2014), Duration of untreated psychosis as predictor of long-term outcome in schizophrenia: systematic review and meta-analysis, doi:10.1192/bjp.bp.113.127753, Abstract (Results); Results, association between DUP and outcome categories, ext 1791

"There is convincing evidence of a modest association between DUP and outcome, which supports the case for clinical trials that examine the effect of reducing DUP."

Marshall M et al. (2005), Association between duration of untreated psychosis and outcome in cohorts of first-episode patients: a systematic review, doi:10.1001/archpsyc.62.9.975, Abstract (Data synthesis, Conclusions), ext 1790

Where it hits

DA1 OF2

Where Cor stands

No label is carved out (DA1). The damage Cor weighs most is the other reading: taking a normal human reaction to a mismatched world for a disorder, a flaw in the person to be fixed. That is the default reading today, and leaving psychosis out would leave it standing for the people it is applied to most heavily.

The evidence for the attack is an association. The correlations are small (0.13 to 0.18) and mostly unadjusted, and the authors of the meta-analysis write that social support and other factors probably matter more in the long term (Penttilä M et al. 2014, ext 1792). In a 20-year follow-up the link was there at admission and gone in the long term, which the authors read as lead-time bias, while warning that this is no evidence against early help (Jonas KG et al. 2020, ext 1793, Jonas KG et al. 2020, ext 1794). None of these studies has a group whose first episode was met by their own people in place of a drug: they compare early drug treatment with late drug treatment. Cor's rule also sends every first episode to a search for a physical defect first, such as antibodies against the brain, a tumour, a drug or an infection.

What would settle it

The study named under OBJ-25.

On record in Cor: Tu LH et al. 2022, ext 1226, Pollak TA et al. 2020, ext 837.

OBJ-29 Migraine has no blood test either

Verdict: Answered

The attack

Migraine is diagnosed from the patient's account: at least five attacks of 4 to 72 hours with a set of pain features. No blood test or scan is among the criteria, and tests serve only to rule out other headaches. Nobody concludes that migraine is no disease. If "no test" does not make migraine a normal reaction, the attack runs, it cannot make depression or psychosis one.

"Headache has at least two of the following four characteristics: 1. unilateral location 2. pulsating quality 3. moderate or severe pain intensity 4. aggravation by or causing avoidance of routine physical activity"

Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (IHS) (2018), The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition, doi:10.1177/0333102417738202, Section 1.1 Migraine without aura, diagnostic criteria, ext 1795

"The medical history is the mainstay of migraine diagnosis; with the assistance of a range of published aids (see the section Diagnostic aids), a full history should enable systematic application of the criteria set out in the ICHD-3. Physical examination is most often confirmatory and further investigations (for example, neuroimaging, blood samples or lumbar puncture) are occasionally required to confirm or reject suspicions of secondary causes for headache."

Eigenbrodt AK et al. (2021), Diagnosis and management of migraine in ten steps, doi:10.1038/s41582-021-00509-5, Step 1, Diagnosis, ext 1797

"A transient wave of neuronal depolarization of the cortex, the cortical spreading depression (CSD), is believed to be the pathophysiological brain mechanism underlying the clinical phenomenon of migraine aura."

Goadsby PJ et al. (2017), Pathophysiology of migraine: a disorder of sensory processing, doi:10.1152/physrev.00034.2015, Sections on aura and CSD; migraine triggers; CGRP therapies, ext 1799

Where it hits

DA1 DA10

Where Cor stands

The attack hits a rule Cor does not hold. The rule is a clear physical defect, found or not yet found (DA1). Whether a test exists decides nothing. Migraine is sorted by the same rule as every other label. The rare hemiplegic form has a single-gene defect (Goadsby PJ et al. 2017, ext 1798) and sits on the physical side. For common migraine the findings so far are of the other kind: 123 regions of the genome, each of small effect, and a tendency to run in families (Hautakangas H et al. 2022, ext 1800). Those mark how sensitive a person is, and a marker of sensitivity is no defect (DA10). An attack has a physical course that can be recorded: a signal crossing the visual cortex during the aura (Hadjikhani N et al. 2001, ext 1801), and a signalling molecule that triggers attacks and whose blockers work in trials (Goadsby PJ et al. 2017, ext 1799). Activity recorded during a response shows the response happening, and by itself it is no defect. If a clear defect is the cause of common migraine, it is a disease by Cor's rule, found or not. The pain is real on either reading.

What would settle it

A defect shown to cause common migraine would place it on the physical side. Attacks that stop when their triggers are removed, with no defect present, would place it on the response side.

On record in Cor: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (IHS) 2018, ext 1796.
